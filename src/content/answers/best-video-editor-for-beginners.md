**For a beginner in 2026 there are two honest answers. If you want to pay nothing and learn a timeline, start with CapCut (mobile and desktop) or Clipchamp (desktop, no watermark on the free tier). If you would rather describe the edit in plain language and skip the timeline, start with Vyra, which is free to start and cuts a first draft from your raw footage in minutes. Most beginners publish their first video within an hour with any of the three.**

## Why

- **The first obstacle is not features, it is the timeline.** Most beginners quit in the first session because trimming, splitting, and layering clips is unintuitive. Any editor that removes that step wins the first week.
- **Captions matter more than effects.** A beginner's first videos need clean cuts and readable captions. Fancy transitions come later, if ever.
- **Free tiers differ in what they withhold.** Some hold back resolution, some hold back captions, some add a watermark. Read the free-tier limits before you commit a week to learning.
- **Beginner is not one person.** Someone posting from a phone between classes needs a different tool than someone editing a 10-minute YouTube video on a laptop.

## What to do about it

1. **Pick by device.** Phone only: CapCut. Laptop, no budget: Clipchamp. Laptop, willing to pay later, hates timelines: Vyra.
2. **Make one 30-second talking head first.** Trim the start and end, remove one pause, add captions, export. That is the whole first lesson.
3. **Learn three operations.** Cut, trim, caption. Everything else can wait a month.
4. **Do not buy a plan in week one.** Every tool here is free to start. Upgrade when you hit a real limit, like export resolution or processing time.
5. **Switch when the tool is the bottleneck, not before.** If you are spending more time in menus than filming, move up.

## FAQ

**Is CapCut still free?**
CapCut has a free tier, but some features that used to be free, including auto captions, are now on the Pro tier according to CapCut's own plan guide. Check the current limits before relying on it.

**Do I need to learn Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve as a beginner?**
No. Those are professional tools with a learning curve measured in weeks. Start simpler and move to them only if your work demands multi-camera, color grading, or advanced audio.

**Can an AI editor replace learning to edit?**
For most social video, yes. You still make the creative calls (what to keep, what the hook is), but the mechanical work is done for you. Vyra also has a full timeline for when you want to learn it.
