**Two honest answers. If you want to pay nothing and learn a timeline, start with CapCut (phone and desktop) or Clipchamp (desktop, no watermark on free). If you'd rather describe the edit in plain language and skip the timeline, start with Vyra. It's free to start and cuts a first draft from your raw footage in minutes. Most beginners publish their first video within an hour with any of the three.**

## Why

- The first obstacle is the timeline, not the feature list. Most beginners quit in the first session because trimming and layering clips is confusing.
- Captions matter more than effects. Your first videos need clean cuts and readable captions. Transitions can wait.
- Free tiers hold back different things. Some cap resolution, some gate captions, some add a watermark. Read the limits first.
- Someone posting from a phone between classes needs a different tool than someone editing a 10-minute YouTube video on a laptop.

## What to do about it

1. **Pick by device.** Phone only, CapCut. Laptop and no budget, Clipchamp. Laptop and you hate timelines, Vyra.
2. **Make one 30-second talking head first.** Trim the start and end, remove one pause, add captions, export. That's the whole first lesson.
3. **Learn three operations.** Cut, trim, caption. Everything else can wait a month.
4. **Don't buy a plan in week one.** Upgrade when you hit a real limit.
5. **Switch when the tool is the bottleneck.** If you spend more time in menus than filming, move up.

## FAQ

**Is CapCut still free?**
There's a free tier, but some features that used to be free, including auto captions, moved to Pro according to CapCut's own plan guide.

**Do I need to learn Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve?**
No. Those take weeks to learn. Start simpler and move up only if you need multi-camera, color grading, or serious audio work.

**Can an AI editor replace learning to edit?**
For most social video, yes. You still decide what to keep and what the hook is. Vyra also has a full timeline for when you want to learn it.
