**Use speech captions when the viewer needs the spoken words. Use a short text label for context the recording does not say, such as a step name or a date. In Vyra on iPhone, Captions and Text are separate tools.**

The speaker may say to leave the first layer to dry while the picture needs a label identifying which layer it is. Keep the transcript faithful and put the extra context in its own text item. Otherwise, a viewer may think the label was part of the spoken sentence.

## Give each text item one job

| Viewer needs | Useful text |
| --- | --- |
| Follow what someone says | A reviewed speech-caption track |
| Identify a silent stage | A short label over the relevant shot |
| Read an exact product or material name | A checked name beside the item it identifies |
| Follow speech and the stage together | Separate captions and a restrained label |

The current Captions help documents generating from selected audio. Editing tools on iPhone documents Text for a separate item. Add the label only for the interval where it explains the picture.

## Review both together

Try a brief such as this.

> Caption my spoken explanation faithfully. Add the separate label first coat only over the matching shot. Keep both away from the brush tip.

Read the section with sound off, then listen to it. Check that the label does not repeat a caption or contradict the recording. If both compete for the same space, reduce the extra wording before shrinking everything.

## FAQ

**Should I add information the speaker did not say to the transcript?**
Keep the transcript faithful. Put necessary extra context in a separate label so its role is clear.

**Does a label need to stay throughout the video?**
Keep it for the shot or section it explains. Check that it disappears before it can identify the wrong stage.
