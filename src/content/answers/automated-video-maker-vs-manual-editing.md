**Use both, in that order. Automate the first cut, captions, reframing, and music. Those steps are mechanical and take most of the time. Edit by hand for the last 5 percent, the one cut that lands a joke or the caption that needs a different word. Go fully manual only for cinematic or client work where every frame is deliberate and the budget covers the hours.**

## Why

- Reviewing, trimming, captioning, and syncing are pattern work. Choosing the hook and the ending is judgment. Automation is good at the first.
- Even a fast editor spends 30 minutes on a 60-second social video. Automation gets that to 5 to 15 minutes including review.
- Automation won't know a particular pause is the point unless you say so. Hand editing closes that gap.
- The good tools have a real timeline underneath (Vyra, Descript, InVideo after the draft), so you can switch modes without exporting.

## Side by side

| | Automated maker | Manual editing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to first draft | Minutes | Hours |
| Consistency across many videos | High | Depends on the editor |
| Control over a single frame | Prompt, then timeline | Total |
| Learning curve | Low | Weeks to months |
| Best for | Social volume, first drafts | Cinematic, client, complex multi-cam |

## What to do about it

1. **Write the brief first.** Length, platform, keep, cut, captions, music.
2. **Let the tool build the draft.** Don't fix as you go. Watch it through once.
3. **Correct in prompts.** "Swap the intro clip." "Tighten the middle by 10 seconds."
4. **Open the timeline for the last pass.** Nudge a cut, move a caption off a face.
5. **Save the brief.** Reuse it next time. That's where the real savings are.

## FAQ

**Is manual editing dead for creators?**
No. It moved from the whole job to the finishing pass. Minutes instead of hours.

**Which is better for a beginner?**
Automated first. You get a publishable result while you learn what a good cut looks like.

**Can automation match my style?**
Partly. Vyra can match a reference video's pacing, captions, and music behavior. Your signature moves still come from you.
