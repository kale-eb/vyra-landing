**Use both, in order. Automate the first cut, captions, reframing, and music, because those steps are mechanical and take most of the time. Edit by hand for the last 5 percent: the one cut that lands a joke, the caption that needs a different word, the frame the AI held a beat too long. Go fully manual only for cinematic or client work where every frame is deliberate and the budget covers the hours.**

## Why

- **The steps are different kinds of work.** Reviewing, trimming, captioning, and syncing are pattern work. Choosing the hook and the ending is judgment. Automation is good at the first and only helps with the second.
- **Manual editing has a floor.** Even a fast editor spends 30 minutes on a 60-second social video. Automation moves that to 5 to 15 minutes including review.
- **Automation has a ceiling.** It will not know that a particular pause is the point, or that the client hates a font, unless you say so. Hand editing closes that gap.
- **The best tools are not either or.** An automated maker with a real timeline underneath (Vyra, Descript, InVideo's editor after the draft) lets you switch modes without exporting and re-importing.

## Side by side

| | Automated maker | Manual editing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to first draft | Minutes | Hours |
| Consistency across many videos | High | Depends on the editor |
| Control over a single frame | Via prompt, then timeline | Total |
| Learning curve | Low | Weeks to months |
| Best for | Social volume, repurposing, first drafts | Cinematic, client, complex multi-cam |
| Failure mode | Generic first draft if the brief is vague | Never finishing |

## What to do about it

1. **Write the brief before you touch anything.** Length, platform, keep, cut, captions, music. This is the input the automated step needs and the plan the manual step follows.
2. **Let the tool build the draft.** Do not fix as you go. Watch it through once.
3. **Correct in prompts first.** "Swap the intro clip", "tighten the middle by 10 seconds". Most fixes are one sentence.
4. **Open the timeline for the last pass.** Nudge a cut, move a caption off a face, extend a hold.
5. **Save the brief.** Next video, reuse it. That is where the compounding savings come from.

## FAQ

**Is manual editing dead for creators?**
No. It moved from the whole job to the finishing pass. Most creators still open a timeline, just for minutes instead of hours.

**Which is better for a beginner?**
Automated first. It gets a publishable result while you learn what a good cut looks like. Manual skills come faster when you have drafts to compare against.

**Can automation match my style?**
Partly. Vyra can match a reference video's pacing, captions, and music behavior, and you can correct any choice in plain language. Signature moves still come from you.
