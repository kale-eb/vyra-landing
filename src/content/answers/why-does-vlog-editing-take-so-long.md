**A 10-minute vlog typically takes 5 to 10 hours to edit, and the time isn't spent cutting. It's spent watching every clip to find the usable seconds, making hundreds of small keep-or-cut decisions, and switching between unrelated jobs. Story, b-roll, audio, captions, music, color. Creators with a heavily polished style report 20 hours or more per vlog.**

## Why

- A day of vlogging produces 60 to 120 clips and 1 to 2 hours of raw material for 10 finished minutes. Reviewing that once is an hour or two before a single cut.
- Vlogs are unscripted, so you're finding the story on the timeline. Every clip is a keep-or-cut call, then a where-does-it-go call, then a how-long call.
- Trimming pauses, ums, and restarts with a blade tool eats an hour on its own.
- Cutting, then color, then audio, then captions, then music, then back to cutting because the music changed the pacing. Each switch costs focus.
- Watch back, notice a half-second flicker, fix it, re-watch the section, repeat.

## What to do about it

1. **Shoot on purpose.** Decide the story before you film. A shot list of 15 to 20 moments halves review time.
2. **Separate passes.** Rough cut first, then audio, then captions, then music, then color.
3. **Lock music early.** Drop the track in after the rough cut so the beat sets the pacing.
4. **Let a machine do the review.** An editor that has transcribed and indexed your footage can list the usable moments in seconds.
5. **Set a time box.** A 10-minute vlog gets 3 hours. When the clock runs out, export.

## FAQ

**Is 10 hours for one vlog normal?**
Yes, for a polished 10-minute video. Daily vloggers get under 2 hours by shooting to a plan and editing in passes, or by handing the first cut to an AI.

**What takes the longest?**
Reviewing footage and trimming pauses. Both are mechanical, and both are what AI editing removes first.

**Does a faster computer help?**
Rendering gets faster. Deciding doesn't.
