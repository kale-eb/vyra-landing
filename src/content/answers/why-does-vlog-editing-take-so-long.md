**A 10-minute vlog typically takes 5 to 10 hours to edit, and the time is not spent cutting. It is spent watching every clip to find the usable seconds, making hundreds of small decisions about what stays, and switching between unrelated jobs: story, b-roll, audio, captions, music, color. Creators with a heavily polished style report 20 hours or more per vlog.**

## Why

- **Footage ratio.** A day of vlogging produces 60 to 120 clips and 1 to 2 hours of raw material for 10 finished minutes. Just reviewing that once is an hour or two before a single cut happens.
- **Decisions per minute.** Vlogs are unscripted, so you are finding the story on the timeline instead of following a script. Every clip is a keep-or-cut call, then a where-does-it-go call, then a how-long call.
- **Manual silence and retake removal.** Trimming pauses, "ums", and restarted sentences with a blade tool is slow, repetitive work that eats an hour on its own.
- **Context switching.** Cutting, then color, then audio leveling, then captions, then music ducking, then back to cutting because the music changed the pacing. Each switch costs focus.
- **Perfectionism loops.** Watch back, notice a half-second flicker, fix it, re-watch the whole section, repeat.

## What to do about it

1. **Shoot less, shoot on purpose.** Decide the story before you film. A shot list of 15 to 20 moments cuts review time in half.
2. **Separate passes.** Rough cut first (story only), then audio, then captions, then music, then color. Do not mix them.
3. **Lock music early.** Drop the track in after the rough cut so the beat sets the pacing before you polish.
4. **Let a machine do the review.** An editor that has transcribed and visually indexed your footage can list the usable moments in seconds instead of you scrubbing for an hour.
5. **Set a time box.** A 10-minute vlog gets 3 hours. When the clock runs out, export.

## FAQ

**Is 10 hours for one vlog normal?**
Yes for a polished 10-minute video. Daily vloggers get under 2 hours by shooting to a plan and editing in passes, or by handing the first cut to an AI.

**What takes the longest?**
Reviewing footage and trimming pauses. Both are mechanical and both are what AI editing removes first.

**Does a faster computer help?**
Rendering gets faster. Deciding does not. The hours are in decisions, not exports.
