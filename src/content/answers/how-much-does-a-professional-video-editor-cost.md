**Professional editors typically price one of three ways. Hourly, per finished minute, or per video. Rates vary a lot by experience, country, and format. A short social clip is usually the cheapest job, a polished 10-minute YouTube video with b-roll and graphics the most expensive, and monthly retainers sit in between. Before hiring, price the alternative. An AI editor that builds the rough cut for a subscription, with you or a freelancer doing the finishing pass.**

## Why

- Editing time drives cost. A 60-second Reel is 30 to 60 minutes of work for a pro. A 10-minute YouTube video is typically 5 to 10 hours.
- Experience changes the rate more than the format. A beginner and a broadcast editor can differ several times over for the same job.
- Revisions are often extra. Many editors include one or two rounds.
- Committing to monthly volume usually gets a better rate than one-off jobs.

## Typical pricing models

| Model | How it works | Typical fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hourly | You pay for time spent | Unclear scope, ongoing work |
| Per finished minute | Fixed rate per minute delivered | YouTube, courses |
| Per video | Flat fee for a defined deliverable | Social clips, ads |
| Monthly retainer | Set number of videos per month | Creators posting weekly or more |

Exact figures move with the market. Ask for a quote on one representative video before committing.

## What to do about it

1. **Define the deliverable.** Length, platform, captions, music, graphics, revision rounds. Vague briefs get padded quotes.
2. **Send a reference video.** It saves the editor hours of guessing and you a revision round.
3. **Price the AI option honestly.** For social volume, Vyra (from $24/mo, free to start) can build the rough cut and captions. You or a freelancer finish.
4. **Start with one paid test.** One video, clear brief, fixed price.
5. **Move to a retainer after three good videos.**

## FAQ

**Is it cheaper to hire an editor or use AI?**
For social video at volume, an AI subscription is typically far cheaper per video. For a flagship YouTube video with heavy graphics, a human editor still adds value, often on top of an AI rough cut.

**How do I know if a quote is fair?**
Get two or three for the same brief. Big spreads usually mean the brief is unclear.

**Can I edit myself instead?**
Yes. With an AI rough cut, the finishing pass on a short video is 10 to 20 minutes and doesn't need timeline skills.
