**A faceless YouTube channel points the camera at your hands, your screen, your things, or the places you go, and your face never appears. The faceless channels that last film something real every week and give it a voice, either a voiceover or the sound of the work itself. Here are 27 faceless YouTube channel ideas in five groups, all things you film or record yourself. Each one has a sample line, the format it fits, and a prompt that edits the footage in Vyra.**

Some of my best clips never show my face. My faceless process clip "The hand vs what it created" got 160K views, and a craft clip of a flower book for Mother Goose got 90.1K.

## How to use this list

1. Pick a channel you can film every week with the gear and space you already have.
2. Mount the phone above or beside your hands, or record your screen. Check the frame for reflections that show your face.
3. Record the voiceover after you film, or keep the natural sound if the work sounds good on its own.
4. Upload the footage to Vyra with the prompt. It transcribes your voiceover and describes every scene, so it can match each sentence to the right clip.

## Hands and making

### 1. The art process channel
Film your drawings or paintings from start to finish with the camera on your hands and the work. Watching a blank surface fill up is satisfying, and every finished piece is a new video.
> "Six hours on one painting, start to finish."

Fits [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video) and [process video](/formats/process-video). My faceless clip "The hand vs what it created" got 160K views.
```
Cut the session into a 10-minute video. Speed up the slow fill-in sections to 8x and keep the detail work at 2x. Put my voiceover on top with captions in a small white serif, bottom center. Every 2 minutes, show a 1-second side by side of the current state and the finished piece. 16:9.
```

### 2. Overhead cooking
Cook one recipe per video from an overhead camera, with only your hands and the cutting board in frame. Recipes are evergreen, so a video can keep getting views for years.
> "A weeknight dinner in 20 minutes, one pan."

Fits [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video) and [tutorial](/formats/tutorial).
```
Put each ingredient's name and amount as text in the top left when it goes into the pan. Keep the chopping sounds and speed up the long simmer to 6x. Add a chapter title at each new step. End on the plated dish for 3 seconds with the full ingredient list on screen.
```

### 3. Fixing old things
Repair something broken, like a pair of shoes, a lamp, or a cassette player, and show it working at the end. Repair videos have a built-in before and after, and viewers stay to see if it works.
> "This radio hasn't turned on since 1994."

Fits [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation) and [process video](/formats/process-video).
```
Open on the broken object for 2 seconds, then the working object for 2 seconds, then go back to the start. Add a parts list as a motion graphic when each new part appears. Zoom in 30 percent on small screws and solder points. Keep the natural workshop sound under quiet music.
```

### 4. Miniatures and models
Build a tiny room, a model kit, or a diorama over several sessions. The scale is fun to see, and a coin or a fingertip in frame makes the size clear.
> "The whole kitchen fits in my palm."

Fits [process video](/formats/process-video) and [art timelapse](/formats/art-timelapse).
```
Use macro shots of the build, sped up to 4x between details. Put a size reference label in the corner the first time each piece appears, like "8mm". Slow to normal speed whenever my tweezers place a final piece. End with a slow 360 pan of the finished model.
```

### 5. Paper and ink ASMR
Record the sounds of your materials, like crunchy paper, ink pens, and tape, with a close mic and no music. ASMR viewers watch for the sound, so long videos are a feature.
> "Ten ink drawings, no talking."

Fits [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog) and [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video). My crunchy paper ASMR video of 10 ink paintings got 10.5K views.
```
Use no music and no voice. Keep every material sound and raise the volume on paper crinkles and pen scratches. Hold each shot at least 6 seconds. Put the material names in small grey text in the lower left. 16:9.
```

### 6. Handmade gifts
Make one handmade gift per video, like a flower book, a knitted scarf, or a painted mug, and explain who it is for. A person on the receiving end gives each project a story.
> "Making a flower book for my friend's birthday."

Fits [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video) and [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video). My flower book for Mother Goose got 90.1K views.
```
Open on my voiceover line about who the gift is for over the first shot of the materials. Cut the making clips on the beat of a soft acoustic track. Put each step name as a small label in the top left. End on the finished gift being wrapped, held for 2 seconds.
```

## Screen-recorded channels

### 7. Spreadsheet tips
Teach one spreadsheet trick per video, recorded on your screen with your voice. People search for these exact problems at work, so each video answers a real question.
> "How to find duplicates in a column in 30 seconds."

Fits [tutorial](/formats/tutorial) and [explainer](/formats/explainer).
```
Cut every pause and filler word from my voiceover. Zoom in on the cursor every time I click a menu or type a formula. Put the formula on screen as large text when I finish typing it. Add chapter titles for each step. 16:9, plus a 30-second 9:16 cut of the main trick.
```

### 8. Code one small project
Build one small app or tool per video, recorded on your screen from empty folder to working result. Viewers learn by following along, and the working demo at the end is the payoff.
> "Building a weather app in one hour."

Fits [tutorial](/formats/tutorial) and [founder build in public](/formats/founder-build-in-public).
```
Open on the finished app working for 5 seconds, then cut to the empty folder. Speed up typing to 4x and keep my explanations at normal speed. Zoom in on the line of code I'm talking about. Add chapter markers for each feature. 16:9.
```

### 9. Your own gameplay, narrated
Record your own gameplay of one game and narrate a challenge, like finishing it without upgrades. A challenge turns ordinary play into a story with stakes.
> "Can I finish this game without ever using a map?"

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video) and [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video).
```
Cut my gameplay down to the key moments using my voiceover transcript. Remove every stretch where I'm walking with nothing happening. Add a counter in the top right for the challenge rule, like deaths or days. Captions on my commentary in bold white with a black outline.
```

### 10. Drawing tablet explainers
Explain one topic per video while you draw diagrams on a tablet. Hand-drawn diagrams are easier to follow than slides, and the drawing gives the viewer something to watch.
> "How a bill becomes a law, drawn in five minutes."

Fits [explainer](/formats/explainer).
```
Sync each drawing to the sentence it explains, speeding up the drawing clips to finish as the sentence ends. Add a title card for each section with a short motion graphic. Captions on, centered low, with the key term in each section highlighted in yellow.
```

### 11. App setups and workflows
Show how you organize one part of your life in an app, like a reading list, a study planner, or a budget template. People copy setups, and they save the video for when they build their own.
> "My whole semester planned in one notes app."

Fits [app walkthrough](/formats/app-walkthrough) and [product demo](/formats/product-demo).
```
Zoom in on each section of my screen recording as I explain it, then zoom back out. Blur any personal information on screen. Add a short label in the corner for each part of the setup. Use calm lo-fi music under my voiceover at 15 percent.
```

### 12. Language flashcards
Teach ten words or phrases in a language you know, with your screen showing flashcards and your voice saying each one. Viewers repeat after you, so the video works as practice and gets rewatched.
> "Ten phrases you'll use on day one in Seoul."

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head) and [tutorial](/formats/tutorial).
```
Make one section per phrase. Show the phrase in large text, then the pronunciation below it, then the translation, each appearing as I say it. Leave a 2-second pause after each phrase with a "your turn" label. Number each phrase in the top left.
```

## Places and walks

### 13. City walks
Walk through a city with the camera at chest height and the natural sound on. Viewers use walk videos to explore places they can't visit, and the format stays the same every week.
> "A rainy evening walk through Brooklyn, 40 minutes."

Fits [POV video](/formats/pov-video) and [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog).
```
Keep the walk as one long sequence with only the shaky or blocked moments cut out. Use the natural street sound with no music. Put the street name in small text in the lower left each time I turn onto a new street. 16:9, 4K export if the source allows it.
```

### 14. Trail walks and hikes
Film hikes from your point of view, with a short voiceover about the trail at the start. Hikers search for trail videos before they go, so each one answers a practical question.
> "The full trail in 12 minutes, so you know what you're getting into."

Fits [POV video](/formats/pov-video) and [travel recap](/formats/travel-recap).
```
Speed up the flat sections to 4x and keep the steep and scenic sections at normal speed. Add a distance counter in the top right. Put a trail map motion graphic at the start that shows the route. End on the view at the top, held for 5 seconds with music fading in.
```

### 15. Window seat rides
Film train, bus, or ferry rides from the window seat. The landscape changes on its own, and viewers play these to relax or fall asleep.
> "Two hours along the coast by train."

Fits [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll) and [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog).
```
Cut out any shots blocked by people or reflections. Keep the train sound and mix in quiet ambient music at 10 percent. Add the station name as small text when we stop. Keep cuts rare, one every few minutes, so it plays like one long ride. 16:9.
```

### 16. Room and studio tours
Tour other people's rooms, studios, and workspaces with the owner narrating off camera. Everyone is curious how other people live and work, and each new space is a new episode.
> "A ceramicist's studio in a converted garage."

Fits [studio tour](/formats/studio-tour) and [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll).
```
Put the owner's voiceover over slow pans of each part of the space. Use their transcript to place each shot under the sentence that describes it. Add small labels for each zone, like "kiln" or "drying shelf". Captions on, white serif, lower third.
```

### 17. Ambience at your window
Record the view and sound from your window for an hour, like rain, snow, or a busy street at night. Ambience videos play in the background while people study or sleep, so they get long watch times.
> "Rain on the window, one hour, no music."

Fits [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog).
```
Make a one-hour video from my window footage, joining the clips with 3-second crossfades so there are no visible cuts. Keep the natural sound only and even out the volume. Add the place and date in tiny text in the bottom corner for the first 10 seconds.
```

## Objects and collections

### 18. Unboxing and first impressions
Open new products with only your hands in frame and share honest first impressions in voiceover. The reveal moment is built in, and buyers search for unboxings before they order.
> "Opening the camera everyone's been asking about."

Fits [unboxing](/formats/unboxing) and [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video).
```
Keep the box opening at normal speed and the natural sound loud. Cut every pause from my voiceover. Zoom in on each item as I name it. Put a short spec label next to each item, like weight or size, as a motion graphic. End on all the items laid out from above.
```

### 19. Collection showcases
Show one part of a collection per video, like records, trading cards, stamps, or vintage cameras. Collectors love the detail, and you never run out of items to film.
> "Every record I bought this year, and the story behind each."

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head) and [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video).
```
Make one section per item with the name and year as a title card. Use a slow overhead pan of each item while I tell its story in voiceover. Number the items in the top right. Put soft music under everything and duck it when I speak.
```

### 20. Side by side tests
Compare two versions of the same thing, like two pens, two brands of paint, or two coffee makers, with only your hands and the results in frame. A comparison promises a winner, and viewers stay for the verdict.
> "Cheap fountain pen versus expensive fountain pen."

Fits [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review) and [product demo](/formats/product-demo).
```
Use a vertical split screen, with option A on the left and option B on the right, labeled at the top. Run each test on both sides at the same time. Add a score in each corner that updates after each test. End with the winner full screen and a short summary.
```

### 21. Desk setups
Show your desk and workspace, with each item explained in voiceover. Desk setup videos get saved and shared by people building their own.
> "Everything on my desk, and the one thing I'd buy again."

Fits [studio tour](/formats/studio-tour) and [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll).
```
Open on a slow push-in toward the full desk. Then cut to close-ups of each item with its name as a small label. Use smooth slider-style moves on every shot. Lo-fi music under the voiceover. 16:9, with a 9:16 version showing only the top five items.
```

### 22. Plant and garden timelapses
Set up a phone on a stand and film your plants or garden as they grow over weeks. Growth timelapses are hard to look away from, and each season gives you new footage.
> "Eight weeks of tomato plants in 60 seconds."

Fits [art timelapse](/formats/art-timelapse) and [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation).
```
Put the timelapse clips in date order and play them as one continuous growth shot. Add a week counter in the top left. Slow down for the first flower and the first fruit. Calm piano music, and end on the harvest held for 3 seconds.
```

## Voice-led channels

### 23. Story time over your own footage
Tell true stories, your own or ones people send you with permission, over b-roll you film yourself. The voice carries the story, so the footage can be simple, like walks, coffee, or rain.
> "The time I got locked out of my apartment for 14 hours."

Fits [story time](/formats/story-time) and [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video).
```
Cut every pause and repeated start from my voiceover. Match each sentence with a b-roll clip from my footage that fits what I'm describing. Captions on, 3 to 5 words at a time, bold white. Bring the music in only during the turning point of the story.
```

### 24. The history of a place
Research the history of your town, a street, or a building, and narrate it over footage you shoot there today. Local history channels have loyal audiences, and nobody else is covering your town.
> "This empty lot used to be the biggest theater in the city."

Fits [explainer](/formats/explainer) and [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video).
```
Place each shot of the building under the sentence that describes it. Add the year as large text whenever my narration jumps to a new date. Use a slow zoom on still shots. Add a short map motion graphic at the start to show where the place is.
```

### 25. Book notes with page flips
Read a book, then share your notes over footage of your hands flipping through it and your handwritten notes. Viewers get the main ideas in minutes, and the book footage feels personal.
> "Five ideas from this book I keep coming back to."

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head) and [sketchbook flip-through](/formats/sketchbook-flip-through).
```
Make one section per idea with the number and a short title as a card. Use page flip and note close-up shots under my voiceover. Highlight the quoted line on the page with a yellow box when I read it. Quiet music at 10 percent.
```

### 26. An audio-first podcast
Record a podcast with only the microphones, hands, and the room on camera. Podcast clips cut well into short videos, and nobody has to be on camera.
> "Two friends, two mics, one topic a week."

Fits [podcast clip](/formats/podcast-clip).
```
Use shots of the mics, hands, and coffee cups as the visuals. Switch to the mic of whoever is speaking, using the transcript to know who's talking. Add an audio waveform motion graphic at the bottom. Cut three 45-second 9:16 clips from the best moments, captioned.
```

### 27. Your day from your point of view
Film your day with the phone at chest height, so viewers see what you see. You still get the appeal of a day in the life, and your face stays out of it.
> "A day at the flower market from 5am."

Fits [POV video](/formats/pov-video) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Keep the clips in recording order with the time in small text in the top right. Speed up walking to 2x and keep the interesting moments at normal speed. Put my voice memo notes from the day over the clips as voiceover with captions. 9:16 and 16:9 versions.
```

## FAQ

**Do faceless channels work on YouTube?**
Yes. Process, tutorial, walk, and ambience channels are common on YouTube, and many run for years without the creator on camera.

**What gear do I need for a faceless channel?**
A phone, an overhead arm or a tripod, and a decent microphone if you plan to do voiceover. Screen-recorded channels need only a computer and a mic.

**Do I have to do a voiceover?**
You can skip it. ASMR, ambience, walk, and timelapse channels run on natural sound or music alone.

**Can I use clips from other channels?**
Film or record your own footage. Vyra edits what you upload, and original footage is what keeps a channel yours.
