**A repair video needs a visible fault and a result that can be checked. Film those from comparable angles and keep any limits of the repair in your explanation.**

These ideas are for people documenting their own repairs. Choose the one you can support with your own recordings. Keep the explanation tied to what the camera actually shows.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The symptom before the work

Show the sticking drawer before the repair and the same movement afterwards. Keep the camera position similar so the change is visible.

### 2. The part that explained the fault

Show a removed or accessible part and the observation that mattered. Use close footage you already recorded without asking viewers to repeat risky work.

### 3. The check after the fix

Film the check you actually performed and describe its scope. Avoid implying a short clip proves long-term reliability.

## Put the edit together on iPhone

1. Choose one idea and gather the source footage that explains it.
2. Add your clips to a project in the [Vyra iPhone app](/ai-video-editor-app).
3. Describe the sequence and the words or sounds that must stay intact.
4. Review the draft for missing context and text covering important details.
5. Export and play the saved video before sharing it.

## FAQ

**Should the edit show every repair step?**
These ideas document your experience. Include the context needed to understand the result, and avoid turning incomplete footage into an instruction someone could mistake for a full procedure.

**How do I make the before and after fair?**
Use a comparable angle and the same visible action. Keep a limitation in the video if it changes how the result should be understood.
