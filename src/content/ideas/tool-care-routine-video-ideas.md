**Record the starting condition, the care step and the result you can actually see. Keep the explanation within the routine and product guidance you can verify.**

These ideas are for makers documenting their own tool-care process. Pick the story your existing clips can explain, then film a missing view only when it adds needed context.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. Before the next session

Show the tool after use, the routine you follow and its stored state. Keep a relevant warning or limitation in the explanation.

### 2. One visible check

Record the condition you inspect before using the tool again. Describe what you observed without claiming the video proves long-term performance.

### 3. What I changed in the routine

Show the previous problem and the documented change you made. Keep a product instruction separate from a personal preference.

## FAQ

**Should I turn a personal habit into universal advice?**
Describe the routine you actually use. Check the maker’s instructions before presenting a material or care method as appropriate for everyone.

**Does a clean-looking tool prove the process worked?**
Show what the recording establishes. Appearance alone may not establish condition or long-term performance.
