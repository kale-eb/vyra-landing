**Start with a change the camera can show clearly. A clay wall becoming even, a trimmed foot or a glaze result gives a ceramics video a sequence to follow.**

These ideas are for ceramic artists filming their own material. Pick the one that fits the clips you can make or already have. Keep the useful explanation and give the finished result enough screen time to see it.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The rim before and after one pass

Film the uneven rim, one useful pass and the new edge from the same angle. Keep the final rim visible long enough to compare. Cut the waiting while preserving the direction of the hand movement.

### 2. The foot you rarely see

Show the underside before trimming, the tool meeting the base and the finished foot. Finish with the piece turned upright so the detail has context. Use a short label if the difference is hard to spot on a phone.

### 3. A glaze prediction and its result

Record the labeled test tile and your expectation, then show the fired result in comparable light. Keep the spoken prediction intact. Put the actual result on screen before explaining what surprised you.

## FAQ

**How do I show a small change in the clay?**
Keep the framing close and consistent before and after the action. A change in angle can hide the improvement.

**Should I speed up every wheel shot?**
Use speed changes where the wait adds little. Keep the moment that explains the shaping action visible at a readable pace.
