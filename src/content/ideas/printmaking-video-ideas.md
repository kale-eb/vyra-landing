**A print reveal has a natural ending. Film enough of the setup and the lift that the viewer can connect the result with the block or plate.**

These ideas are for printmakers filming their own material. Pick the one that fits the clips you can make or already have. Keep the useful explanation and give the finished result enough screen time to see it.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. One pull from start to finish

Record the inked surface, paper placement, pressure and the lift. Keep the reveal in real order. Shorten repetitive preparation while leaving enough footage to understand how the printed image appears.

### 2. The detail that did not print

Show a close-up of the block and the matching area on the print. Explain the difference in one sentence. Keep the two views close together in the edit so people can compare them.

### 3. One design, two papers

Film each print in similar light and show the paper names beside the results. End with both prints together. Avoid changing the color grade between them because that can make the comparison confusing.

## A brief to adapt

> Build the sequence around the paper lift. Keep the lift uncut, shorten the repeated rolling and show the matching detail on the block and print afterward.

## FAQ

**Should I open with the finished print?**
Use a brief finished view when it helps establish the design. Keep enough of the later reveal to make the process satisfying.

**How can I show paper texture?**
Choose a source shot where the texture is visible in the light. Hold it still long enough to inspect before moving on.
