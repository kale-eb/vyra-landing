**A vlog is a video of your real life, filmed as it happens and cut down to the parts worth watching. The vlogs people finish have one thread running through them, like a deadline, a trip, a first day, or a question you answer by the end. Here are 28 vlog ideas in five groups, with daily vlogs, travel vlogs, and student and study vlogs each getting their own. Each one has a line to open on, the format it fits, and a prompt that turns your raw clips into a cut in Vyra.**

My most watched vlog post was a demo of hours of vlog footage cut down in Vyra, and it got 2.9M views. My day one at Y Combinator vlog got 73.2K. Both had a clear start and a clear end, and the edit kept only what moved the day forward.

## How to use this list

1. Pick an idea before you start filming, so you know which thread to follow all day.
2. Film short clips of 3 to 10 seconds. They cut together faster than long takes.
3. Say one sentence to camera early in the day that sets up what the vlog is about.
4. Upload everything to Vyra, paste the prompt, and change the details to match your footage.

## Daily vlogs

### 1. The one-errand day
Build the whole vlog around one small task, like returning a package or getting a haircut. A tiny goal gives a slow day a beginning, a middle, and an end.
> "Today's only goal is to finally return this jacket."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Open on the clip where I hold up the jacket and say today's goal. Keep the walk to the store under 20 seconds using the best 2-second clips. Add a checkmark graphic in the center when I hand the jacket over at the counter, then end on the next clip with no outro. 9:16, captions on in white, bottom third.
```

### 2. Same coffee, five mornings
Film your morning coffee every weekday for one week, then cut the five mornings together. The repeated shot gives the viewer something to compare, and small changes between days become the story.
> "Same order, five very different mornings."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [montage](/formats/montage).
```
Make one section per day, in recording order. Put the day name as text in the top left at the start of each section. Use the coffee pour from each day as the transition into the next day. Lo-fi music under everything, ducked to 20 percent when I talk. Keep the whole video under 90 seconds.
```

### 3. The reset day
Clean the room, do the laundry, restock the fridge. People watch reset vlogs for the before and after, so film both from the same spot.
> "My room has looked like this for two weeks."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation).
```
Open on the messy room for 1 second, then the clean room for 1 second, then go back to the start. Speed the cleaning clips up 4x and cut them on the beat. Slow back to normal speed for the final pan across the clean room and let the music swell there.
```

### 4. The 5am experiment
Wake up at 5am for one day and report on it honestly, hour by hour. The early time works as a challenge, and viewers stay to see if you make it to lunch.
> "I tried waking up at 5am. Here's how it went, hour by hour."

Fits [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Add a clock graphic in the top right that shows the time from each clip's timestamp. Cut every pause in my talking clips. When I yawn, hold the frame for half a second and zoom in 15 percent. Captions on, 3 words at a time, yellow on the key word.
```

### 5. The commute vlog
Film only your commute, every day for a week, and talk about what you notice on the way. A fixed route gives you the same frame each day, so the people and the weather do the work.
> "Twenty minutes on the same train, five days in a row."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [talking head b-roll](/formats/talking-head-b-roll).
```
Cut the commute into a 45-second piece. Use only window shots and walking shots, 1.5 seconds each, cut to the beat of a calm instrumental. Put the voice memo I recorded on the walk over the top as voiceover, with captions in a small serif font at the bottom.
```

### 6. Day one of something
Film the first day of a new job, gym, program, or class. Firsts come with nerves built in, and the viewer wants to know how it went.
> "Day one. I have no idea where anything is."

Fits [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story) and [founder build in public](/formats/founder-build-in-public). My day one at Y Combinator got 73.2K views.
```
Open on the shot of the building entrance with "day one" as large text. Put a small label in the lower left each time I arrive somewhere new. Captions on every talking clip, 3 to 5 words at a time. End on the last shot of the night with the music fading out over 2 seconds.
```

## Travel vlogs

### 7. One day, one city
Spend 24 hours in one city and label every stop. A single day keeps the vlog tight and doubles as a guide people save for later.
> "24 hours in Lisbon, eight stops, all on foot."

Fits [travel recap](/formats/travel-recap) and [vlog](/formats/vlog).
```
Add a location label with a pin icon at the start of each new place, lower left. Keep each place to about 15 seconds. Between places, add a map motion graphic that draws a line from one stop to the next. 16:9 for YouTube, with music that changes at each new neighborhood.
```

### 8. Everything I ate
Make a travel vlog that covers only the food. Food clips are short and satisfying, and a price on screen gives viewers a reason to save it.
> "Everything I ate in Mexico City, ranked at the end."

Fits [travel recap](/formats/travel-recap).
```
Make each dish its own section. Open each section on the first bite, then cut back to ordering. Put the name of the place and the price as text along the bottom. Add a short pop sound when each price appears. End with a ranked list of all the dishes as a motion graphic.
```

### 9. Airport to bed
Film one full travel day in order, from leaving the house to falling asleep at the hotel. Long travel days are relatable, and the time change gives you a built-in joke.
> "Left the house at 4am. Landed at 2pm. Still have a whole day ahead."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Keep the clips in recording order. Show the local time in the corner of each clip from its timestamp, and switch to the new time zone on the first clip after landing. Speed the long walking shots to 2x. End on me in bed with the lights off and 2 seconds of silence.
```

### 10. The packing list, then the reality
Pack on camera, then show which items you actually used on the trip. The second half pays off the first, and the unused items make people laugh.
> "I packed 14 things. I used six."

Fits [travel recap](/formats/travel-recap) and [unboxing](/formats/unboxing).
```
Split the video into two halves. In the first half, each item goes into the bag with its name as text, one item per beat. In the second half, show the same items being used on the trip, with a green check or a red cross over each one. Same song across both halves.
```

### 11. The trip on a budget
Give yourself a fixed amount for the trip and show every purchase as it happens. A running total adds tension to ordinary shots of buying snacks.
> "I gave myself $200 for three days in Tokyo."

Fits [travel recap](/formats/travel-recap) and [vlog](/formats/vlog).
```
Add a running total in the top right that goes up each time I pay for something, using the amounts I say out loud in the clips. Turn the total red when it passes $200. Captions on, bottom third. End on a summary card with the final total.
```

### 12. The silent travel vlog
Skip the talking and keep only the sound of the place. Rain, traffic, and market noise carry a city better than narration.
> "Kyoto, three days, no talking."

Fits [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog) and [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll).
```
Use no music and no voice. Keep the natural sound from each clip and crossfade it for half a second between shots. Hold each shot for 3 to 4 seconds. Put only the city name and the date in small text on the first shot. 16:9.
```

## Student and study vlogs

### 13. Study with me in real time
Record a full study session with a timer on screen. Viewers play it in the background and study alongside you, so long watch times come naturally.
> "Four rounds of 25 minutes. Let's go."

Fits [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog).
```
Make a study session with a 25-minute countdown timer centered at the top. Keep each round as one continuous shot of the desk. When the timer ends, cut to a 5-minute break section with soft music and the word "break" in the center. Repeat for 4 rounds. 16:9.
```

### 14. Exam week countdown
Film a short check-in every day of exam week. A countdown gives each day a stake, and the last clip after the final exam lands as a release.
> "Five days until my organic chemistry final."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Make one section per day with "5 days to go", "4 days to go", and so on in large text at the start of each. Use my notes and laptop clips as b-roll under my talking clips. Keep music quiet all week, then bring it in loud on the first clip after the exam.
```

### 15. First week back
Film the first week of a new semester, from move-in to the first Friday night. New schedules, new rooms, and old friends give you plenty of variety.
> "First week back. My room is still in boxes."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog). My first week back at college got 8.5K views.
```
Cut the week into 7 short sections with the day name in the top left. Open on the move-in shot. Keep talking clips under 6 seconds each and cut the rest as b-roll on the beat. 9:16, captions on in a clean sans serif, centered low.
```

### 16. The all-nighter
Film the night before a big deadline, start to finish. The clock does the storytelling, and people stay to see if you submit on time.
> "Midterm is at 9am and I haven't started."

Fits [all-nighter painting vlog](/formats/all-nighter-painting-vlog) and [vlog](/formats/vlog). My all-nighter for a painting midterm got 9.1K views.
```
Put the time from each clip's timestamp in the bottom right. Speed up everything between midnight and 5am to 8x. Slow to normal speed for the moment I finish. Add the word "submitted" in large text when I click send, then cut to black.
```

### 17. Where I actually study
Give a tour of the places you study and work on campus. Students love seeing other campuses, and prospective students search for exactly this.
> "Every spot I study at, ranked by how much I get done."

Fits [studio tour](/formats/studio-tour) and [vlog](/formats/vlog). My tour of the Brown University painting studios got 262K views.
```
Make one section per place, with the place name as a title card for 1 second. Use one slow pan to open each place, then my talking clip. Add a score out of 10 in the top right of each section. End on the top-ranked spot with music rising.
```

### 18. The semester in 60 seconds
Save one clip from each week of the semester and cut them into one minute at the end. The recap rewards everyone who followed along, and it reaches new viewers on its own.
> "Fifteen weeks of junior year in one minute."

Fits [semester recap montage](/formats/semester-recap-montage).
```
Put the clips in date order, one clip per week, each cut to exactly 4 beats of the song. Add the week number in small text in the top left. Speed up any clip longer than 4 seconds so it fits. 9:16, and end on the last clip of the final week held for 2 seconds.
```

## Project and life-event vlogs

### 19. Build something in one night
Pick a small project and finish it before sunrise. A clear finish line makes every clip feel like progress.
> "One night to build my personal website."

Fits [founder build in public](/formats/founder-build-in-public) and [all-nighter painting vlog](/formats/all-nighter-painting-vlog). My one-night personal website build got 14.9K views.
```
Open on the finished website for 1.5 seconds, then cut to the blank screen at the start. Use screen recordings at 6x speed with my talking clips at normal speed in between. Zoom in on the cursor whenever something new appears on the page.
```

### 20. A shift at your job
Film one full shift at your job, from clocking in to clocking out. Jobs like barista, bookseller, or lifeguard are familiar to everyone and still full of details people have never seen.
> "An eight-hour shift at a bakery, starting at 4am."

Fits [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Show the shift time in the top left from each clip's timestamp. Keep the setup clips short, about 1 second each, cut on the beat. Slow down for the moment the first customer walks in. Captions on my talking clips in white with a dark outline.
```

### 21. Moving day
Film packing, moving, and the first night in the new place. The empty room at the end is the payoff, and the chaos in the middle is easy to cut.
> "Everything I own fit in one car. Barely."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation).
```
Open on the empty new room for 2 seconds with the date on screen. Then go back to packing. Use a box counter in the corner that goes up with each box I carry. End on a slow pan of the finished room with the same angle as the opening shot.
```

### 22. Learn one skill in seven days
Pick a skill you can't do yet, like juggling or a kickflip, and practice every day for a week. Failure is funny, and the last-day attempt gives the whole vlog a climax.
> "I can't skateboard. I have one week."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned).
```
Make one section per day with "Day 1" to "Day 7" as a title card. In each section, show my worst attempt first, then my best. Add a fail counter in the top right that goes up with each fall. On day 7, play the final attempt at normal speed with no music, then bring the track in when I land it.
```

### 23. Try a friend's hobby for a day
Spend a day doing whatever your friend does on weekends, like climbing, pottery, or birdwatching. Your friend is the expert, you are the beginner, and the gap makes the vlog.
> "My friend has been climbing for six years. I've been climbing for six minutes."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Put a small name label on my friend the first time they appear. Cut between my friend's attempt and mine on the same move, side by side if the angles match. Captions on all talking. Upbeat music, ducked to 15 percent under dialogue.
```

## Story-led vlogs

### 24. The last day somewhere
Film your last day in an apartment, a city, or a job. Endings carry their own emotion, so the edit can stay quiet.
> "Last night in the apartment I lived in for four years."

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video) and [vlog](/formats/vlog).
```
Keep the clips in order and hold each shot a little longer than usual, 3 to 5 seconds. Use soft piano music at low volume. Put my voiceover from the end of the day over the empty rooms. Captions in a light serif font, lower third. End on the door closing and a black frame for 2 seconds.
```

### 25. A day with someone you love
Spend a day with a grandparent, a parent, or an old friend and let them talk. Their stories carry the vlog, and the people closest to them will share it.
> "My grandma taught me her dumpling recipe today."

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video) and [talking head b-roll](/formats/talking-head-b-roll).
```
Use her voice as the main audio. Find the three best stories in the transcript and build the video around them, with cooking clips as b-roll under each one. Captions on her speech in a warm yellow. Fade the music out completely whenever she tells a story.
```

### 26. The honest bad week
Talk through a rough week over clips of the week itself. Honesty builds trust with your audience, and quiet b-roll keeps the talking from feeling heavy.
> "This week didn't go the way I planned."

Fits [story time](/formats/story-time) and [talking head b-roll](/formats/talking-head-b-roll).
```
Cut my talking clip down to the key sentences, removing every pause and repeated start. Cover every other sentence with b-roll from the week. Keep the music low and slow. Captions on, 4 to 6 words at a time, white, centered low. 9:16.
```

### 27. The vlog that answers a question
Start with a question about your life and answer it by the end, like whether you can cook every meal at home for a week. The question is the hook, and the answer is the reason to stay.
> "Can I go a whole week without ordering takeout?"

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned).
```
Open on the question as large text over the first clip. Add a day counter in the top left. Cut the cooking clips to 1 second each on the beat. Save my answer for the final 10 seconds and put it on screen in large text after I say it.
```

### 28. Film everything, find the story later
Film all day with no plan, then let the edit find the story. Vyra transcribes every word and describes every scene, so it can find the funny lines and best shots in hours of footage.
> "I filmed everything today. Here's what was worth keeping."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog). My demo of hours of vlog footage, edited in Vyra, got 2.9M views.
```
Watch all of today's footage and cut it to a 3-minute vlog. Keep the three funniest lines from the transcript and the best shot from each location. Remove every clip where nothing happens. Add music between talking sections and captions on every line. 9:16.
```

## FAQ

**How long should a vlog be?**
Short vlogs for TikTok and Reels usually run 30 to 90 seconds. YouTube vlogs usually run 8 to 15 minutes, with the setup line in the first 15 seconds.

**What should I film if my day is boring?**
Pick one small goal and film the attempt. A quiet day with a goal is easier to watch than a busy day without one.

**Do I need to talk to the camera?**
You can skip it. A silent vlog with natural sound works, and so does a voiceover recorded at the end of the day.

**How do I edit hours of vlog footage?**
Upload all of it to Vyra and describe the cut you want. It transcribes every word and describes every scene, so it can find the best clip of each place without you scrubbing through.
