**Choose one phrase of movement and let it play long enough to follow. Use the rehearsal recordings to show a decision without presenting an edited sequence as one continuous take.**

These ideas are for dancers reviewing rehearsal recordings. Choose the one you can support with your own recordings. Keep the explanation tied to what the camera actually shows.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. One phrase across two rehearsals

Show the same movement phrase from two recordings and label their dates if known. Keep enough lead-in that the comparison starts at the right place.

### 2. The count where we changed direction

Show a short explanation and the rehearsal moment it refers to. Keep the useful music or count audible.

### 3. The wide take and one detail

Start with a wide view of the phrase and then show a detail you recorded separately. Make the change in angle clear so timing is not misrepresented.

## Put the edit together on iPhone

1. Choose one idea and gather the source footage that explains it.
2. Add your clips to a project in the [Vyra iPhone app](/ai-video-editor-app).
3. Describe the sequence and the words or sounds that must stay intact.
4. Review the draft for missing context and text covering important details.
5. Export and play the saved video before sharing it.

## A brief to adapt

> Keep the full eight-count phrase in the wide shot. Then show the separate close-up as a detail. Preserve the original rehearsal count and label the later take.

## FAQ

**Should every cut land on a beat?**
A beat can be a useful cue, but the movement must remain understandable. Keep the full phrase when cutting would hide the action you want to show.

**Can I publish the rehearsal music?**
Check your rights and the platform’s rules for the recording. Owning the rehearsal footage does not automatically grant music publishing rights.
