**Use the same view across recorded dates to show a change in the garden. Label the actual interval instead of making a short montage stand in for unrecorded growth.**

These ideas are for people filming their own gardens. Choose the one you can support with your own recordings. Keep the explanation tied to what the camera actually shows.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. One plant across recorded dates

Choose matching views from your own camera roll and label the dates you know. Keep the final plant visible long enough to inspect.

### 2. The corner before and after a change

Show the area before the work and after the change. Explain what you did without implying the edit proves a treatment works for every garden.

### 3. A harvest with the preparation

Show the produce you picked and the earlier preparation footage you actually have. Preserve any missing interval in the explanation.

## Put the edit together on iPhone

1. Choose one idea and gather the source footage that explains it.
2. Add your clips to a project in the [Vyra iPhone app](/ai-video-editor-app).
3. Describe the sequence and the words or sounds that must stay intact.
4. Review the draft for missing context and text covering important details.
5. Export and play the saved video before sharing it.

## A brief to adapt

> Order the matching plant shots by their recorded dates. Keep the date labels accurate and hold the final view. Do not invent a growth interval for the missing weeks.

## FAQ

**Can I call any montage a timelapse?**
Explain whether it is a continuous capture or selected clips from different days. Labels help the viewer understand the actual record.

**Should I include a growing claim in the caption?**
Limit claims to what your recording and reliable sources establish. A visible result in one garden does not prove a general treatment effect.
