**Keep the conditions of a material comparison visible. Show the same kind of mark or action so viewers can see what changed and what stayed the same.**

These ideas are for artists comparing materials they have tested. Choose the one you can support with footage or records you already have. Keep the explanation close to what the viewer can actually see.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The same mark on two surfaces

Film the same mark on each surface and label the materials. Keep lighting and framing comparable, then hold the results together long enough to inspect.

### 2. The dry result after the wet look

Show each recorded stage and state the actual interval if known. Do not imply an unrecorded drying time from a fast cut.

### 3. The limit of my sample

Show the test area and explain what the sample can establish. A small trial does not prove long-term durability or every possible use.

## FAQ

**Should I use different lighting to make the difference clearer?**
Keep the lighting comparable when presenting a comparison. Explain a lighting change if the source recordings differ.

**Can I recommend one material from a small test?**
Describe what happened in the test you recorded. Keep broader safety, durability or performance claims separate unless you have reliable evidence.
