**Show the task you started, the point where you checked your understanding and the work you actually finished. A tidy desk alone cannot show what you learned.**

These ideas are for students documenting their own study habits. Choose the one you can support with your own recordings. Keep the explanation tied to what the camera actually shows.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The question I could not explain

Start with your own description of the problem, show a short practice passage and finish with your checked explanation. Keep private course or student details out of frame.

### 2. A session with one stopping rule

Show the work you intended to complete and where you stopped. Use time labels only when you recorded the times accurately.

### 3. The page I revised

Compare your first notes with a clearer version and explain one change. Hold the relevant area long enough for it to be readable.

## Put the edit together on iPhone

1. Choose one idea and gather the source footage that explains it.
2. Add your clips to a project in the [Vyra iPhone app](/ai-video-editor-app).
3. Describe the sequence and the words or sounds that must stay intact.
4. Review the draft for missing context and text covering important details.
5. Export and play the saved video before sharing it.

> Use the original notes, the short practice clip and the revised page. Keep my explanation of what I still do not understand. Do not add a study-time number I did not record.

## FAQ

**Should I make the study session look longer?**
Use the real sequence and any times you actually recorded. The useful part is explaining the task and outcome.

**How can I avoid exposing private information?**
Inspect screens, papers and notifications before importing or sharing footage. Remove sensitive material from the source or keep it outside the published frame.
