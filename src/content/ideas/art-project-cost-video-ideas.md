**Use costs you actually recorded and label what is included. Keep materials, time and an eventual sale price distinct so the comparison makes sense.**

These ideas are for makers explaining a project’s recorded costs. Choose the one you can support with footage or records you already have. Keep the explanation close to what the viewer can actually see.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The materials I used

Show the materials and the recorded amounts used for this project. Explain whether a figure is the whole purchase or only the part used.

### 2. The cost I forgot to count

Show a recorded expense you left out of the first total and explain the corrected scope. Keep the source record private where it contains personal details.

### 3. Two versions with different inputs

Compare two of your own versions and the documented differences in materials or time. Do not imply the more expensive version necessarily sold better.

## FAQ

**Should I include a cost I only estimated?**
Label an estimate as an estimate and keep it separate from recorded amounts. Do not give it the same apparent precision as a receipt.

**Does a material total show profit?**
No. A material total alone leaves out other costs and revenue. State the scope of the figure shown instead of calling it profit.
