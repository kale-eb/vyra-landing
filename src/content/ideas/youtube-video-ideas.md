**A good YouTube video idea is one somebody would type into the search bar, or click when it shows up next to a video they already watched. Long videos give you room for a real story, a full tutorial, or an honest test. Here are 28 YouTube video ideas in six groups, starting with ideas for beginners. Each one has a line to say, the format it fits, and a prompt that builds an 8 to 15 minute 16:9 edit in Vyra from your footage.**

Most of these ideas need one camera, a quiet room, and a weekend. Titles carry a lot of weight on YouTube, so many ideas start from a phrase people already search for. Use the list for YouTube content ideas whenever your own list runs dry.

## How to use this list

1. Pick an idea you could film this week with what you already own.
2. Write the title before you film. The first 30 seconds have to deliver what the title promises.
3. Film more than you need. Long videos need b-roll to cover the cuts in your talking.
4. Upload the footage to Vyra, paste the prompt, and change the timings to match your video.

## YouTube video ideas for beginners

### 1. The setup you actually use
Show the camera, apps, and space you film with, including the cheap stuff. Beginners search for this, and you already own everything in it.
> "Everything I use to make videos, and what I'd skip."

Fits [studio tour](/formats/studio-tour) and [talking head with b-roll](/formats/talking-head-b-roll).
```
Make a 9 to 11 minute 16:9 video. Open with a 20 second cold open of the best wide shot of the full setup, then my line "everything I use to make videos". Add a chapter marker and a lower third with the item name each time I pick up something new. Cover every cut in my talking with a close-up of the item I'm talking about. Keep the music low and duck it further whenever I speak.
```

### 2. Your first attempt, filmed honestly
Try something hard for the first time and keep the bad parts. Viewers like watching someone learn, and you need no experience to make it.
> "I've never done this before. Let's see how bad it is."

Fits [process video](/formats/process-video) and [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned).
```
Cut this into a 10 minute 16:9 video in the order I filmed it. Keep my reactions to mistakes, and speed up the slow middle stretches to 4x with a small "4x" label in the corner. Add a progress bar along the bottom that fills as the project gets closer to done. End on the finished result held for 5 seconds with no music.
```

### 3. The ten questions everyone asks
List the ten questions beginners ask in your niche and answer each in under a minute. Each question becomes a chapter, and each chapter can show up in search.
> "These are the ten questions I get asked the most."

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head).
```
Make a 12 minute 16:9 video with ten chapters, one per question. Put each question on a full-screen title card for 2 seconds before I answer it. Cut every pause longer than half a second. Add captions at the bottom, white with a black outline, and show the question number in the top left corner.
```

### 4. Explain it to a friend
Sit a friend who knows nothing about your topic next to you and explain it to them. Their questions are the same ones your viewers would ask.
> "Okay, explain it like I've never heard of it."

Fits [explainer](/formats/explainer).
```
Cut this two-person conversation to 9 minutes in 16:9. Switch to whoever is speaking, and use the wide shot when we laugh or talk over each other. When I name a term for the first time, show it as a text card on the right third of the frame for 3 seconds. Remove the long setup at the start and open on my friend's first question.
```

### 5. Rank everything you own
Line up every item in one category, such as pens, sneakers, or houseplants, and rank them from worst to best. A tier list gives the video a built-in ending.
> "I own 23 pens. Let's find out which ones are bad."

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head) and [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review).
```
Make an 11 minute 16:9 video. Build a tier list graphic on the right side of the screen with rows S, A, B, C and D, and add each item's name to its row at the moment I say the rank. Keep the full list on screen for the last 20 seconds. Use upbeat background music at low volume and cut every pause.
```

## Videos people search for

### 6. How to do it, for complete beginners
Pick one skill and teach it from zero, in the order a beginner would need it. Put the exact phrase people search for in the title.
> "By the end of this video you'll have made your first one."

Fits [tutorial](/formats/tutorial).
```
Make a 14 minute 16:9 tutorial. Add a chapter at each new step and show the step number and name as a lower third when it starts. Cut to the overhead camera whenever my hands are doing the work, and back to my face when I explain why. Zoom in 20 percent on the hands for any detail shot under 5 seconds. Keep music off during the steps and use it only in the intro and outro.
```

### 7. This one or that one
Compare two popular options with the same tests. People search for this right before they choose.
> "I used both for a month. Here's the one I'd pick."

Fits [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review) and [product demo](/formats/product-demo).
```
Make a 10 minute 16:9 comparison. Use a split screen for every test, the first option on the left and the second on the right, with the name over each half. After each test, update a small scoreboard in the top corner. End on a full-screen verdict card with the winner's name for 4 seconds.
```

### 8. Everything you need to start
Walk through the short list of things a beginner needs, and the things they can skip for now. A starter kit video answers a question people ask every week.
> "You need five things. Everything else can wait."

Fits [unboxing](/formats/unboxing) and [talking head with b-roll](/formats/talking-head-b-roll).
```
Make an 8 to 10 minute 16:9 video. Each time I introduce an item, show a clean product shot of it on a plain background with its name as a lower third. Add a running checklist on the left side that ticks off each item as I cover it. Put the "skip these" section after a chapter break with a slower track.
```

### 9. Beginner mistakes and the fix for each
List the mistakes you made when you started, then show what you do now. Real mistakes make the advice believable.
> "Mistake number one cost me a whole year."

Fits [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned).
```
Make a 12 minute 16:9 video with one chapter per mistake. For each one, show the old footage or photo with a red "before" label, then cut to the current version with a green "now" label. Open each chapter with the mistake on a title card. Bring in a soft piano track under the whole video at low volume.
```

### 10. One topic explained in ten minutes
Pick a topic people find confusing and explain it with diagrams and examples. Explainers keep getting found long after you post them.
> "Here's how it works, start to finish."

Fits [explainer](/formats/explainer) and [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video).
```
Make a 10 minute 16:9 explainer. Each time I name a new part of the process, add an animated diagram that builds one piece at a time in the center of the frame, with me shrunk to a circle in the bottom right corner. Show the complete diagram again at the end for 8 seconds. Use calm instrumental music at low volume and caption only the key terms.
```

## Challenges and experiments

### 11. Thirty days of one habit
Do one thing every day for 30 days and film a short check-in each day. The day counter keeps people watching to see if you make it.
> "Day 1. I already regret this."

Fits [montage](/formats/montage) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Make a 13 minute 16:9 video from my 30 daily check-ins in date order. Show "Day 1" through "Day 30" as a large counter in the top left that changes at each new day. Keep the days where something changed at full length and cut the quiet days down to one sentence each. At day 30, play day 1 and day 30 side by side in a split screen.
```

### 12. Cheap tools against expensive tools
Make the same thing twice, once with budget tools and once with high-end ones, then compare the results. Viewers want to know if the expensive version is worth it.
> "Same project, two budgets."

Fits [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review) and [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation).
```
Cut this as a 12 minute 16:9 video that alternates between the two builds at every step. Put a price tag graphic in the top corner that shows which version is on screen. Line up matching camera angles so each cut between the builds lands on the same framing. End with both finished pieces side by side and a slow push in for 6 seconds.
```

### 13. Recreate something famous
Pick a famous painting, dish, outfit, or movie scene and try to recreate it from scratch. The original gives viewers a target to judge you against.
> "Can I make this with what's in my kitchen?"

Fits [process video](/formats/process-video) and [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation).
```
Make an 11 minute 16:9 video. Keep a small picture-in-picture of the original in the top right corner for the whole video so viewers can compare as I go. Add a chapter at each attempt or major step. End with the original and my version side by side, full screen, held for 8 seconds while I give myself a score out of 10.
```

### 14. Test the internet's advice
Pick three popular tips in your niche and test whether they work. You get a clear structure and an honest verdict.
> "Everyone says to do this. So I tested it."

Fits [tutorial](/formats/tutorial) and [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned).
```
Make a 10 minute 16:9 video with three chapters, one per tip. Open each chapter with a screenshot of the tip, with the account name blurred. End each chapter with a stamp that says "works", "sort of", or "skip", based on what I say. Cut every pause and caption my verdict lines in yellow.
```

### 15. Ask ten people the same question
Ask friends, coworkers, or strangers one question and edit their answers together. The range of answers carries the video.
> "What's the one thing you'd tell a beginner?"

Fits [talking head with b-roll](/formats/talking-head-b-roll) and [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video).
```
Make a 9 minute 16:9 video. Group the answers by theme instead of by person, and add each person's first name as a lower third the first time they appear. Cut between people on the beat of a soft instrumental. Open with a 15 second montage of the funniest one-word answers.
```

## Story and personal videos

### 16. Why you quit
Tell the story of leaving a job, a school, a sport, or a plan. Viewers stay for the decision and for what happened after.
> "I left halfway through the year. Here's why."

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video).
```
Structure this as a 12 minute 16:9 story in three chapters, before, the decision, and after. Open on the hardest sentence in the story, then cut to the beginning. Cover my talking with old photos and clips from each period, with the year as small text in the corner. Drop the music out completely while I describe the decision.
```

### 17. The worst day of your career
Tell a story about one day that went badly, with every detail you remember. A specific failure holds attention better than general advice.
> "Everything that could go wrong that day did."

Fits [story time](/formats/story-time).
```
Make a 9 minute 16:9 story time video. Keep my face on screen for most of it and punch in 15 percent on every sentence that ends in a laugh or a twist. Add a dark, slow track that starts only when the story turns bad. Put the date of that day on screen in the first 5 seconds.
```

### 18. One year in fifteen minutes
Cut a year of clips into one long recap, month by month. You can only make this once a year, so start saving clips now.
> "This was the year I finally started posting."

Fits [semester recap montage](/formats/semester-recap-montage) and [montage](/formats/montage).
```
Make a 15 minute 16:9 video with twelve chapters, one per month, each opening on a full-screen title card with the month name. Use my voiceover clips over the footage from each month, and fill the gaps with clips cut to the music. Grade every clip with the same warm look so the year feels like one piece. End with a 30 second fast montage of the whole year, one clip per beat.
```

### 19. A short documentary about someone you know
Film a person whose work you admire, like a parent, a local shop owner, or a teacher. A long video gives you time to let them talk.
> "She's run this shop since before I was born."

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video) and [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll).
```
Make a 12 minute 16:9 mini documentary. Open on 30 seconds of cinematic b-roll of their hands working with no voice, then bring in their first answer. Add their name and role as a lower third in a thin serif font. Cut out my questions so only their answers remain, and lay b-roll over every jump cut.
```

### 20. How you got started
Tell the story of how you started the thing your channel is about, with old photos and footage. New viewers who like one of your videos go looking for this one.
> "It started with a sketchbook my aunt gave me."

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video) and [talking head with b-roll](/formats/talking-head-b-roll). My short version, about the journey since the beginning, got 18.2K views as a reel.
```
Make a 10 minute 16:9 video in date order. Put every old photo on screen with a slow zoom and the year in the bottom left. Cut back to my face only for the lines where I reflect on the past. Use one gentle track for the whole video and raise it slightly in the gaps between chapters.
```

## Reviews and reactions

### 21. React to your first video
Watch the first video you ever posted and comment on it. Viewers enjoy the gap between then and now, and you already have the footage.
> "I have not watched this since the day I posted it."

Fits [reaction video](/formats/reaction-video).
```
Make an 8 minute 16:9 reaction. Put my old video in the top two thirds of the frame and my face in a rounded box in the bottom right. Freeze the old video each time I start talking, and resume it when I stop. Add a "then" label on the old video and a "now" label on my box.
```

### 22. The six month review
Review something you've used for months, after the excitement wore off. Honest long-term reviews are hard to find, so people look for them.
> "I've used this every day since March."

Fits [product demo](/formats/product-demo) and [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review).
```
Make a 10 minute 16:9 review with chapters for what I like, what broke, and who should buy it. Show a close-up of every scratch or wear mark I mention, with a circle around it. In the last chapter, put a pros list on the left and a cons list on the right, adding each line as I say it.
```

### 23. Rate your viewers' work
Ask viewers to send in their work and give kind, specific feedback on each piece. It builds a community and gives you a repeatable episode.
> "You sent me 200 submissions. I picked 12."

Fits [reaction video](/formats/reaction-video) and [green screen commentary](/formats/green-screen-commentary).
```
Make a 12 minute 16:9 video. Show each submission full screen for 3 seconds first, then put me in front of it green screen style while I give feedback. Add the submitter's username as a lower third. Give each one a score card from 1 to 10 at the end of its segment, and end on a grid of all twelve.
```

## Behind the scenes and series

### 24. Make the whole thing, start to finish
Film a full project and talk through each choice as you make it. Long videos let people watch the parts a short has to cut.
> "Let's make this from nothing."

Fits [process video](/formats/process-video) and [speed paint](/formats/speed-paint).
```
Make a 14 minute 16:9 process video from about three hours of footage. Keep every section where I explain a decision at normal speed, and speed up the repetitive parts between them to 8x. Show the elapsed real time in the top right. Add a chapter at each new stage and end with a slow orbit around the finished piece.
```

### 25. A workspace tour with the stories
Tour your space and tell the story behind each object. The stories turn a list of things into a video.
> "This lamp has been with me through four apartments."

Fits [studio tour](/formats/studio-tour).
```
Make a 9 minute 16:9 tour that plays as one continuous walk through the room. Hide the jump cuts under b-roll of the object I'm talking about. Add a small floor plan in the top left corner with a dot that moves to the part of the room I'm in. Use soft lo-fi music under the whole video.
```

### 26. Answer the comments
Pick the best comments from the last month and answer them in one sit-down video. It shows viewers you read what they write.
> "Someone asked if I'm ever going to finish the big canvas."

Fits [talking head](/formats/talking-head).
```
Make an 8 minute 16:9 Q&A. Before each answer, slide in the comment as a styled card from the left, with the username blurred. Cut all my pauses and filler words. Alternate between a wide crop and a close crop on each new question.
```

### 27. The monthly build update
Post the same update every month on a project or business you're building. Regular viewers come back for the next chapter.
> "Month four. Here's what worked and what didn't."

Fits [founder build in public](/formats/founder-build-in-public). My reel inviting people to follow along while I build Vyra got 77.8K views.
```
Make a 10 minute 16:9 update. Open with a 20 second recap of the past month, three clips with the key number from each as large text. Use chapters for what shipped, what broke, and what's next. Put screen recordings full screen with my face in a small box in the bottom right.
```

### 28. A sit-down conversation
Invite someone from your niche for a long conversation and cut it to the best 15 minutes. Two people often go places one person on camera wouldn't.
> "I wanted to ask you about the year you almost quit."

Fits [talking head](/formats/talking-head) and [podcast clip](/formats/podcast-clip).
```
Cut this hour-long two-camera conversation to 15 minutes in 16:9. Keep the answers with a story in them and drop the small talk. Switch cameras to whoever is speaking and use the two-shot for reactions. Before each new chapter, show a pull quote from that chapter as full-screen text.
```

## FAQ

**How long should a YouTube video be?**
Long enough to deliver what the title promises. Most ideas on this list work at 8 to 15 minutes.

**What are good YouTube video ideas for beginners?**
Ideas that need no audience and no expertise, like your first attempt at something, the questions beginners ask, or a ranking of things you own. The first group on this list is built for that.

**How do I come up with YouTube content ideas every week?**
Read your comments, type your topic into the YouTube search bar and note the suggestions, and turn one good video into a recurring series. A series means next week's idea is already decided.

**Can I turn these into Shorts?**
Yes. Pull the best 30 to 50 seconds from each long video and reframe it to 9:16 with captions.
