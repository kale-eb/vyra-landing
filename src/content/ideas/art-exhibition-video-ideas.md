**An exhibition video should let viewers see both the artwork and its placement. Start with the part of the room or piece that gives the rest of the footage context.**

These ideas are for artists showing work filming their own material. Pick the one that fits the clips you can make or already have. Keep the useful explanation and give the finished result enough screen time to see it.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. One wall from wide to detail

Film the wall as a whole, one artwork and a close detail. Return to the wide view or show the next piece. Keep the detail long enough to inspect before changing scale again.

### 2. The hang before the opening

Record the empty wall, the work being positioned and the finished installation. Keep the pieces in a recognizable order. Use a short date or exhibition title after the room has been established.

### 3. The work with the artist’s words

Record a short explanation, then show the relevant work while the sentence continues. Preserve any qualification about the material or idea. End on the artwork while the final thought finishes.

## FAQ

**How do I keep the artwork’s color recognizable?**
Compare the source and export. Clips from differently lit parts of the room may need separate corrections.

**Should I put all the exhibition information over the art?**
Choose a readable clear area or a separate title moment. Let the work have an unobstructed view as well.
