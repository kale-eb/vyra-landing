**Show a find clearly before cutting into styling or story. A useful thrift video gives viewers enough detail to understand what makes the item worth showing.**

These ideas are for thrift sellers filming their own material. Pick the one that fits the clips you can make or already have. Keep the useful explanation and give the finished result enough screen time to see it.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The detail that made you stop

Film the whole item, the label or construction detail and the item in use or styled. Keep the detail legible. A moving close-up can hide the very thing the voiceover is naming.

### 2. Three finds with different roles

Group the finds by a clear purpose, such as a base layer, a statement piece and a finishing accessory. Use one short label per role. End with the complete grouping so the sequence connects.

### 3. One item across two looks

Use matching framing for the two looks and keep the item recognizable in both. Show the complete result after each change. If the lighting changes, check that the item still looks like the same color.

> Show each find with its useful detail and one short label. Finish with the full grouping. Keep the close-up of the label steady long enough to read.

## FAQ

**Should I hide wear in the edit?**
Use a clear view of the actual item and include the details the viewer needs to understand its condition.

**How do I compare two looks?**
Use a similar camera position and hold each finished view. A side-by-side layout can help when both need to be visible together.
