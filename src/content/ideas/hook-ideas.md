**A hook is the first one to three seconds of a video, the part that decides whether anyone watches the rest. The hooks that work show the payoff first, raise the stakes, or say something the viewer recognizes about themselves. Here are 30 hook ideas in six types. Each one has a line you can say, the format it fits, and a prompt that cuts the opening in Vyra once you have the footage.**

The best hook I've posted was four words over a demo, "editors, are we cooked?" It hit 8.7M views. The second best was a complaint, "I just spent 6 hours editing this and got zero views." Neither is clever. Both say something true in under two seconds.

## How to use this list

1. Pick a type that fits the video you already filmed. Don't film for a hook you can't pay off.
2. Say the line in the first second, or put it on screen if the opening is silent.
3. Cut everything before the hook. No "hey guys", no breath, no logo.
4. Paste the prompt into Vyra with your footage, then check the first three seconds on mute.

## Show the result first

### 1. Open on the finished thing
Start on the final result, then cut back to the beginning. The viewer stays to see how you got there.
> "This took 40 hours. Here's hour one."

Fits [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation) and [speed paint](/formats/speed-paint).
```
Open on the shot of the finished piece for 1.5 seconds, then hard cut to the very first clip. Put "40 hours, start to finish" in the top third over the opening shot.
```

### 2. The two-clip reveal
Show the empty start, then jump straight to the end. No process in between until later.
> "About time I actually painted something."

Fits [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation). My two-clip reveal got 641K views.
```
Make the first two seconds a two-clip reveal. The blank canvas for 1 second, then a hard cut on the beat to the finished painting. Then play the process clips in order.
```

### 3. The finished product in someone's hands
Open on the moment someone sees or uses what you made. Their face does the work.
> "She hasn't seen it yet."

Fits [commission reveal](/formats/commission-reveal).
```
Find the clip where the client sees the piece for the first time and open on their reaction. Hold it for 2 seconds before cutting back to the start of the process.
```

### 4. The number up front
Lead with the one number that makes the result impressive. Time, size, count, or price.
> "28 by 37 inches, acrylic and oil."

Fits [process video](/formats/process-video) and [studio tour](/formats/studio-tour).
```
Put the size of the piece as large text over the first shot, centered, for 1.5 seconds. No music for the first second, then bring the track in on the first cut.
```

### 5. Sketch versus final
Put the rough version next to the finished one. People compare without being asked to.
> "Sketch vs final."

Fits [sketchbook flip-through](/formats/sketchbook-flip-through).
```
Open with a side-by-side split screen. The sketch on the left, the final piece on the right, for 2 seconds. Then go full screen on the sketch and continue.
```

## Raise the stakes

### 6. The deadline
Say how little time you have. A clock gives every following clip a reason to exist.
> "My first solo show is in 10 hours."

Fits [all-nighter painting vlog](/formats/all-nighter-painting-vlog) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Open on me saying "my first solo show is in 10 hours". Cut every pause in that line. Then add a small timer in the corner that counts down at each new clip using the clip timestamps.
```

### 7. The impossible amount
Name a workload that sounds like too much. The viewer stays to see if you finish.
> "10 ink paintings due in 10 hours."

Fits [all-nighter painting vlog](/formats/all-nighter-painting-vlog).
```
Open on the line where I say how many pieces are due. Then show one 1-second shot of each piece in order, cut to the beat, before going back to the start of the night.
```

### 8. The thing that went wrong
Start at the mistake or the disaster, then rewind.
> "This is the moment I ruined the whole canvas."

Fits [story time](/formats/story-time) and [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned).
```
Find the clip where the mistake happens and put it first, with a short freeze frame at the worst moment. Then cut to the beginning of the session.
```

### 9. The bet with yourself
Say what you're trying and what happens if you fail.
> "If this doesn't sell, I'm stopping prints."

Fits [founder build in public](/formats/founder-build-in-public).
```
Open on my line about the bet. Captions on, 3 to 5 words, bold. Cut straight into the first action shot with no pause.
```

### 10. The countdown to a launch
Say when something goes live and how ready you are.
> "Launch is Friday. Nothing works."

Fits [founder build in public](/formats/founder-build-in-public).
```
Open on the line about the launch date. Add "Friday" as large text that pops in on the word. Then cut to the messiest shot of the workspace.
```

## Say what they're thinking

### 11. The complaint everyone shares
Say the frustration out loud. It works when the viewer has felt it this week.
> "I just spent 6 hours editing this and got zero views."

Fits [talking head](/formats/talking-head) and [relatable video](/formats/relatable-video). This one got 104K views.
```
Open on the complaint line. Cut every pause and breath before it so it starts in the first frame. Then punch in 10 percent on the next sentence.
```

### 12. The POV
Put the viewer in a situation in one line of text.
> "POV: your crush's favorite color is blue."

Fits [POV video](/formats/pov-video). Mine got 67.4K views.
```
Add the POV line as text in the top third for the first 3 seconds, white with a soft shadow. No voice for the first second, just the track.
```

### 13. The confession
Admit something small and specific.
> "Does it count as an all-nighter if I slept two hours?"

Fits [relatable video](/formats/relatable-video). It got 139K views.
```
Open on the confession line as on-screen text over the first clip. Keep the first clip under 2 seconds and cut on the beat.
```

### 14. The "you're doing it wrong"
Point at a habit the viewer has. Then show the better way.
> "Stop filming your art from above."

Fits [tutorial](/formats/tutorial) and [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head).
```
Open on the clip of the wrong way with a red cross graphic, 1 second. Then hard cut to the right way with a green check, and keep going.
```

### 15. The question they've asked you
Read out a question you get often and answer it in the video.
> "How many months have you been painting?"

Fits [talking head](/formats/talking-head). It got 48.1K views.
```
Put the question at the top of the frame as if it were a comment, for the first 3 seconds. Then cut to me answering, with captions.
```

## Make them curious

### 16. The unfinished sentence
Start a sentence the viewer needs to hear the end of.
> "Nobody tells you this about art school."

Fits [story time](/formats/story-time) and [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned).
```
Open on the line "nobody tells you this". Cut it so there's no pause after the line, straight into the first point.
```

### 17. The odd object
Open on something that doesn't belong. Explain later.
> "Why is there a rock on my desk?"

Fits [relatable video](/formats/relatable-video) and [process video](/formats/process-video).
```
Open on a close-up of the object for 1.5 seconds with no context and no captions. Then cut to the wide shot that explains it.
```

### 18. The pattern
Point out something you just noticed about your own work.
> "Hmm, I'm noticing a pattern here."

Fits [montage](/formats/montage). It got 292K views.
```
Open on the line, then show 6 to 8 one-second shots of the pieces that share the pattern, cut to the beat. Add "notice anything?" as text over the montage.
```

### 19. The hidden detail
Tell them to watch for something. They'll rewatch to find it.
> "Watch the left corner."

Fits [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation) and [art timelapse](/formats/art-timelapse).
```
Add a small circle around the left corner for the first 2 seconds, then remove it. Keep the timelapse at the same speed.
```

### 20. The throwback
Show old work with no explanation, then the new work.
> "My drawings from middle school."

Fits [semester recap montage](/formats/semester-recap-montage). That one got 395K views.
```
Open on the oldest piece for 1.5 seconds with the year as text. Then cut through the rest in date order, one second each, ending on the newest.
```

## Disagree with something

Say what you think most people in your niche get wrong.

```
Open on the opinion line, captioned, with the key word in yellow. Cut every pause in the first 10 seconds.
```

### 22. The myth

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head).
```
Open on the line. Put "MYTH" as a stamp graphic over the first second. Then cut to my first reason.
```

### 23. The test
Say you tried the thing everyone recommends, and what happened.
> "I tried the color wheel trend with my art."

Fits [tutorial](/formats/tutorial). Mine got 131K views.
```
Open on the finished color wheel for 1 second, then cut back to the empty template. Use beat sync for the fill-in shots.
```

### 24. The comparison
Put two options side by side and promise a winner.
> "$5 paint vs $50 paint."

Fits [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review).
```
Open with a split screen of the two options and labels with the price over each. Hold for 2 seconds, then go to the first test.
```

### 25. The reaction
React to something in your niche. Let the viewer see what you're reacting to.
> "This is the worst advice I've seen this week."

Fits [reaction video](/formats/reaction-video) and [green screen commentary](/formats/green-screen-commentary).
```
Open with the screenshot on screen and me in front of it, green screen style. Cut straight to my first sentence with no pause.
```

## FAQ

**How long should a hook be?**
One to three seconds on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts. On YouTube, the first 15 seconds should pay off the title.

**Should the hook be spoken or on screen?**
Both, if you can. Many people watch on mute, so put the line on screen too.

**Can I add a hook after filming?**
Yes. Move a strong moment from later in the video to the front, then let the video play from the start.

**What's the most common mistake?**
Starting before the hook. A breath, a "hey guys", or a slow first shot loses people before the hook plays.
