**Film the first action you tried and the mark or result it produced. Keep a first impression separate from a claim about long-term performance.**

These ideas are for makers documenting a real first attempt. Choose the one your own recordings can support. Keep the explanation and the visible outcome connected.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The first mark

Show the tool before the first mark and keep the useful part of your reaction. Let the viewer see the actual result before you describe it.

### 2. The adjustment I needed

Show the first attempt and the change you made to your handling or setup. Explain the difference you observed rather than claiming it proves the tool is better.

### 3. The next thing I will check

Finish with a limited test you actually performed or a clearly stated next question. Keep any untested outcome in the future tense.

## FAQ

**Does the first attempt need to look successful?**
The useful part can be what you learned from the attempt. Keep the result visible instead of hiding it behind a reaction shot.

**Can I call it the best tool from one use?**
Describe the experience you recorded. A broad comparison needs more evidence than one attempt.
