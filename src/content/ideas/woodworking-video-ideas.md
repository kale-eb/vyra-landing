**Choose a visible join, surface change or working detail for the video. Give the finished object enough context that viewers can understand its size and purpose.**

These ideas are for woodworkers filming their own material. Pick the one that fits the clips you can make or already have. Keep the useful explanation and give the finished result enough screen time to see it.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The joint before it closes

Show the pieces apart, the fit coming together and the closed joint. Keep the orientation consistent across the cut. Use a wider finished shot afterward so the detail belongs to a recognizable object.

### 2. The surface across two stages

Use matching views before and after the surface work. Include a short section of the recorded process. Keep lighting as comparable as possible so the video shows the surface rather than just a brighter lamp.

### 3. The object doing its job

Film the finished drawer opening, stand holding its object or door closing. Open on the useful action, then cut back to a few build details. End on the object working again with its natural sound.

## FAQ

**Do I need every stage of the build?**
Use the stages needed to understand the one feature you are showing. A full build and a joint detail need different amounts of footage.

**What helps a before-and-after comparison?**
Use similar framing and light. Keep the feature in the same part of the screen across the cut.
