**Build the video around something the viewer can see change. Dough becoming a shaped item, a cross-section or the first tray of the morning can each support a different edit.**

These ideas are for bakers filming their own material. Pick the one that fits the clips you can make or already have. Keep the useful explanation and give the finished result enough screen time to see it.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. The cross-section with a reason

Film the whole item, the cut and the inside. Hold the cut surface steady enough to see the texture. Let the explanation name one detail instead of putting a long ingredient list over the reveal.

### 2. One tray through the morning

Choose a tray you can follow from preparation to the counter. Use the actual order of your recordings. Keep a finished view at the end so the morning sequence has an outcome.

### 3. The last finishing touch

Show the item before the finishing detail, the detail being added and the finished item in its serving setting. Keep the hand movement readable. Use natural sound if the recording adds something to the action.

## A brief to adapt

> Keep the pastry cut continuous, then hold the cross-section. Put the product name above the food and leave the inside visible. Keep the original sound of the cut.

## FAQ

**Should I include every ingredient?**
Include the information the video promises. A texture reveal can stay focused, while a recipe needs the necessary ingredients and steps.

**How do I make the inside easier to see?**
Choose a source view with clear focus and light. Keep the frame stable long enough for the viewer to inspect it.
