**Instagram reels sit next to your grid and your stories, so the reels that work look good, feel personal, and give people a reason to save or send them. Here are 28 reel ideas in six groups, from aesthetic recaps to carousels turned into reels. Each one has a line to say or put on screen, the format it fits, and a prompt that cuts the 9:16 reel in Vyra.**

Most of my reels are about painting, and plenty of the ones people responded to took almost no setup. An unboxing with my honest take on a brand's products got 17.8K views. One clip of me painting with a single joke on screen got 10K. Neither needed a script. The ideas below work as Instagram content ideas for any niche, with an example from my account wherever I have one. For reels about making art, see the artist content ideas list.

## How to use this list

1. Pick a reel idea that uses footage or photos you already have.
2. Choose the cover frame before you post, since it shows on your grid.
3. Put the main line on screen. Plenty of people watch reels with the sound off.
4. Paste the prompt into Vyra with your clips and adjust the timings to your footage.

## Aesthetic recaps

### 1. A month in 30 seconds
Collect one or two clips from every day of the month and cut them into a soft, even recap. One color grade across every clip makes it look like a single piece.
> "october, in order."

Fits [montage](/formats/montage) and [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll).
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel from this month's clips in date order, one clip per day at about 1 second each. Apply the same warm, slightly faded grade to every clip. Add the month name in small lowercase serif text in the bottom center for the first 3 seconds. Cut on the beat of a soft acoustic track.
```

### 2. One second a day
Film one second every day for a few months and string them together. Small changes in seasons, haircuts, and rooms add up to something people watch twice.
> "100 days, one second each."

Fits [montage](/formats/montage) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Make a 9:16 reel from my daily clips in date order, trimmed to exactly 1 second each. Keep the original sound of each clip low under one continuous instrumental track. Show the date in small white text in the bottom right of every clip. Hold the final day for 2 extra seconds.
```

### 3. The color story
Sort a pile of clips by color instead of by time, from red through to blue. The color shift does the storytelling.
> "everything red I saw this month, then everything blue."

Fits [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll) and [beat-synced edit](/formats/beat-synced-edit).
```
Make a 25 second 9:16 reel. Sort my clips by their main color so the reel moves from red to orange, yellow, green, and blue. Show each clip for one beat and use a quick dissolve wherever the color group changes. Add no text except a single small color name in the corner when each new group starts.
```

### 4. A golden hour walk
Film your street or campus during golden hour with slow, steady shots. People save calm reels to watch again later.
> "the walk home."

Fits [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll) and [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog).
```
Make a 20 second 9:16 reel from my golden hour clips. Slow every clip to 60 percent speed and use soft cross dissolves between them. Keep the warm color and lift the shadows slightly. Show one line of text in the center for 3 seconds, then let the footage play with only the music.
```

### 5. A slow morning with no talking
Film your morning in small details, like coffee, light on the window, and an open sketchbook. A quiet reel gives people a break from louder videos.
> "slow mornings before the studio."

Fits [silent vlog](/formats/silent-vlog) and [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel from my morning clips. Keep the natural sounds, like the kettle and the pages, and mix soft piano under them. Use 2 to 3 second clips with gentle fades. Add short lowercase captions describing each moment in the bottom third.
```

## Carousels turned into reels

### 6. Your best carousel, as a reel
Take a carousel that did well and turn each slide into a beat in a reel. The same images reach people who skip carousels.
> "The full set, in motion."

Fits [montage](/formats/montage) and [beat-synced edit](/formats/beat-synced-edit).
```
Turn these 10 carousel images into a 15 second 9:16 reel. Show each image for one beat with a small zoom in, and put the carousel's first line of text on the first image. Fit the square images into the vertical frame with a blurred copy of each image filling the background.
```

### 7. A photo dump that moves
Mix photos and short video clips from a week or a trip into one fast reel. A photo dump feels casual even when every frame is picked with care.
> "photo dump, in motion."

Fits [montage](/formats/montage) and [travel recap](/formats/travel-recap).
```
Make a 20 second 9:16 reel that alternates between photos and video clips. Give each photo a slight tilt or a slow zoom so the reel never sits still. Cut on every beat of an upbeat track. Add a small location tag in the top left that changes with each place.
```

### 8. The process as stills
Use a photo of each stage of a project as a slide, one per beat. It works even when you forgot to film video.
> "Six stages of one painting."

Fits [process video](/formats/process-video) and [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation).
```
Make a 12 second 9:16 reel from my six progress photos. Show them in order, each for 2 beats, with a quick crossfade so the painting looks like it builds itself. Put a stage number in the corner. End on the finished piece with a slow push in.
```

### 9. A text carousel, typed out
Turn a text-heavy carousel, like a list of tips, into a reel where each line types onto the screen over b-roll.
> "5 things I'd tell a new painter."

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head) and [faceless video](/formats/faceless-video).
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel with my studio b-roll as the background, darkened by 30 percent. Type each tip onto the screen one letter at a time in the center, then hold it for 3 seconds. Number the tips. Use a calm lo-fi track.
```

## Save-worthy tips

### 10. The save-this checklist
Make a reel that's a list people will want later, like a packing list or a supply list. Ask them to save it on screen.
> "Save this for your next art fair."

Fits [listicle talking head](/formats/listicle-talking-head).
```
Make a 20 second 9:16 reel. Build a checklist in the center that adds one item every 2 seconds, with a quick clip of each item behind it. Keep "save this for later" at the top for the whole reel. Hold the full list on screen for the last 4 seconds so people can screenshot it.
```

### 11. A recipe-style how-to
Show the materials first, then the steps, like a recipe card. Viewers can follow along straight from the reel.
> "How to build a giant canvas."

Fits [tutorial](/formats/tutorial).
```
Make a 45 second 9:16 reel. Open with an overhead shot of all the materials and a list of them as text on the left. Then show each step with its number in large text at the top and a 3 to 5 word caption under it. Speed up any step longer than 5 seconds.
```

### 12. What's in my bag
Empty your bag, kit, or desk drawer and name each item. People screenshot the items they want.
> "What I carry to every painting class."

Fits [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review) and [unboxing](/formats/unboxing).
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel from my overhead clip. Each time I place an item, add its name as a small label with a line pointing to it. Cut out the gaps between items. End on the full spread with every label showing for 3 seconds.
```

### 13. Three things before you start
Give three short pieces of advice for someone about to start what you do. Keep each one to a sentence.
> "Three things to know before your first oil painting."

Fits [lessons learned](/formats/lessons-learned) and [talking head](/formats/talking-head).
```
Make a 40 second 9:16 reel. Put me in the top half and a b-roll clip for each point in the bottom half. Add a large "1", "2", "3" between the halves at each new point. Cut every pause and add captions under my face.
```

## Behind the scenes

### 14. The posted version and the real one
Start on the pretty, framed shot you'd post, then pull back to show the mess around it. Instagram is full of perfect corners, so the reveal gets a laugh.
> "What you see vs what's just out of frame."

Fits [relatable video](/formats/relatable-video) and [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation).
```
Make a 12 second 9:16 reel. Hold the styled close-up for 3 seconds with soft music, then cut the music on a record scratch and play the wide shot that shows the whole messy room. Put "what you see" at the top of the first shot and "what's out of frame" at the top of the second.
```

### 15. The tool you can't work without
Make a short love letter to one tool, from a pen to a camera to a mixing bowl. Close-ups of a worn favorite say a lot about how you work.
> "Six years, same pair of scissors."

Fits [art supplies review](/formats/art-supplies-review) and [product demo](/formats/product-demo).
```
Make a 20 second 9:16 reel from my close-ups of the tool. Use slow push ins on the worn spots and two quick clips of it in use. Add one line of text per shot in the bottom third, in a small serif font. End on the tool set down on the table with the sound of it landing turned up.
```

### 16. How you filmed it
Show the setup behind one of your best-looking shots, like a phone balanced on books or a lamp held by a friend. People save these to copy the trick.
> "This shot was a phone, two books, and a desk lamp."

Fits [process video](/formats/process-video) and [tutorial](/formats/tutorial).
```
Make a 20 second 9:16 reel. Open on the finished shot for 2 seconds, then cut to the behind-the-scenes clip of the setup and label each object with a small arrow and its name. End by playing the finished shot again, full screen, for 3 seconds.
```

### 17. A brand sent you something
Open a package from a brand and give your honest opinion while you try the products. Add one thought about working with brands.
> "A brand sent me products, so here's my honest take."

Fits [unboxing](/formats/unboxing) and [talking head](/formats/talking-head). Mine, with some advice about posting, got 17.8K views.
```
Make a 45 second 9:16 reel. Open with the box being opened from above, then cut to me talking with the products in frame. Add the product names as small labels when I hold them up. Captions in the lower third, and cut every pause.
```

### 18. A workday in 15 shots
Film fifteen short moments of one workday and cut them in order. It shows what your days look like.
> "A Tuesday at work, in 15 shots."

Fits [day in the life](/formats/day-in-the-life-story).
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel with fifteen 2 second clips in time order. Add the time of day as text in the bottom left of each clip. Add captions for the two or three moments where I talk. Use a steady, upbeat track and cut on every other beat.
```

## Text on screen

### 19. One line over one long shot
Put a single sentence over a calm clip that lasts the whole reel. The viewer reads it, watches, and often reads it again.
> "I almost stopped posting this year. I'm glad I didn't."

Fits [cinematic b-roll](/formats/cinematic-b-roll).
```
Make a 10 second 9:16 reel from one clip with no cuts. Put the line in the center in a thin serif font, fading in over the first second. Keep the room sound low and add a quiet ambient track.
```

### 20. A caption joke over your work
Film yourself working and put a line on screen that makes it funny. The footage can be plain if the line is good.
> "Painting for you doing literally anything productive."

Fits [relatable video](/formats/relatable-video). That one got 10K views.
```
Make a 12 second 9:16 reel from one clip of me painting. Put the line at the top in white text with a black outline for the whole reel. Speed the clip up to 2x and end it on a natural pause so it loops.
```

### 21. A letter to someone
Write a short note to someone who matters to you and put it on screen over footage connected to them.
> "For my mom."

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video). My reel for my mom, a break from my usual art posts, got 8K views.
```
Make a 40 second 9:16 reel. Show the note one sentence at a time in the center, each for 3 seconds, over old photos with a slow zoom. Use a soft piano track and fade it out on the last sentence.
```

### 22. A big life update
Tell followers about a real change, like a move, a new job, or leaving school, with text and a few clips. Updates bring back people who haven't seen your posts in a while.
> "A new chapter."

Fits [storytelling video](/formats/storytelling-video). My update about leaving Brown to build Vyra got 24.5K views.
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel. Open on "a new chapter" in large text over a still photo. Then show five clips that tell the change in order, each with one short line of text at the bottom. End on a current photo of me with the date.
```

## Collabs and community

### 23. A collab reel from two angles
Film the same event with a friend, each on your own phone, and cut both views together. Post it as a collab so it shows on both accounts.
> "Same day, two points of view."

Fits [vlog](/formats/vlog) and [montage](/formats/montage).
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel that cuts between my footage and my friend's footage of the same moments. Put a small name tag in the top corner to show whose view is on screen. Match the color of both phones so the cuts feel smooth. Use a split screen for the one moment where we film each other.
```

### 24. Let followers vote
Ask followers to vote in your stories, then post a reel showing what won. People who voted come back to see it.
> "You picked the green one. Here it is."

Fits [founder build in public](/formats/founder-build-in-public) and [process video](/formats/process-video).
```
Make a 25 second 9:16 reel. Show the poll screenshot with the results for 3 seconds, then the process of making the winning option at 4x. End on the finished version with the winning vote share as text.
```

### 25. Feature a follower
Share a follower's work or story, with their permission, and tag them. It thanks them and shows new viewers who your audience is.
> "You made this from my tutorial."

Fits [testimonial](/formats/testimonial).
```
Make a 20 second 9:16 reel. Show my tutorial clip for 3 seconds, then their result full screen with their handle as a label in the bottom left. End with my version and theirs side by side in a split screen.
```

### 26. Your stories, saved as a reel
Collect the best stories you posted this week and cut them into one reel, since stories disappear after a day.
> "In case you missed my stories this week."

Fits [montage](/formats/montage) and [vlog](/formats/vlog).
```
Make a 30 second 9:16 reel from my saved story clips. Trim the first and last half second of each clip and remove any repeats. Keep the text I wrote on each story and cut on the beat of a light track. Add the day of the week in small text in the top corner.
```

### 27. A launch reel
Announce a product, print, or service with the thing itself on screen in the first second.
> "It's finally here."

Fits [product demo](/formats/product-demo).
```
Make a 20 second 9:16 reel. Open on the product in someone's hands for 2 seconds, then show three close-ups of the details on the beat. Add the launch date in large text in the center for the last 3 seconds, with "link in bio" under it.
```

### 28. The grid-ready reveal
Plan a reel so its cover frame looks good on your grid, then reveal the work inside. A strong cover brings people in from your profile.
> "'skating', finished."

Fits [before and after](/formats/before-after-transformation) and [art timelapse](/formats/art-timelapse).
```
Make a 15 second 9:16 reel. Pick a cover frame of the finished piece that still looks good when the grid crops the center. Start on the blank canvas, play the timelapse to the beat, and end on the exact frame chosen for the cover.
```

## FAQ

**How long should an Instagram reel be?**
Long enough for the idea. Recaps work at 15 to 30 seconds, and tutorials can run 45 to 90.

**What are easy reel ideas if I don't want to show my face?**
A golden hour walk, a color story, a carousel turned into a reel, and a love letter to your favorite tool all work without your face.

**Should I post reels or carousels?**
Both. Turn your best carousels into reels so the same work reaches people who mostly watch reels.

**What's a good Instagram content idea when I have nothing new?**
Recap what's already on your phone. A month in 30 seconds, a color story, or a reel made from this week's stories uses footage you already have.
