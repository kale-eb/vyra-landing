**Jewelry footage needs both a close view and a sense of scale. Give the viewer time to see the piece before asking them to read a caption.**

These ideas are for jewelry makers filming their own material. Pick the one that fits the clips you can make or already have. Keep the useful explanation and give the finished result enough screen time to see it.

## Three ideas to film

### 1. Small detail, full piece

Open close on a setting or clasp, then pull back or cut to the full piece. Keep the detail in focus in the source recording. Use one label naming what you want people to notice.

### 2. The piece in the hand

Show the jewelry beside a hand or another familiar size reference, then show it being worn if you have that footage. Match the lighting so the color stays recognizable between shots.

### 3. A pair with a tiny difference

Place two variations beside each other and hold the comparison. Point out the difference in the voiceover or with a short text label. Do not cover the feature with the label itself.

## FAQ

**How do I make a tiny detail readable?**
Use a source close-up with clear focus and give it enough screen time. Enlarging a soft shot can make the softness more obvious.

**Should I use music over the explanation?**
Check that the spoken detail stays understandable. Preview the sequence with the music low before choosing a level.
