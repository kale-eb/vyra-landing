## Your three most-used prompts

**Lesson cleanup**
```
Clean up this 48-minute lesson. Remove pauses over one second, filler words, and the two minutes at the start where I'm sharing my screen. Keep everything else in order. Title card with the lesson name for the first 3 seconds. No captions.
```

**Social clip**
```
Find the moment where I explain why people undercharge. Cut a 45-second 9:16 clip around it, starting on the strongest sentence. 3-5 word captions, bold white, lower third. End on my name and handle for 2 seconds.
```

**Course promo**
```
Build a 60-second promo from the lesson footage and the student call recordings. Open on a student saying what changed for them, then three short clips of me teaching, then a title card that says "Enrollment opens Monday." Calm music under everything, ducked when anyone speaks.
```

## A typical workflow

1. Upload the recording. Vyra transcribes it and reads what's on screen, slides included.
2. Ask for the cleaned full version first.
3. Ask for clips by topic. "Every place I mention accountability."
4. Lock a caption and title style, then say "same style" for the rest.
5. Export the lesson at full resolution and the clips at 9:16.

## What Vyra does that matters for you

- Transcript search across long recordings. No timestamps needed.
- Screen recording reframing. Crop to the region you're pointing at when going vertical.
- One caption and title style across a whole module.
- Bring your own Claude or ChatGPT over MCP if you already write course material with it.

## What it does not do

- It doesn't host or sell the course. Export and upload to your platform.
- It doesn't generate a presenter. Your face and voice stay yours.

## FAQ

**Can it handle a two-hour recording?**
Yes. Work in sections if the timeline gets heavy, and ask for clips by topic.

**Will my slides stay readable at 9:16?**
Say which region to crop to, or ask for a split layout with your face on top and the slide below.

**Can I reuse one style across a whole course?**
Yes. Pick a template and style for the project, or attach a finished lesson and say "match this."
