**In Vyra on iPhone, select the clip and use Adjust to reduce Saturation, or describe the color change in chat. Compare the result with your source before applying it to other shots.**

The painted red may look much louder than it did in the room. Start with the shot that shows the work clearly. Check the paper, skin and nearby colors too, so fixing one distracting area does not flatten the whole picture.

The [official iPhone listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) documents editing supplied media. Vyra’s current in-app help, Color and the Adjust panel, documents the controls described here.

## Work through the edit

1. Choose a representative shot with the colors you need to preserve.
2. Select the clip and open Adjust, or ask chat for a modest saturation reduction.
3. Reduce the color intensity gradually while comparing with the source.
4. Check neutral surfaces and other important colors in the same frame.
5. Review neighboring shots and the saved export before copying the treatment.

## A brief to try

> Reduce the saturation a little in this shot. Keep the paper neutral and the red paint recognizably red. Do not change the exposure or the other clips yet.

## Review the result

The Color and the Adjust panel help documents Saturation and separate HSL controls. A whole-picture change can affect every color. If just one range is wrong, review the selected-color guide instead of reducing everything. A screen comparison is not a calibrated reproduction test.

## FAQ

**Is saturation the same as brightness?**
Saturation changes color intensity. Review exposure separately when the picture is too light or dark.

**Should I copy the same change to every shot?**
Check shots filmed under different lighting individually. A useful change on the close-up may make another shot look dull.
