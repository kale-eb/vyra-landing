**Vlogging properly means shooting for the edit. Instead of one long take, I grab short pieces. A line to camera when something happens, a wide of where I am, a close detail of what I'm doing, and a moving shot to the next place. Audio close to my mouth. Film 3 to 5 times more than I need, then cut it down to one throughline. A good 60 second vlog comes from 15 to 25 minutes of footage.**

## Why it matters

Raw vlog footage is the hardest thing to edit because nothing was planned.

## The method

1. **Decide the throughline before you leave.** One sentence. "I'm trying to finish this commission before the gallery pickup." Every clip serves that or gets cut.
2. **Shoot the checklist at each location.** Wide, medium of you doing the thing, close detail, one moving shot in or out.
3. **Talk to camera in short bursts.** 5 to 15 seconds, one thought each. Narrate the turns, not everything.
4. **Capture transitions on purpose.** A door, a threshold, putting the phone down, a whip pan.
5. **Audio close.** Arm's length or closer for talking. A clip-on mic in wind or crowds.
6. **Film the ending while it happens.** The finished thing, the reaction. You can't fake it later.
7. **Edit to the throughline.** Open mid-action, hit 3 to 5 turns, end on the payoff.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Talking clip | 5 to 15 s |
| Clips per location | 4 minimum |
| Shooting ratio | 15 to 25 min per 60 s |
| Locations in 60 s | 3 to 5 |
| Music | one bed, 6 to 10 dB under speech |
| Shot length in edit | 2 to 5 s, longer for the payoff |
| Orientation | vertical, decided before the day starts |

## Common mistakes

- One 20 minute take. Nothing to cut to.
- Narrating everything.
- No wide shot, so nobody knows where you are.
- Ending with "anyway, that's it, bye."

## FAQ

**How long should a vlog be?**
Short-form, 45 to 90 seconds. YouTube, 8 to 15 minutes if enough actually happened.

**Do I have to talk to camera?**
No. A silent vlog with captions and music works, especially for travel and process.
