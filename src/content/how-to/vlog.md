**Proper vlogging means shooting for the edit. Instead of one continuous take, capture short pieces of coverage: a talking-to-camera line when something happens, a wide shot of where you are, a close detail of what you are doing, and a moving shot that carries you to the next place. Record audio close to your mouth, film 3 to 5 times more than you need, and edit it down to one throughline. A good 60 second vlog usually comes from 15 to 25 minutes of footage.**

## Why it matters

Raw vlog footage is the hardest kind to edit because nothing was planned. Shooting with the edit in mind turns a pile of clips into a story with a beginning, middle, and end, and makes the edit take minutes instead of hours.

## The method

1. **Decide the throughline before you leave.** One sentence: "I am trying to finish this commission before the gallery pickup." Every clip either serves that line or gets cut.
2. **Shoot the coverage checklist at each location.** Wide establishing shot, medium of you doing the thing, close detail of hands or object, and one moving shot in or out.
3. **Talk to camera in short bursts.** 5 to 15 seconds each, one thought per clip. Say it once, cleanly. Do not narrate everything; narrate the turns.
4. **Capture transitions on purpose.** Door opening, walking through a threshold, putting the phone down, a whip pan. These are your location changes.
5. **Get audio close.** Hold the phone at arm's length or closer for talking moments. Use a clip-on mic in wind or crowds.
6. **Film the ending while it is happening.** The finished thing, the reaction, the reveal. Do not try to fake it later.
7. **Edit to the throughline.** Open mid-action, hit 3 to 5 turns, end on the payoff. Everything else goes.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Talking-to-camera clip length | 5 to 15 s |
| Clips per location | 4 minimum (wide, medium, detail, moving) |
| Shooting ratio | 15 to 25 min raw per 60 s finished |
| Locations in a 60 s vlog | 3 to 5 |
| Music | one bed, ducked 6 to 10 dB under speech |
| Shot length in edit | 2 to 5 s, longer only for the payoff |
| Phone orientation | vertical for short-form, decide before the day starts |

Talking in public: hold the phone at chest height, look at the lens not the screen, speak at normal volume, and keep it under 10 seconds. Confidence comes from brevity.

## Examples

Studio day vlog, 60 seconds:
- 0 to 3 s: hands mixing paint, spoken "gallery pickup is at 5 and this is not dry"
- 3 to 12 s: wide of studio, medium of painting, talking clip "here is the plan"
- 12 to 30 s: three progress moments, each with a detail shot and one line
- 30 to 40 s: the problem (a smudge), close detail, talking clip
- 40 to 52 s: the fix, moving shot carrying the canvas out
- 52 to 60 s: pickup, reaction, last line, cut on the last word

Travel day with friends: title card, arrival wide, 8 to 12 short moments with speech-driven captions, one Polaroid-style photo montage as the climax, single music bed under it all.

## Common mistakes

- One 20 minute take with no coverage. There is nothing to cut to.
- Narrating everything. Save speech for the turns, let footage carry the rest.
- Forgetting the wide shot. The viewer never knows where they are.
- Shooting horizontal and cropping vertical later. Frame it right on the day.
- Ending with "anyway, that's it, bye." End on the payoff.

## FAQ

**How long should a vlog be?**
Short-form: 45 to 90 seconds. YouTube: 8 to 15 minutes if you have enough real turns in the story. Length follows the number of things that actually happened.

**Do I need to talk to camera at all?**
No. A silent vlog with captions and music works, especially for travel and process content. Talking adds personality; it is not required.

**How do I film myself without feeling awkward?**
Keep clips under 10 seconds, plan the one sentence before you press record, and accept that the first three days feel strange. It passes.
