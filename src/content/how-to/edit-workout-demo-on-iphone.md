**To edit a workout demonstration on iPhone, keep a complete visible movement and label it with the instructions you intend to teach. In Vyra, select clear repetitions from your recording and avoid cuts that hide the start or finish.**

This is an editing workflow, not exercise instruction. Use movement guidance you are qualified to give. A short clip can show an exercise poorly if it crops the feet, hides the equipment or cuts away during the part you are explaining.

## Work through the edit

1. Choose a recording that shows the full movement and relevant equipment.
2. Select a complete repetition at normal speed.
3. Remove setup and unrelated waiting without cutting through the demonstration.
4. Add your verified movement name and cues as short labels.
5. Check the exported framing shows the movement throughout and the labels do not obscure it.

The [official Vyra App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) confirms chat editing, timeline changes, text, audio and export to Photos. Use the brief below as a starting point and check the result before posting.

## Make the important choices

| What you find | Editing choice |
| --- | --- |
| One full repetition explains the movement | Preserve its beginning and end |
| A close crop hides placement | Use the wider shot |
| A cue changes how the movement is performed | Use only instructions you have checked |

## A brief to try

```
Keep one complete demonstration from this clip at its original speed. Remove the setup before it starts. Add only the exercise name and cues I provide, and keep my feet and equipment visible.
```

Watch the final clip from the perspective of someone seeing the movement for the first time. Do not let a quick edit imply a range or sequence that the original take did not show. Keep health or performance claims out of automatic text requests.

## FAQ

**Can I speed up the demonstration to fit more repetitions?**
A teaching clip should make the intended movement clear. Keep normal speed when changing it would misrepresent the demonstration.

**Where should I put the exercise name?**
Place it away from the body and equipment you are demonstrating. Check the whole repetition because a label that is clear at the start may cover the movement later.

**Does every repetition need to stay?**
Keep enough complete movement to explain the point. Remove redundant repeats only after checking what they contribute.
