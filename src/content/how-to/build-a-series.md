**A series is one repeatable premise with a numbered episode, a fixed opening, and a reason to watch the next one. It grows faster than one-off videos because every episode advertises the rest: a viewer who likes episode 7 goes looking for 1 through 6, and the platform learns that your videos get binge-watched. It also removes the hardest part of posting, which is deciding what to make.**

## Why it matters

One-off videos compete alone. Series episodes compete as a group. A series also gives new followers a reason to stay: they are not following a person yet, they are following the next episode.

## The method

1. **Pick a premise that regenerates.** "Painting every piece from my semester" ends. "Day N of building a startup" does not. The premise should produce a new episode from ordinary life.
2. **Fix the opening.** Same first line, same visual, same text card with the episode number. Viewers should recognize episode 12 in half a second.
3. **Number it on screen.** "Day 34" in the corner. Numbers imply a back catalog and a future.
4. **End with the next episode's hook.** "Tomorrow I find out if it dried" is a reason to follow.
5. **Keep episodes the same length and format.** A series is a promise about what the viewer gets. Do not surprise them with a 3-minute episode 9.
6. **Batch-film three at once.** A series dies when episode 5 is late. See the batch-filming guide.
7. **Recap at milestones.** Episode 10, 25, 50: a montage of the run so far. Recaps bring in new viewers and reward old ones.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Episodes before judging | 10 |
| Episode length variance | under 20% |
| Fixed opening | first 2 s identical |
| Episode number on screen | first 3 s |
| Next-episode hook | last 2 s |
| Recap episode | every 10 to 25 |

## Examples

"Day one of this next chapter with Y Combinator" is an episode one. The premise regenerates every day the company exists.

"One painting a week until I graduate" has an end date, which is fine; a finite series with a countdown is its own hook.

"Rating art supplies from my drawer" regenerates until the drawer is empty, then becomes "rating supplies you sent me."

## Common mistakes

- A premise that needs a special event to produce an episode.
- Changing the opening every time so nothing is recognizable.
- Episode 1 being twice as long as episode 2.
- No number on screen, so viewers cannot tell it is a series.
- Quitting at episode 6 because episodes 1 through 5 were slow. Series compound late.

## FAQ

**How many episodes before it works?**
Typically 10 to 15 before the back catalog effect shows up in analytics.

**Can I run two series at once?**
Once the first one is established. Two new series at once splits your consistency.

**What if I miss an episode?**
Post the next one with the correct number and say nothing. Nobody minds a gap; they mind the series ending.
