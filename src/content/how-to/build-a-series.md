**A series is one premise, a numbered episode, the same opening every time, and a reason to watch the next one. Someone who likes episode 7 goes looking for 1 through 6. It also removes the hardest part of posting, which is deciding what to make.**

## Why it matters

One-off videos compete alone.

## The method

1. **Pick a premise that keeps going.** "Every piece from my semester" ends. "Day N of building a startup" doesn't.
2. **Fix the opening.** Same first line, same visual, same episode number card. Episode 12 should be recognizable in half a second.
3. **Put the number on screen.** "Day 34" in the corner.
4. **End with the next episode's hook.** "Tomorrow I find out if it dried."
5. **Keep episodes the same length.** A series is a promise about what the viewer gets.
6. **Batch film three at a time.** A series dies when episode 5 is late.
7. **Recap at milestones.** Episode 10, 25, 50. A montage of the run so far.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Episodes before judging | 10 |
| Length variance | under 20% |
| Fixed opening | first 2 s identical |
| Number on screen | first 3 s |
| Next-episode hook | last 2 s |
| Recap | every 10 to 25 |

## Examples

- "Day one of this next chapter with Y Combinator" is an episode one. The premise regenerates every day the company exists.
- "One painting a week until I graduate" has an end date. That's fine, a countdown is its own hook.

## Common mistakes

- A premise that needs a special event to make an episode.
- A different opening every time.
- Episode 1 twice as long as episode 2.
- Quitting at episode 6. Series pay off late.

## FAQ

**How many episodes before it works?**
Usually 10 to 15 before the back catalog shows up in analytics.

**Missed an episode?**
Post the next one with the right number and say nothing. Nobody minds a gap.
