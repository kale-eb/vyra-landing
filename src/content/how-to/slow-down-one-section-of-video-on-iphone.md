**In Vyra on iPhone, identify the exact action and ask chat to slow only that section. Keep the surrounding clips at their original pace and check the sound at both boundaries.**

The useful moment may be the brush touching the page, rather than the whole ten-second shot. Give that action enough time to see. Slowing a low-frame-rate recording too far can make motion look uneven.

The [official iPhone listing and release notes](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) document native editing. Vyra’s current in-app help section Audio features supports the controls or chat workflow below.

## Work through the edit

1. Play the clip and identify the beginning and end of the action.
2. Describe that action or give its timestamps in chat.
3. Ask Vyra to slow that portion while preserving the rest of the sequence.
4. Check the changed duration and any music or captions affected by it.
5. Play the export and review motion and audio at the entry and exit.

## A brief to try

> Slow only the moment when the brush meets the page. Keep the preparation and the shot after it at normal speed. Preserve the voiceover and review the caption timing after the change.

## Check the result

The official help documents exact speed instructions through the assistant. Changing playback speed does not add detail that the camera never recorded. Choose a gentler slowdown if the result looks choppy, and compare the saved file rather than judging one paused frame.

New iPhone accounts receive 100 free credits. Free iPhone exports are 720p with a watermark. Check the App Store’s Plus and Pro options for paid limits.

## FAQ

**Will slowing a section change the total duration?**
Yes. A slowed section occupies more time unless another part of the edit is shortened. Review the ending and any timed music cues.

**Should I slow the spoken explanation too?**
Specify that separately. If the explanation needs to sound natural, keep its voiceover at the original pace while changing the picture.
