**To shorten caption lines in Vyra on iPhone, keep the spoken wording, review long words and adjust the text layout so each phrase fits without covering the video.**

A caption can be spelled correctly and still be hard to read. One long product name may push everything onto another line. Start with the narrowest frame you plan to publish, then make the layout work there.

The App Store version history documents long-word caption wrapping and shared caption styling. See the [official Vyra App Store listing and release notes](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012).

## Work through the edit

1. Generate captions for the final spoken sequence.
2. Find lines that wrap awkwardly or reach the edge of the frame.
3. Keep names and meaningful phrases together when reviewing the text.
4. Adjust the caption size or placement and inspect long words at the new setting.
5. Read the saved video at normal phone size without pausing it.

## Make the important choices

| Situation | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| One unusually long word | Check wrapping before shrinking every caption |
| Several lines fill the screen | Use shorter visible phrases where the editing controls allow |
| A name splits awkwardly | Keep its spelling intact and adjust the layout |

## A brief to try

```
Keep the full spoken wording in my captions. Make the long product names fit inside the frame. Use a readable size, and show me any lines that still wrap badly before changing their text.
```

## Check the result

Do not solve a layout problem by quietly deleting an important qualifier. A sentence such as “works with some phones” changes meaning if the small word disappears. Review long lines alongside the recording, especially where a caption crosses a cut.

## FAQ

**Should I shorten what the speaker actually said?**
Keep a faithful transcript. Change the layout or phrase grouping before removing meaningful words.

**Does the app handle long words?**
The native release notes describe wrapping long caption words instead of cutting them off. Check the exported result.

**Will a smaller font always help?**
It can fit more text while making the words harder to read. Test the video at its intended viewing size.
