**Four numbers explain almost every short-form result. Hook rate tells you whether the first 3 seconds worked. The retention curve tells you where people left. Average watch time tells you whether the platform will keep pushing it. Shares versus saves tells you what kind of value the video delivered. Each one points at a different edit, so reading them in order tells you exactly what to change on the next video.**

## Why it matters

Likes and follower counts are outcomes. The four numbers below are causes. A creator who reads causes can fix a video in one re-cut; a creator who reads outcomes just makes another video and hopes.

## The method

1. **Start with hook rate.** On TikTok and Reels this is the share of viewers still watching at 3 seconds. On Shorts it is "viewed vs swiped away." If this is low, nothing else matters yet. Fix the first 3 seconds.
2. **Read the retention curve next.** Find the steepest drop. That timestamp is a pause, a tangent, a slow section, or a moment you said "so anyway." Cut it.
3. **Check average watch time as a percentage of length.** If people watch 60% of a 30-second video, that is 18 seconds. Above 50% typically means the platform keeps distributing. Below 35% typically means it stops.
4. **Compare shares to saves.** Shares mean the video was relatable or surprising. Saves mean it was useful or beautiful. Whichever is higher tells you what your audience wants more of.
5. **Look at followers versus non-followers.** A video that only followers watched to the end did not get tested by strangers yet. Judge it on non-follower numbers.
6. **Decide one change.** Hook, cut, length, or value type. One change per video, or you cannot tell what worked.

## Rules of thumb

| Number | Typically weak | Typically working |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook rate (watching at 3 s) | below 50% | above 65% |
| Viewed vs swiped (Shorts) | below 60% | above 70% |
| Average watch time | below 35% of length | above 50% of length |
| Completion rate (under 30 s) | below 20% | above 35% |
| Shares per 1,000 views | below 2 | above 5 |
| Saves per 1,000 views | below 3 | above 10 |

These are general benchmarks, not platform rules. Compare your own videos against each other first.

## Examples

A vlog with hook rate 72% and a retention cliff at 0:14: the opening works, the check-in at 14 seconds is too long. Cut it in half, repost as a variant.

A tutorial with average watch time 48%, shares 1 per 1,000, saves 14 per 1,000: people are keeping it, not sending it. Make more tutorials and add a "save this" line.

A storytelling Reel with hook rate 45%: the first 3 seconds are the problem. Do not touch the rest. Re-cut the opening three ways and test.

## Common mistakes

- Reading likes. Likes lag everything else and come mostly from people who already follow you.
- Changing three things at once after a weak video.
- Judging a video in the first hour. Wait 48 hours on TikTok and Shorts, 3 to 5 days on Reels.
- Ignoring the non-follower split.
- Comparing your numbers to a creator with a different format.

## FAQ

**Where do I find these numbers?**
TikTok: Analytics, then the video, then "retention." Instagram: Insights on the Reel, "watch time" and "sends." YouTube Studio: the Short's analytics tab, "viewed vs swiped away."

**How many views before the numbers mean anything?**
Typically 500 to 1,000. Below that, one viewer skews everything.

**What if hook rate is high and watch time is still low?**
The promise in the hook was not paid off. Deliver the payoff earlier or cut the middle.
