**In Vyra on iPhone, open Elements to choose a text bubble, or ask chat to add one with your wording and timing. Position it beside the subject and review where its pointer appears.**

A bubble can show a short thought in a skit, but it becomes distracting when it follows the wrong person or covers the action. Use a few words and keep its meaning clear when the video is watched muted.

The [official Vyra iPhone listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) documents editing your own footage. Vyra’s current in-app help, Elements and Editing tools on iPhone, supports the controls or chat workflow below.

## Work through the edit

1. Choose the moment and write the short phrase for the bubble.
2. Open Elements in the editor’s tool row, or describe the bubble in chat.
3. Set the wording and ask for the intended position and interval.
4. Play the full section and check the pointer against the subject.
5. Check that the bubble leaves the face, hands and important captions visible.

## A brief to try

> Add a small thought bubble saying wrong brush beside me during the reaction shot. Keep it off my face and remove it before the painting close-up.

## Check the result

Vyra’s Elements help documents text bubbles and the native Elements tool. The prompt sets the desired composition without assuming an undocumented automatic tracking switch. If the subject moves, review the bubble throughout the shot.

## FAQ

**Should I put every spoken sentence inside a bubble?**
A bubble works best for a short reaction or separate thought. Use a caption track when the goal is to transcribe the full spoken explanation.

**Will the pointer automatically follow a moving person?**
Do not assume that behavior. Preview the entire interval and ask for a positional correction when it points at the wrong subject.
