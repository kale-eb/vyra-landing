**In Vyra on iPhone, select the clip and ask chat to reduce its highlights while keeping the subject’s overall brightness usable. Check the brightest area for recorded detail before making a stronger adjustment.**

A window behind your desk may look bright while the painting still needs light. Lowering the whole image can make the painting too dark. Work on the bright range and compare the result across the shot.

The [official Vyra iPhone listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) documents editing your own footage. Vyra’s current in-app help, Color and the Adjust panel, supports the controls or chat workflow below.

## Work through the edit

1. Locate the brightest area and check whether it contains visible detail.
2. Select the clip and open Adjust or describe it in chat.
3. Ask for a modest highlight reduction while preserving the subject.
4. Compare the original and adjusted clip at more than one point.
5. Check the exported file for flat bright areas and an overly dark subject.

## A brief to try

> Reduce the bright window highlights in this shot. Keep the drawing readable. If the window has no recorded detail, do not darken the whole image trying to invent it.

## Check the result

Vyra’s Adjust help documents Highlights separately from Exposure and Shadows. Start with a small change. A completely clipped patch may stay featureless even after it becomes gray, so judge the rest of the frame too.

## FAQ

**Can lowering highlights restore a completely white window?**
Only detail present in the recording can be adjusted. A blank clipped area may remain blank after its brightness changes.

**Should I lower exposure at the same time?**
Compare the subject first. Exposure changes the overall picture, while a highlight adjustment targets the bright range. Make one change at a time so you can judge its effect.
