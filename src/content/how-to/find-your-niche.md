**A niche is the overlap of three things: what you can make every week without burning out, what a specific group of people wants to watch, and what you can say that they cannot get from someone else. You do not find it by thinking. You find it by posting ten videos, looking at which ones strangers watched to the end, and doing more of that.**

## Why it matters

A niche is what makes a stranger's first video from you predictive of the next one. Platforms show your content to people who watched similar content; if your videos are all different, there is no "similar." A niche also makes you easier to describe, which is how people recommend you.

## The method

1. **List what you already make.** Not what you wish you made. The things you have actually filmed in the last month.
2. **List who asks you questions.** In comments, DMs, and real life. Those people are the audience you already have.
3. **Post ten videos across two or three topics.** Same format, different subject. Ten is enough to see a pattern.
4. **Sort by average watch time from non-followers.** Not by likes. Watch time from strangers tells you what the platform will push.
5. **Pick the top topic and narrow it by one word.** "Painting" becomes "oil painting process." "Vlogs" becomes "art school vlogs."
6. **Commit for 60 days.** Narrow feels limiting for the first two weeks and then becomes the reason people follow.
7. **Widen only from strength.** Once one topic works, add an adjacent one. Sulan's account went from painting process to studio vlogs to building a startup, each added after the last one held.

## Rules of thumb

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Test videos before deciding | 10 |
| Topics to test | 2 to 3 |
| Commitment after choosing | 60 days |
| The niche in words | 3 to 5 |
| Signal to watch | average watch time, non-followers |
| When to widen | after one topic holds for 2 months |

## Examples

Too wide: "art content." Nobody searches for that and nothing is "similar" to it.

Right size: "oil painting process videos with real flowers." Specific enough to be recommended, wide enough for a video a week.

Too narrow: "painting magnolias in September." Three videos and it is done.

The test: if a stranger watched one video, could they guess the next three? If yes, that is a niche.

## Common mistakes

- Choosing a niche from a list of "profitable niches" instead of from what you already make.
- Reading likes from friends as a signal. Friends like everything.
- Switching topics every week because week two felt slow.
- Picking something you cannot film weekly.
- Never widening, so the account plateaus at one subject.

## FAQ

**What if I have two things I love?**
Test both for ten videos. Lead with the one strangers watch longer. Bring the other in later as an adjacent topic.

**Can my niche be "me"?**
Only after people know who you are. Early on, the subject carries the account; later, you do.

**How do I know it is working?**
When new followers arrive from one video and stay for the next three. Check follower retention in analytics after 30 days.
