**In Vyra on iPhone, select the clip, open Adjust and use the WB controls to correct its color cast. Compare an area that should look neutral while keeping skin and important colors believable.**

A painting filmed under a warm desk lamp can look more orange than it does outside. Use a neutral surface in that same shot as a starting point. A wall painted cream should still look cream after the correction.

Vyra’s in-app help describes this workflow under “Editing tools on iPhone”. The [official App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) documents native editing and export.

## Work through the edit

1. Find a frame with a neutral surface and the subject in the same light.
2. Open Adjust and then WB for the selected clip.
3. Change the balance gradually or ask chat to reduce the unwanted color cast.
4. Compare the artwork or skin with the original recording and the surrounding shots.
5. Watch through changes in lighting before applying the correction to the whole clip.

## A brief to try

> Reduce the orange cast in the desk-lamp shot. Keep the cream paper warm and the blue paint recognizable. Match the next studio shot as closely as the footage allows.

## Check the result

Mixed light can put a warm cast on one side of a face and a cool cast on the other. One global correction may not solve both. Choose the balance that represents the subject fairly and check another frame.

## FAQ

**Can I use the same correction on every recording?**
Recordings made under the same light may share a correction. Check clips from windows, lamps and outdoors separately.

**Does white balance change the exposure?**
White balance changes the color treatment. Review brightness separately if the image is also too dark.
