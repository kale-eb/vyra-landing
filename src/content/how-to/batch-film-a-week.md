**Batch filming is shooting five to seven videos in one two or three hour block. Same setup, same outfit, then the edits run across the week. The slow part of a video is the setup and the deciding, and batching pays that once.**

## Why it matters

Posting daily grows accounts.

## The method

1. **Write seven hooks before you set up.** One line each. If I can't write the hook, the video isn't ready.
2. **Set up once.** Light, tripod, framing, audio. Check a 5 second test clip and don't touch it again.
3. **Film in order of energy.** Hardest talking video first. Silent process footage last.
4. **Two takes each.** Take one is the rehearsal, take two is the video. The third is usually worse.
5. **Change one thing between videos.** A different shirt or angle so the week doesn't look like one day.
6. **Upload everything to one project.** Vyra indexes it once. Each day's edit is then a prompt.
7. **Edit one, queue six.** Cut the first the same day while it's fresh.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Session | 2 to 3 hours |
| Videos per session | 5 to 7 |
| Takes per video | 2 |
| Hooks written first | 7 |
| Outfit or angle changes | 2 to 3 |
| Edit time per video | 5 to 15 minutes |

## Examples

- Sunday at 2pm. Seven hooks on a sticky note. Four talking heads in a row at 30 minutes each, then two process videos at the easel, then a sketchbook flip. Done by 5. Monday to Sunday is one prompt a day.
- Three supply reviews in one sitting with the same overhead rig. Three matching posts for the setup cost of one.


## Common mistakes

- Filming without hooks written, so half the session is thinking.
- Re-lighting between videos.
- Ten takes of one video.
- Editing all seven on Sunday night and burning out.

## FAQ

**How far ahead should I batch?**
One week. Two if you're traveling. Past that it goes stale.

**Can vlogs be batched?**
The talking parts can. Film seven check-ins on Sunday and pair each with that day's real footage.
