**Batch filming means shooting five to seven videos in one two-to-three hour block: one format, one setup, one outfit, then letting the edits run across the week. It is the only reliable way most creators post daily, because the expensive part of a video is not the filming, it is the setup and the deciding. Batching pays that cost once.**

## Why it matters

Daily posting is the growth lever on every short-form platform. Daily filming is not sustainable for anyone with a job, a class, or a studio practice. Batching separates the two: film on Sunday, post all week.

## The method

1. **Write seven hooks before you set up.** One line each. If you cannot write the hook, the video is not ready to film.
2. **Set up once.** Light, tripod, framing, audio. Check a 5-second test clip. Do not touch it again.
3. **Film in order of energy.** Hardest talking video first while you are fresh. Silent process footage last.
4. **Two takes each, no more.** Take one is the rehearsal. Take two is the video. A third take is usually worse.
5. **Change one thing between videos.** A different shirt, a different angle, or a different prop, so the week does not look like one session.
6. **Upload everything to one project.** Vyra indexes all of it once. Then each day's edit is a prompt, not a session.
7. **Edit one, schedule six.** Cut the first video the same day while the batch is fresh. Queue the rest.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Session length | 2 to 3 hours |
| Videos per session | 5 to 7 |
| Takes per video | 2 |
| Hooks written before filming | 7 |
| Outfit or angle changes | 2 to 3 across the batch |
| Editing time per video after upload | 5 to 15 minutes |

## Examples

Sunday, 2 pm: seven hooks on a sticky note. Tripod and window light. Four talking heads in a row (30 minutes each, two takes), then two process videos on the easel, then a sketchbook flip-through. Done by 5. Monday through Sunday: one prompt per day.

A painter who batches three "supply review" videos in one sitting with the same overhead rig gets three consistent-looking posts for the setup cost of one.

## Common mistakes

- Filming without hooks written, so half the session is thinking.
- Re-lighting between videos. Set it once.
- Ten takes of one video. Two, then move.
- Same shirt and angle for all seven, so the week looks like one day.
- Editing all seven on Sunday night and burning out on the part that should be easy.

## FAQ

**How far ahead should I batch?**
One week. Two if you travel. Further than that and the content goes stale.

**What about vlogs? They cannot be batched.**
The talking parts can. Film seven check-ins on Sunday and pair each with that day's real footage.

**Does the audience notice batching?**
Only if every video has the same shirt and light. Change one thing between videos.
