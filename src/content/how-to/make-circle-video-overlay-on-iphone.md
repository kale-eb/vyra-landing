**In Vyra on iPhone, use an ellipse mask to show a round area of a video, then position that masked clip over the main picture. Check the face throughout the shot.**

A circular face overlay can leave more of a demonstration visible, but a tight circle may cut off your chin when you lean forward. Set the crop while looking at the frame where you move the most.

Vyra’s in-app help describes this workflow under “Masks”. The [official App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) documents native editing and export.

## Work through the edit

1. Place the face recording over the demonstration in the timeline.
2. Add a mask and choose Ellipse from the available mask shapes.
3. Adjust the visible area to keep the face inside it.
4. Position the overlay away from important controls and captions.
5. Play the full section and check every movement against the mask edge.

## A brief to try

> Show my face in a small circular overlay at the upper left of the screen demonstration. Leave room above my head and keep the menu on the right visible.

## Check the result

If the mask clips your face, expand the visible area or choose a different recording. Feather changes the softness of an edge. A very soft edge can make the overlay hard to distinguish from a busy background.

## FAQ

**Will the circle automatically follow my face?**
This guide uses a fixed mask. Check the full recording for movement beyond the visible area.

**Can I change the mask edge?**
Vyra help lists Feather and Mask opacity among native mask controls. Adjust them while checking the overlay against the real background.
