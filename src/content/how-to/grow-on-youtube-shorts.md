**YouTube shows you one number that decides everything, viewed versus swiped away. So the job is a first frame that stops the swipe and a video that pays off before 30 seconds. The difference from TikTok is what comes after. A Short can send people to a long video and a subscribe button, which is where YouTube growth actually compounds.**

## Why it matters

Shorts are the cheapest way onto YouTube, and YouTube subscribers actually come back.

## The method

1. **Design the first frame.** Some placements show a still before playback. A face, a piece, or bold text.
2. **Pay off by 30 seconds.** The viewed-versus-swiped number drops fast past 30 for new channels.
3. **Loop when it fits.** Ending where you began gets rewatches, and those count.
4. **Title like a search.** "Speed paint" is weak. "Oil painting a magnolia in 20 minutes" is a query.
5. **Point to a long video.** That's where the subscribe happens.
6. **3 to 5 a week.**
7. **Watch the swipe number.** Above 70 percent in YouTube Studio usually means the hook works.

## Rules of thumb

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Shorts per week | 3 to 5 |
| Length | 15 to 35 s |
| Viewed vs swiped | typically above 70% |
| Average view duration | typically above 80% of length |
| Title | under 60 characters, searchable |
| Long-form alongside | 1 every 1 to 2 weeks |

## Examples

- A 25 second process Short titled "Painting with real magnolia petals," first frame a petal pressed into paint, typically holds above 70 percent. Same clip titled "new piece" with a blank canvas first frame doesn't.
- A loop. The finished piece dissolves back to the blank canvas at the end.

## Common mistakes

- A first frame that's a blank canvas or a black frame.
- Running past 45 seconds before you know what holds.
- Titles nobody would search.
- Reposting a TikTok with the watermark.

## FAQ

**Do Shorts help long videos?**
Shorts bring subscribers, long videos keep them. You need both.

**Same channel?**
Yes for most people. Split only once each has its own audience.
