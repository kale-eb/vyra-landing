**To replace one clip in an iPhone edit, identify the shot to remove, add the replacement and check its timing against the surrounding footage. In Vyra, describe which parts of the edit should stay unchanged.**

You filmed a better close-up after the first edit was finished. The replacement may be longer, louder or framed differently. Treat those differences as editing choices instead of assuming the new shot will fit automatically.

The listing confirms adding, trimming and rearranging clips, text and audio adjustment. See the [official Vyra App Store listing and version history](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012).

## Work through the edit

1. Add the new source clip to the existing Vyra project.
2. Identify the old shot using its content and position in the sequence.
3. Choose the useful section of the replacement and fit it into that part of the edit.
4. Check neighboring cuts, captions and the source audio.
5. Preview the whole sequence and export a revised copy.

## Make the important choices

| What you find | Editing choice |
| --- | --- |
| The new clip is longer | Trim it to the intended section or deliberately change the pacing |
| The replacement includes loud camera audio | Review its volume against the existing soundtrack |
| A label points to the old subject | Update or reposition the label |

## A brief to try

```
Replace the blurry close-up of the finished painting with the new close-up I added. Keep the introduction and ending. Match the intended duration of that section and check the label still describes the shot.
```

Compare the new shot with what comes before and after it. Check that the subject does not jump sides unexpectedly and that a sound from the replacement does not interrupt a sentence. This workflow does not depend on a particular one-tap replacement control.

## FAQ

**Must the replacement have the same original length?**
No. Choose the section you need and decide how long it should stay in the finished edit.

**Can I keep the rest of the sequence?**
State that constraint clearly, then review the surrounding cuts to confirm it was respected.

**Will labels always follow the new subject?**
Check them yourself. A label placed for the old shot may need a new position or wording.
