**B-roll is the footage you cut to while the main shot or the voice continues. The b-roll that gets used follows a three-shot rule for every subject (wide, medium, detail), uses one simple movement per clip, runs 8 to 15 seconds so there is room to trim, and is shot in light that matches the main footage. Random pretty shots with no relationship to the story stay on the card.**

## Why it matters

B-roll hides cuts, shows evidence, and gives the eye something new every few seconds. It is the difference between a talking head that feels like a lecture and one that feels like a video. The problem is never having too little footage; it is having the wrong footage.

## The method

1. **List the nouns in your script.** Every object, place, and action you mention is a b-roll shot. "I mix the paint on a glass palette" gives you paint, palette, mixing, and hands.
2. **Shoot three sizes of each.** Wide (the whole scene), medium (the subject and its context), detail (the texture or the hands). Three shots per noun.
3. **Add one movement per clip.** Push in, pull out, pan, tilt, or slide. Slow and steady. One movement, not three.
4. **Hold each clip 8 to 15 seconds.** You will use 2 to 3 seconds. The extra gives the editor a clean start and end.
5. **Match the light.** Shoot b-roll in the same room, same time of day, same white balance as the main footage. Mixed light is the most common reason b-roll gets rejected.
6. **Shoot the action from two angles.** The same pour from the side and from above. It lets you cut within the action.
7. **Get the transition shots.** Hands entering frame, objects placed down, doors, footsteps. They connect scenes.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Shots per subject | 3 (wide, medium, detail) |
| Clip length when shooting | 8 to 15 s |
| Clip length when used | 1.5 to 3 s |
| Movement per clip | 1 |
| Frame rate for slow motion | 60 fps, played at 30 |
| Shooting ratio | 10 to 15 clips per minute of finished video |
| Light | same source and white balance as main footage |

Detail shots are the most reused. When in doubt, get closer.

## Examples

Talking head about pricing an art commission: b-roll list is the invoice on a laptop, hands writing a quote, the canvas in progress, the client message on a phone, a wide of the studio. Three sizes each, 15 clips total.

Coffee shop vlog segment: wide of the shop exterior, medium of ordering, detail of the pour, medium of sitting down, detail of the cup, one moving shot walking to the table. Six clips, 30 seconds of footage, 8 seconds used.

Product demo: detail of the product from three angles at 60 fps, medium of it in use, wide of the setting, a hand placing it down as the transition.

## Do it in Vyra

- "Wherever I mention an object or an action in this talking head, find the matching clip in my project footage and insert it as b-roll for 2 to 3 seconds. Prefer detail shots. Keep my voice continuous."
- "Search my footage for shots of hands mixing paint and use the closest one at 14 seconds when I say 'mixing'."
- "The b-roll at 22 seconds is warmer than the main shot. Match its color to the talking head."

## Common mistakes

- Only wide shots. Wides are context; details are the ones that cut in.
- Two second clips with the movement still settling. Hold longer.
- Shooting b-roll a different day in different light. It will never match.
- Three movements in one clip. The editor cannot find a clean piece.
- Pretty shots that relate to nothing in the script.

## FAQ

**How much b-roll do I need for a 60 second talking head?**
Six to ten inserts of 2 to 3 seconds each. Shoot 15 to 20 clips to have choice.

**Should b-roll have audio?**
Record it. Natural sound under the voice adds texture and can be pulled up between sentences.

**Can I reuse b-roll across videos?**
Yes, if it is generic (studio wides, hands working). Keep a library organized by subject. Vyra can search your library by what is in the shot.
