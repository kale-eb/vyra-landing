**In Vyra on iPhone, select the dark clip and open Adjust. Raise the brightness carefully while checking both the subject and the lightest parts of the image.**

You can make a dim studio easier to see and still lose the white paint on the palette. Check that bright area while you adjust the face or artwork. Look at a frame with movement as well as a still frame.

Vyra’s in-app help describes this workflow under “Editing tools on iPhone”. The [official App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) documents native editing and export.

## Work through the edit

1. Choose a representative frame in the dark clip.
2. Open Adjust or ask chat to brighten only that shot.
3. Increase the adjustment a little at a time and watch the lightest areas.
4. Play the moving shot to check grain and changes in exposure.
5. Compare it with the neighboring clip before keeping the correction.

## A brief to try

> Brighten the studio shot enough to see the painting. Keep detail in the white paint and window. Leave the brighter outdoor clips unchanged.

## Check the result

A very dark recording may have limited usable detail. Raising brightness can reveal grain and color patches. Keep the version that shows the subject clearly without making the background distracting.

## FAQ

**Will brightening recover every detail?**
The result depends on what the camera recorded. Check the source and the corrected version at the same moment.

**Should I apply the correction to every shot?**
Start with the dark shot. Clips filmed in another light may need a different adjustment.
