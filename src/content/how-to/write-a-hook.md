**A hook is the first 1.5 seconds of a short-form video. It works when three layers land at once: something moving in the frame, a spoken line that opens a specific question, and on-screen text that restates the promise for viewers watching muted. Weak hooks describe the video. Strong hooks make a claim, name a person, or start mid-action.**

## Why it matters

Most viewers decide to stay or scroll before the second second. Retention platforms weight that first decision heavily, so a hook is the single edit with the highest leverage on reach. Everything after it only matters if people are still there.

## The method

1. **Write the payoff first.** Decide the one thing the viewer will know, feel, or see by the end. The hook is a promise of that payoff, so you cannot write it until you know what you are promising.
2. **Pick a hook type.** Choose from claim, question, contrarian, curiosity gap, direct address, result-first, or mid-action. Match the type to the format: result-first for demos, mid-action for vlogs, contrarian for talking head opinions.
3. **Cut the first three words.** Draft the line, then remove everything before the first concrete noun or verb. "So today I wanted to talk about why" becomes "Why your captions are killing retention."
4. **Add a visual hook.** Start on movement, a face already mid-expression, a reveal, or a cut. Never start on a static wide shot of you settling into frame.
5. **Add a text hook.** Put a 3 to 7 word restatement on screen for the first 1 to 2 seconds. It should be readable without sound and not identical to the spoken line.
6. **Say it once, then move.** Do not repeat the hook or explain it. The next line should already be delivering.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Time to first spoken word | under 0.5 s |
| Spoken hook length | 6 to 12 words |
| Text hook length | 3 to 7 words |
| Text hook on screen | 1.5 to 2.5 s |
| First cut or movement | within 1.5 s |
| Hooks to draft per video | 5, keep 1 |

## Common mistakes

- Opening with "hey guys" or your name. Nobody is invested yet.
- Describing the video instead of promising the result.
- A text hook that just transcribes the spoken hook. Use the two layers to say two things.
- Starting on a static frame while you reach for the phone.
- Overpromising and paying it off in the last second. Deliver early, then go deeper.

## FAQ

**Does a hook have to be spoken?**
No. A visual hook plus a text hook carries silent formats like montages and cinematic edits. Spoken hooks matter most for talking head and storytelling.

**How many hooks should I test?**
Draft five, post one, and if a video underperforms, re-cut the first 3 seconds with a different hook type before changing anything else.

**Should the hook match the caption or title?**
The post caption can extend the hook, but do not repeat it word for word. Give each surface a different angle on the same promise.
