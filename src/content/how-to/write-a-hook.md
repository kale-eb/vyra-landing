**A hook is the first 1.5 seconds of a video. Mine work when three things happen at once. Something moves in the frame, I say one specific line, and a few words on screen say it a second way for people watching muted. Weak hooks describe the video. Good ones make a claim or start in the middle of the action.**

## Why it matters

People decide to stay or scroll before the second second.

## The method

1. **Write the ending first.** Know the one thing the viewer gets by the end. The hook is a promise of that.
2. **Pick a type.** A claim, a question, a result, or mid-action. Result-first for demos, mid-action for vlogs, a claim for opinions.
3. **Cut the first three words.** "So today I wanted to talk about why your captions" becomes "Your captions are killing your retention."
4. **Start on movement.** A face already talking, a reveal, a cut. Never a wide shot of you sitting down.
5. **Add 3 to 7 words on screen.** Readable without sound. Different words from what you say.
6. **Say it once and move on.** The next line should already be delivering.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Time to first word | under 0.5 s |
| Spoken hook | 6 to 12 words |
| Text hook | 3 to 7 words |
| Text on screen | 1.5 to 2.5 s |
| First cut or movement | within 1.5 s |
| Hooks I draft per video | 5, post 1 |

## Common mistakes

- Opening with "hey guys" or your name.
- Describing the video instead of promising the result.
- Text on screen that just repeats the spoken line.
- Starting on a still frame while you reach for the phone.

## FAQ

**Does a hook have to be spoken?**
No. A visual plus text carries montages and silent edits. Spoken hooks matter most for talking head and story videos.

**How many should I test?**
Draft five, post one. If it flops, re-cut the first 3 seconds before changing anything else.
