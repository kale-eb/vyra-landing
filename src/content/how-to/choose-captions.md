**Captions exist because most short-form viewing is muted. The right default is 3 to 5 word phrases that appear as spoken, in a bold sans-serif around 5 percent of frame height, positioned in the lower-middle of a 9:16 frame above the platform UI. Word-by-word captions suit fast, punchy delivery. Phrase captions suit explanation and story. A few enlarged emphasis words per video do more than animating every word.**

## Why it matters

Muted viewing is the norm on Instagram, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. Captions are not accessibility extras; they are the primary text layer of the video. They also give the eye something to track between cuts, which is a pacing tool on its own.

## The method

1. **Decide the caption mode per video, not per platform.** Phrase captions for talking head, tutorials, storytelling, and vlogs. Word-by-word for fast opinion pieces and hype-driven UGC.
2. **Pick one face and one weight.** A heavy sans-serif is standard. Vyra's default is Inter Bold for captions and Montserrat Black for titles and emphasis. Do not mix three fonts.
3. **Size for the phone, not the monitor.** Cap height around 4 to 6 percent of frame height on 9:16. Test on a real phone before exporting.
4. **Place in the safe zone.** Lower-middle, above the bottom 20 percent where platform UI sits, and inside the central 80 percent horizontally so nothing clips.
5. **Choose 1 to 3 emphasis moments.** The claim, the turn, the payoff. Give those words a larger composed treatment and let ordinary captions disappear during that moment.
6. **Clean the transcript.** Remove filler words, fix names and product terms, and break lines at natural speech pauses rather than at a character count.

## Rules of thumb

| Setting | Phrase captions | Word-by-word |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Words per caption | 3 to 5 | 1 |
| Time on screen | 0.8 to 2 s | 0.15 to 0.4 s |
| Best for | explanation, story, vlog | hype, opinion, hooks |
| Font weight | 700 | 800 to 900 |
| Animation | none or instant | pop or instant |
| Max lines | 2 | 1 |

Contrast rule: white text with a subtle dark outline or scrim works on almost any footage. A bright accent color only on emphasis words, and only one accent per video.

When to use no captions: silent cinematic sequences, montages with no speech, and any shot where the frame is the point. Captions over a beautiful wide shot with no dialogue are clutter.

## Examples

Phrase captions, talking head about pricing:
- "I charge by the" / "day, not the hour" / "and here is why"

Same line, word-by-word for a punchier cut:
- "DAY" appears large on the beat, ordinary captions return after.

Emphasis moment: speaker says "I just dropped out to build something." Ordinary captions stop, "I just / dropped / out." accumulates in large Montserrat Black with "dropped" in the accent color, holds for one second, clears, and ordinary captions resume on "to build something."

## Common mistakes

- Captions in the bottom 15 percent, covered by the caption box and buttons on the platform.
- Animating every word with a bounce. It reads as noise after 10 seconds.
- Auto-transcript posted uncorrected. One wrong product name costs credibility.
- Four-line captions. Break earlier.
- Yellow highlight on every third word. Save color for one idea.

## FAQ

**Should I use platform auto-captions instead of burned-in captions?**
Burn them in. Platform captions vary in placement and styling and are off by default for some viewers. You can add the platform's captions on top for accessibility.

**What about long-form on YouTube?**
Use the platform's closed captions and keep burned-in text to titles and emphasis. Constant burned-in captions on 16:9 look heavy.

**Do captions need to match the spoken words exactly?**
Match meaning, not filler. Cut "um," "like," and repeated words, but never change what was actually said.
