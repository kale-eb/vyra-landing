**To mute a video clip in Vyra on iPhone, select the clip on the timeline and choose Mute. This is useful when a shot looks right but you want to leave out its recorded sound. Play the surrounding shots to check that the other audio you want is still there.**

Say you filmed the finished painting while a fan was running. Keep that shot for the reveal and mute its noisy audio. If the previous shot has a brush sound you like, leave that clip audible. Vyra’s in-app help lists Mute and Volume among the tools for selected clips on iPhone.

## Mute the clip you mean to change

1. Play the section and identify which clip contains the sound you want to remove.
2. Tap that video clip in the timeline and choose **Mute**.
3. Play the section again. If the same sound still plays, check for [extracted audio](/how-to/extract-audio-from-video-on-iphone) or another copy of the recording already on the timeline.
4. Listen through the cut. If the change makes the room suddenly go quiet, try lowering the clip’s **Volume** so a little room sound remains.
5. [Save the video to Photos](/how-to/export-video-to-photos-on-iphone) and listen to the saved file before sharing it.

## A brief to try

> Mute the original audio in the last shot of the finished painting. Keep the brush sound in the shot before it. Leave my narration and the picture cuts where they are.

Check the quiet section with headphones. It is easy to miss a second audio track when your phone speaker is turned down.

## FAQ

**Can I mute one clip and keep the music?**
Yes. Select the video clip whose sound you want to silence. Leave the music track audible and replay the same section to check the result.

**Does muting a clip also mute its speech?**
Muting the clip silences its recorded speech too. If the speech and noise are recorded together, try a quieter passage or a clearer take. If the noise comes from another clip or track, adjust that source separately.

**How do I mute only part of a clip?**
Split on either side of the unwanted sound and mute the middle piece. Check the joins, especially if the sound starts or ends close to a word.
