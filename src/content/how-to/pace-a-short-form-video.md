**Pacing is how quickly the video gives the viewer something new: a cut, a camera move, a new caption, a new sound, or a new idea. Short-form pacing that holds is not uniformly fast. It runs fast-slow-fast: a dense opening, a slower middle where the actual value lands, and a fast close. A visual change every 2 to 4 seconds keeps the eye busy without making the content feel rushed.**

## Why it matters

Retention graphs for short-form almost always show the same shape: a cliff in the first 3 seconds, a slow bleed through the middle, and a second drop where viewers realize the ending is coming. Pacing is how you flatten each of those drops. Cuts alone do not do it; changes in information do.

## The method

1. **Map the beats before you cut.** Write the 4 to 8 ideas the video delivers in order. Each beat gets its own pacing decision.
2. **Front-load density.** The first 3 seconds should contain the hook, a cut, and a text element. Three events in three seconds.
3. **Slow down for the payoff.** When you deliver the actual point, hold the shot longer, drop the music, or let a caption sit. Contrast is what makes the fast parts feel fast.
4. **Place a pattern interrupt every 5 to 8 seconds.** A punch-in, a b-roll insert, an on-screen word, a sound effect, a camera angle switch. Rotate types so the interrupt itself does not become predictable.
5. **Cut on action or on the word.** Cut when a hand moves, a head turns, or a key word lands. Cuts on nothing feel like errors.
6. **Accelerate into the ending.** Shorten shot lengths over the last 20 percent and end on the last useful frame, not a lingering outro.

## Rules of thumb

| Format | Average shot length | Interrupt cadence | Typical length |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Talking head | 3 to 6 s between visual changes | every 6 to 8 s | 30 to 60 s |
| UGC hook and demo | 2 to 3 s | every 4 to 5 s | 20 to 40 s |
| Montage (beat-synced) | 0.5 to 2 s | on musical phrases | 15 to 30 s |
| Vlog | 2 to 5 s | every 5 to 8 s | 45 to 90 s |
| Cinematic | 3 to 8 s | on music swells | 20 to 45 s |

Velocity rule: the middle third of the video should have the longest average shot length. If your middle is your fastest section, you have buried the value.

## Examples

Cut map for a 30 second talking head:

- 0.0 to 0.4 s: hook line starts, text hook on screen
- 1.2 s: punch-in on key word
- 3.0 s: cut to wide, first point
- 7.5 s: b-roll insert, 2 s
- 9.5 s: back to talking head, second point, hold 6 s
- 15.5 s: on-screen word, punch-in
- 16.0 to 23.0 s: payoff, single shot, music drops 6 dB
- 23.0 s: cut, third point, shorter phrases
- 26.0 s: b-roll 1.5 s
- 27.5 to 30.0 s: closing line, cut on the last word

Cut map for a 20 second beat-synced montage at 120 BPM: cuts every 2 beats (1 s) for bars 1 to 4, every beat (0.5 s) for bars 5 to 8, one 4 second hold on the hero shot, then every beat to the end.

## Do it in Vyra

- "Re-pace this 45 second talking head. Keep every sentence but add a visual change at least every 5 seconds: alternate punch-ins, my b-roll clips, and single emphasized words. Slow down on the part where I explain the pricing and drop the music under it."
- "Cut this footage to the beat of the track. Start with cuts every 2 beats, switch to every beat after the first chorus, and hold one long shot on the finished painting before the last section. 9:16, 25 seconds max."
- "The middle of this video drags from 12 to 25 seconds. Tighten the pauses, add two b-roll inserts from my library that match what I am describing, and put a caption emphasis on the word 'free'."

## Common mistakes

- Cutting every second the whole way through. Uniform speed reads as noise.
- Interrupts that never change type. Twenty punch-ins in a row stop working by the fifth.
- Fading out at the end. The fade tells the viewer to leave before the algorithm counts the finish.
- Pacing to the music while ignoring the speech. Cut around sentence boundaries first.
- Speeding up the payoff because you are nervous it is boring. Slow it down instead.

## FAQ

**Is faster always better for retention?**
No. Faster openings help. Faster middles usually hurt because the viewer cannot absorb the point they stayed for.

**How do I know where viewers drop?**
Check the retention graph in the platform's analytics. Place an interrupt 1 to 2 seconds before each recurring dip.

**Should every cut be on a beat?**
Only in music-led formats. For speech-led formats, cut on words and gestures and let the music follow.
