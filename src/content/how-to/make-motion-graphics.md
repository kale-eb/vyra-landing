**Motion graphics are animated text, shapes, and media layered over footage. In short-form they work best as a small vocabulary used with intent: kinetic type for one or two key phrases, a lower third when a person needs a name, a callout when the viewer should look at a specific spot, and an accumulate-and-release composition when several pieces of evidence should gather and then clear. The footage stays the subject; the graphic clarifies or emphasizes it.**

## Why it matters

A graphic that shows what the footage cannot (a number, a name, a comparison, a relationship) makes the point land faster. A graphic added because the frame felt empty makes the video feel like a template. The difference is whether it carries information.

## The method

1. **Name the job of the graphic.** Emphasize a claim, identify a person, point at a detail, compare two things, or show a collection. If you cannot name the job, cut the graphic.
2. **Choose the simplest element that does the job.** Native text, a shape, or a placed image before a coded composition. Editable elements are faster to change.
3. **Match the type system.** One title face, one caption face, one accent. Vyra's default is Montserrat Black for titles and Inter Bold for captions with a single accent color per video.
4. **Time the entrance to the words.** A kinetic word appears on the spoken word, a lower third appears when the person first speaks, a callout appears when the speaker refers to the detail.
5. **Decide how it leaves.** Every graphic needs an exit. Clear it when the idea ends, usually on a cut or at the start of the next sentence.
6. **Build accumulate-and-release as a single scene.** Compose the finished layout first, reveal each element as it is named, hold the full set for 1 to 2 seconds, then clear everything together and return to clean footage.

## Rules of thumb

| Graphic | On screen | Size on 9:16 | Entrance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kinetic key phrase | 1 to 2 s | 12 to 20 percent frame height | instant or pop on the word |
| Lower third | 3 to 5 s | name 3 percent, role 2 percent | slide or fade, once per person |
| Callout (arrow, circle) | 1.5 to 3 s | small, near the detail | instant on the reference |
| Comparison (two items) | as long as the comparison | matched scale, equal weight | together |
| Accumulate-and-release | 4 to 10 s total | primary item largest | staggered on each name |

Limit: 2 to 3 graphic moments in a 60 second video. Ordinary captions are not counted.

## Examples

Kinetic key phrase: speaker says "I just dropped out to build something." Captions stop, "I just / dropped / out." stacks in Montserrat Black with "dropped" in Sky, holds 1 second, clears, captions resume.

Accumulate-and-release, maker's story: full-bleed face for 2 seconds, then a process clip appears lower-left as she says "in the studio," a sketchbook page upper-right on "the sketches," a street shot lower-right on "the walk." All three hold with the face still primary, then clear together on the cut to the finished painting. This is the structure used in @sulansart process videos.

Callout: tutorial about a phone setting. A circle appears around the toggle on "this one here," holds 2 seconds, gone on the next sentence.

- "Build an accumulate-and-release composition from 8 to 16 seconds: keep my face as the main shot and add the sketchbook clip, the studio wide, and the street photo as smaller inserts, each appearing when I name it. Clear all three on the cut at 16 seconds."
- "Add a lower third the first time each person speaks: name in Inter Bold, role smaller underneath, bottom-left, on screen 4 seconds."

## Common mistakes

- A graphic on every sentence. The emphasis stops meaning anything.
- Six tiny inserts instead of one readable one. Choose a footprint the phone can read.
- Graphics that enter but never leave.
- Text floating above a head with no relationship to the frame. Use real scale and, if you want depth, real occlusion.
- Copying a dashboard aesthetic onto a personal vlog. Match the graphic language to the content.

## FAQ

**Do I need After Effects?**
No. Native text, shapes, and placed media cover most short-form needs. Coded compositions are for coupled animation or geometry those elements cannot express.

**How many fonts?**
Two roles at most in a video: captions and titles. An occasional accent face is optional.

**Should graphics have sound effects?**
Only when the sound carries meaning. A whoosh on every entrance becomes the same problem as a bounce on every caption.
