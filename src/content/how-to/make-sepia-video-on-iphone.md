**In Vyra on iPhone, select the clip and open Effects to try Sepia, or ask chat for the warm brown treatment. Keep the look confined to the section where it helps the story.**

You might use a warm treatment while talking about an earlier sketch. Keep its date or label accurate. An effect can distinguish a section visually, but it should not imply that a recording is older than it really is.

The [official iPhone listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vyra-ai-video-editor/id6808889012) documents editing supplied media. Vyra’s current in-app help, Effects, documents the controls described here.

## Work through the edit

1. Choose the section that should have the warm treatment.
2. Select the clip and open Effects, or name the section in chat.
3. Apply Sepia and compare it with the untreated shot.
4. Check small text, pale details and the join back to the ordinary picture.
5. Play the exported sequence to judge whether the change helps explain the section.

## A brief to try

> Give the clip of my older sketch a warm sepia treatment. Keep the date label I supplied readable. Return to the normal look when the current painting appears.

## Review the result

Vyra’s current Effects help describes Sepia as a warm brown tone and documents native Effects. Use the treatment as a visual choice. If the video explains actual paint or fabric colors, include a clear untreated view so the viewer can inspect those colors.

## FAQ

**Does a sepia effect establish when footage was recorded?**
No. Use the dates you can support. A chosen look is separate from the recording’s history.

**Should I use sepia for a color comparison?**
Keep an untreated view for a comparison where the actual colors matter. A warm treatment changes how those colors appear.
