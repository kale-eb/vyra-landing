**To make a J-cut in Vyra on iPhone, start the incoming clip's audio under the end of the current shot. Cut to the incoming picture after the sound starts. Use Extract audio when the sound you need is attached to the incoming video.**

Imagine a shot of you walking up to a studio. Let people hear the door chime while they still see the street. Then cut inside. The chime tells the viewer where they are about to go. You can do the same thing with the first few words of a new speaker.

## Set the sound ahead of the picture

1. Place the outgoing and incoming shots in the order you want to show them.
2. Choose an incoming sound that makes sense before you see its source. A door chime or a clear voice is easier to follow than an unexplained thump.
3. Work with the incoming sound separately. Select the video clip and use Extract audio if you need its original sound on its own track.
4. Let sound from the start of the incoming recording play before the picture changes. Keep the incoming sound and picture in sync where a speaker's lips are visible. You can trim the start of the incoming picture and keep that part of its sound.
5. Play the cut with headphones. If two voices compete, shorten the overlap or make room before the next speaker starts. Check incoming captions once the audio timing is final.

## A brief to try

> Let the studio door chime begin a little before we cut from the street to the inside of the studio. Keep the action in order. Leave space between the last words of my introduction and the chime.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between a J-cut and an L-cut?**
In a J-cut, the next shot's sound starts over the current picture. In an L-cut, the current shot's sound continues over the next picture. Choose the one that makes the action or conversation easier to follow.

**How long should the sound lead the picture?**
Try just long enough to recognize the sound or hear the first part of a phrase. Replay the two shots together. Shorten the lead if the viewer has to guess who is speaking.

**When should I check the captions?**
Check them after you move the audio. They should follow when the words are heard, including any speech that starts before the picture changes.
