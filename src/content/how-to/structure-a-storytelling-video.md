**A storytelling video has four parts in order: setup (who, where, what is at stake, in one line), tension (a specific problem or question), turn (the moment something changes), and payoff (the result and what it means). Open loops planted early and closed late hold viewers through the middle, and a callback to the opening line makes the ending feel finished. In short-form the whole arc fits in 45 to 90 seconds.**

## Why it matters

Stories are the format that gets shared and remembered. They also have the flattest retention curves because the viewer is waiting for the payoff. The structure is not a formula for creativity; it is the scaffold that lets a real event become a video someone watches to the end.

## The method

1. **Start at the tension, not the setup.** Open on the problem or a line from the middle of the story. Deliver the setup in the second sentence.
2. **State what is at stake in one sentence.** "If this does not dry by 5, the gallery pickup is off." The viewer needs to know what losing looks like.
3. **Plant an open loop.** Mention something you will explain later: "and I had no idea what was about to go wrong." Do not resolve it yet.
4. **Show the turn as an event.** Something happened. A message arrived, a plan failed, a person said something. Show it in footage, not narration.
5. **Deliver the payoff plainly.** What happened, what it cost, what you learned. One or two sentences.
6. **Close the loop and call back.** Answer the planted question and echo the opening line or image.
7. **Cut everything that does not move the story.** Beautiful shots with no story function go in a montage, not here.

## Rules of thumb

| Part | Share of runtime | Example length in 60 s |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook (tension line) | 5 percent | 3 s |
| Setup | 15 percent | 9 s |
| Rising tension | 35 percent | 21 s |
| Turn | 15 percent | 9 s |
| Payoff | 20 percent | 12 s |
| Callback and close | 10 percent | 6 s |

Open loops: 1 to 2 per video. Plant before 20 percent, close after 70 percent.
Turns: 1 in a 60 second video, 2 to 3 in a 3 minute one.

## Examples

Commission story, 60 seconds:
- Hook: "The client wanted it by Friday. It was Thursday night and the canvas was wet."
- Setup: who the client is, what the piece is, one wide shot of the studio.
- Tension: hair dryer, fan, checking the clock. Loop planted: "and then she texted."
- Turn: the text says the pickup moved to Saturday. Reaction on camera.
- Payoff: finished piece on Saturday, her reaction, what it taught about buffer days.
- Callback: "It was Thursday night. It is fine to say no to Friday."

Relatable story, 30 seconds: hook "I almost quit painting in March," setup one line, tension one moment of the empty studio, turn one sale notification, payoff the current studio, callback "March was the month I almost quit."

## Do it in Vyra

- "This is a storytelling video. From the transcript, find the line with the most tension and open on it. Then order the footage as setup, problem, turn, payoff. Keep my talking head as the spine and use the b-roll to show each event. 60 seconds, vertical."
- "Find the moment I read the text message and make it the turn: hold on my reaction for 2 full seconds with no music, then bring the music back."
- "End on a callback: repeat the shot from the opening and put my first line on screen in Montserrat Black as the last frame."

## Common mistakes

- Starting with the setup. "So last week I got a commission" loses the viewer before the problem arrives.
- No stakes. If nothing can be lost, there is no tension.
- Narrating the turn instead of showing it.
- Resolving the loop too early. Hold it until the last third.
- Ending on a lesson paragraph. One sentence of meaning, then the callback, then out.

## FAQ

**Does every video need a story?**
No. Tutorials, montages, and demos have their own structures. Use story structure when something actually happened.

**What if the story has no turn?**
Then it is a vlog or a process video, not a story. Cut it as one of those, or find the moment your plan changed and build around it.

**How long can a short-form story be?**
Up to 90 seconds holds if every part is earning its time. Past that, the format shifts to long-form pacing.
