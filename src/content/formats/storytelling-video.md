**A storytelling video is a short narrative with a setup, a turn, and a resolution, told in voiceover or to camera and covered with footage that shows the story rather than the storyteller.**

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Best for | Personal experiences, brand origin, lessons, emotional content |
| Hook style | Start in the middle of the tension: "The client had already paid and I had nothing" |
| Pacing | Slow open, faster middle, one held beat at the turn |
| Caption style | Phrase captions, one large composed line at the turning point |
| Music | Essential, builds and drops with the story |
| Shot list | 10-20 clips or photos that show the story's places, objects, people. One to-camera take for the payoff. Ambient sound. |
| Common mistakes | Starting at the beginning instead of the tension. Saying feelings instead of showing moments. No clear turn. |

## Structure

- 0-3s: the moment of highest tension, mid-story
- 3-15s: rewind, the normal before it changed
- 15-50s: rising problem, 2-3 specific beats
- 50-60s: the turn, held for a beat, music drops
- 60-85s: what happened, what it cost or gave
- 85-95s: the line you want remembered, on your face

## How to shoot it

- Write the story in six sentences before recording anything.
- Record the voiceover in one go, close to the mic, sitting down.
- Gather real footage and photos from the time if they exist. Authenticity beats quality.
- Shoot new b-roll of the places and objects that appear in the story.
- Record one take of yourself saying the final line to camera.

## How to edit it

- Cut the voiceover tight, keep breaths only at the turn.
- Put footage on the nouns. When you say "the studio", show the studio.
- Slow the cut rate down at the turning point, let one shot hold 3-4 seconds.
- Music: quiet under the setup, build through the problem, drop out or drop low at the turn.
- One composed large caption on the sentence that matters most. Ordinary captions everywhere else.
- Return to your face for the last line.

## Prompt it in Vyra

> This is a voiceover about the time I almost quit painting. Build a 90-second vertical story: open on the line "I had one week and no money", then go back to the beginning. Cover each part with the studio clips and old photos in the project. Music should build and drop out at the line "then the email came". Captions in 3-5 word phrases, make that one line big.

> Cut the to-camera ending onto the last 10 seconds so I'm on screen for the final sentence. Keep the music low under it.

> Use the pacing of the attached reference for the turn: hold the shot longer and cut the music there.

## Variations

- Story time: to-camera the whole way, no b-roll, energy carries it.
- Photo story: still images with slow motion, voiceover only.
- Two-timeline: then vs now, cross-cut.
- Client story: the subject is a customer, told in their words.

## FAQ

**Do I need to be on camera?**
No. Voiceover over footage works, but one to-camera line at the end raises trust and follows.

**How do I find the hook?**
Take the moment where things were worst or most surprising and start there. Tell the rest as a flashback.

**Can Vyra structure it from a rambling voice memo?**
Yes. It transcribes the audio and can reorder segments if you describe the beats you want, such as "tension first, then rewind".
