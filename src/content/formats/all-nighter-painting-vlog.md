**An all-nighter painting vlog compresses one overnight session into a short that follows the clock, from setup at night through the energy dips to the finished piece at dawn, with the time on screen doing the storytelling.**

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Best for | Deadline stories, art school audiences, relatable "why do I do this" content, showing the work behind a piece |
| Hook style | The time and the state of the canvas ("11:40 pm, it's blank, it's due at 9") |
| Pacing | Time-stamped chapters every 8-12 seconds, faster as the night goes on, slow at dawn |
| Caption style | Timestamps as titles, phrase captions over talking clips |
| Music | Starts upbeat, drops to quiet around 3 am, lifts at dawn |
| Shot list | Locked canvas angle all night. Selfie check-ins every hour or so. The clock or phone screen. Coffee, snacks, the floor. The window as it gets light. The finished piece in morning light. |
| Common mistakes | Only canvas footage with no you. Skipping the low point. Ending before the dawn shot. |

## Structure

- 0-4s: the time, the blank canvas, the deadline, one line
- 4-25s: 11 pm to 1 am, setup and block-in, upbeat, quick timestamps
- 25-45s: 2 am to 4 am, the dip, music quieter, a check-in where you are tired
- 45-60s: 4 am to 6 am, the push, fast canvas progress
- 60-75s: dawn, the window, the finished piece in real light
- 75-90s: one honest closing line, the piece held still

## How to shoot it

- Lock one angle on the canvas and leave it running or restart it at each check-in.
- Do a selfie check-in every hour. Say the time. Say how you feel. Twenty seconds max.
- Film the small things: the clock, the coffee, the mess. They carry the night.
- Do not miss the window at dawn. Set an alarm.
- Shoot the finished piece in the morning light, not under the lamp.

## How to edit it

- Every chapter opens on a timestamp title in the corner.
- Canvas footage sped up between check-ins; check-ins at real time.
- Cut the check-ins hard: the one sentence that says the time and the mood.
- Music energy follows the night: up, down, up.
- Captions on the talking clips only.
- Dawn plays slow with no speed-up and no cuts for at least 4 seconds.

## Prompt it in Vyra

> Cut a 75-second all-nighter vlog from this footage. Follow the clock: use my selfie check-ins in order and put a timestamp in the top left for each one. Speed up the canvas footage between check-ins so the painting visibly progresses. Keep the 3 am check-in where I say "I think I'm going to cry" at real time. Music upbeat until 2 am, quiet until 5, then lift at the window shot. End on the finished piece in the morning light held for 5 seconds.

> Add 3-5 word captions on the check-ins only, none over the canvas footage.

> Find the shot where the window starts to go blue and use it as the dawn transition.

## Variations

- Deadline vlog: the piece is due, and you show the submission.
- Two-person all-nighter: alternating check-ins with a friend.
- Failed all-nighter: it did not get done, and that is the story.
- Silent all-nighter: timestamps and ambient sound only, no talking.

## FAQ

**How much footage do I need?**
The locked canvas angle running, plus 6 to 10 short check-ins. Most of it will be sped up.

**Should I show the low point?**
Yes. The tired check-in is the most relatable clip in the video and the reason people watch to the end.

**Can Vyra keep the check-ins in order automatically?**
Yes. It reads the timestamps in the file metadata and what you say on camera, so "put the check-ins in time order with a timestamp title" works.
