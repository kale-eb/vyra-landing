**A storytelling prompt gives Vyra the four beats (setup, tension, turn, payoff) and the line that starts each one. Chronological order is the default for any editor; a story often needs the ending first, a cold open, or a detour. You have to say so.**

Minimal:
```
Cut this into a 60-second story: start with the setup, build the tension, then the turn, then the payoff.
```

Standard:
```
Make a 60-second vertical storytelling video from this talking head. Open cold on the line "I almost didn't post it." Then the setup: the two sentences about starting the account. Then the tension: the part where I explain the first month of no views. Then the turn: the line "and then one video hit." Then the payoff: what changed. Cut everything that is not one of those beats. Straight cuts, no music yet.
```

Full:
```
Build a 75-second 9:16 storytelling video from this footage. Structure, with the line that opens each beat: cold open, "I almost didn't post it," 3 seconds max. Setup, starting at "so in January I made an account," keep it under 12 seconds. Tension, from "for a month, nothing" through the sentence about wanting to quit, up to 25 seconds, and let the pauses breathe here, do not tighten below 0.6 seconds. Turn, on the line "and then one video hit," punch in 15% on that line only. Payoff, from "now I" to the end, under 20 seconds. Cover the tension section with 2 to 3 b-roll clips of me at the desk at night. Everything else stays on my face. End on the last word, no fade, no CTA.
```

## What each part does

Four beats with a quoted opening line each is the structure. The per-beat length caps keep the middle from swallowing the video. "Let the pauses breathe" in the tension beat is the opposite of the usual tighten-everything rule, and it matters: tension needs air. The single punch-in on the turn line marks the turn without decorating the rest. B-roll scoped to one beat keeps the face present for the emotional parts. "No fade, no CTA" protects the ending.

## Fixes

- Chronological instead of cold open: "Move the line 'I almost didn't post it' to the very start, before the setup."
- Middle drags: "Cut the tension section to 18 seconds. Keep the sentence about wanting to quit."
- Payoff is buried: "End on 'now I edit in ten minutes' and cut the two sentences after it."
- Too tight, feels rushed: "Allow pauses up to 0.8 seconds in the tension section."
- B-roll over the payoff: "Remove all b-roll from the payoff. Face only."

## FAQ

**Do I need a cold open?**
Not always, but it is the fastest way to earn the first three seconds. Pick a line from the payoff or the turn and put it first.

**How long should each beat be?**
Setup short, tension longest, turn one line, payoff medium. For 60 seconds: roughly 8, 25, 3, 20.

**Can Vyra find the beats itself?**
Ask it: "List the setup, tension, turn, and payoff lines from this transcript with timestamps." Then correct the list and use it as the prompt.

## Rules that apply to every prompt

1. One target per prompt. Length, platform, purpose.
2. Quote the exact line when you mean a specific moment.
3. Give thresholds ("pauses over half a second") instead of adjectives ("snappy").
4. Say what NOT to do when the agent tends to over-decorate.
5. One correction per follow-up message.
6. Ask for a timestamped list of changes when you want to verify.
7. Name what to copy from a reference. Attaching it is not an instruction.
