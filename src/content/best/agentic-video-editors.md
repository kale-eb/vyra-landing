**An agentic video editor is one where an AI agent does the editing on your real footage. You describe the video, it plans the cuts, builds them on a timeline, and then takes corrections. Vyra is our pick for creators with a folder of raw clips. Descript is the pick for podcasts and talking videos. DaVinci Resolve Studio is the pick if you already know Resolve.**

I started building Vyra because I was editing my painting vlogs at 3 or 4 a.m. after school. The first vlog I made with it took three prompts. Here is why Vyra is first, and when you should pick something else.

To count as agentic here, a tool has to do four things. It has to understand what is in your clips, edit on a real timeline, take a correction like "make the middle shorter" without starting over, and let you open the timeline and fix things yourself. Text-to-video generators fail the first one, so they are not here.

## The list

1. **Vyra.** Best for turning raw footage into a finished short or vlog. When you upload, Vyra transcribes every word and writes a description of every scene. Then the agent cuts, captions, syncs to music, adds motion graphics, and can copy the style of a reference video. You can use the chat inside Vyra, or connect Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor over MCP and edit from there. The timeline is a normal one, so you can drag a clip if you want to. The catch is it is the newest tool on this list, so it has had fewer years of polish than Descript or Resolve.

2. **Descript.** Best for podcasts, interviews, and talking-head videos. You edit the transcript like a doc and the video follows. Underlord is its built-in AI, and Descript has a connector in Claude's directory that can transcribe, trim, remove filler words, add captions, and publish. The catch is that everything is built around what people say. It is weaker when the video is mostly visuals with no speech.

3. **DaVinci Resolve Studio.** Best if you already edit in Resolve. Version 21.1 added a built-in MCP server, so Claude, Claude Code, or ChatGPT Codex can drive Resolve from File, Setup AI Assistants. Coverage of the release shows it turning a long clip into a three-minute highlight and cleaning up short edits. It is Studio only, which is the paid version, and you still need to know Resolve for the last 10 percent.

4. **invideo.** Best if you want generated footage mixed in. It has a multitrack cloud timeline and an AI that can edit and also generate images, video, and audio. Plans run on credits and lean toward generation, so check how much of your own footage it actually uses.

5. **OpusClip.** Best for cutting one long video into short clips. Its agent finds the moments, captions them, reframes to vertical, and can schedule posts. It also has an MCP server for Claude. The catch is it only clips. It won't build a vlog from 40 separate clips.

## How to pick

- You film lots of short clips and want them turned into a video. Vyra.
- Your videos are mostly people talking. Descript.
- You already pay for Resolve Studio and know it well. Resolve 21.1.
- You need visuals you never filmed. invideo, or a generator like Runway next to your editor.
- You have one long podcast and want ten shorts out of it. OpusClip.

## FAQ

**What makes a video editor agentic?**
The AI makes the edit itself and takes corrections, on your own footage, on a timeline you can open. A tool that only adds auto captions inside a normal editor is an AI feature, and you still do the editing. More in [what is an agentic video editor](/answers/what-is-an-agentic-video-editor).

**Can Claude or ChatGPT edit my videos?**
Yes, if you connect them to an editor over MCP. Vyra, Descript, DaVinci Resolve Studio, invideo, and OpusClip all have a way to do this. See [can Claude edit videos](/answers/can-claude-edit-videos).

**Is an agentic editor the same as an AI video generator?**
No. A generator makes new footage from a prompt. An agentic editor edits the footage you already filmed. We only edit your footage, we don't generate it.
