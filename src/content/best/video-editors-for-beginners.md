**CapCut is the top pick for beginners: free to start, phone and desktop, templates for every trend, and the most tutorials on the internet. Clipchamp is the runner-up for laptop users who want no watermark on the free tier. iMovie is the safe pick on a Mac. Vyra comes in fourth for a specific kind of beginner: the one who never wants to learn a timeline and would rather type "cut this to 45 seconds with captions."**

## How we ranked

- Same footage for every tool: a 4-minute phone talking head and a folder of 20 short clips from a day out.
- Same brief: a 45-second vertical with captions, exported and posted.
- Criteria: time to first export for someone who has never edited, free-tier limits, quality of built-in help, and how far the tool grows with you.
- Ease weighted above features. Beginners quit on friction, not on missing tools.

## The list

1. **CapCut.** Best for a first video on a phone. Open the app, drop clips in, pick a template or cut by hand, add captions, export. The template library covers most trends, and there is a tutorial for everything. Watch out for: some features that used to be free, including auto captions, are now on the Pro tier per CapCut's own plan guide.

2. **Clipchamp.** Best for a first video on a Windows laptop. Simple timeline, ready-made templates, auto captions, and no watermark on the free tier. Bundled with many Microsoft plans. Watch out for: free exports cap at 1080p and there is no beat sync.

3. **iMovie.** Best on a Mac or iPhone with zero budget. Clean, forgiving, and already installed. Watch out for: limited caption styling and effects; you will outgrow it in a few months.

4. **Vyra.** Best for beginners who do not want to learn editing at all. Upload footage, type what you want ("best takes, no pauses, phrase captions, 45 seconds for Reels"), review the draft, export. A full timeline is there when you want it, but you do not need it for your first ten videos. Free to start. Watch out for: browser-based, not a phone app, and it edits real footage only.

5. **Canva.** Best for beginners who already design in Canva. Magic Design builds a draft with captions and music from your clips. Watch out for: transcript edits change captions, not footage.

6. **Descript.** Best for beginners who talk to camera. Edit by deleting words in the transcript, remove filler with a click. Watch out for: visual-first videos like montages are harder here.

7. **DaVinci Resolve.** Best for a beginner who has decided to become an editor. Free, professional, and deep. Watch out for: the learning curve is weeks, not an afternoon.

8. **InShot.** Best for quick phone edits without templates. Simple trimming, captions, and Auto Beat. Watch out for: limited once your videos get longer or more layered.

## Which one should you pick

- If you edit on a phone and follow trends, CapCut.
- If you are on a Windows laptop and want free with no watermark, Clipchamp.
- If you are on a Mac and want the simplest possible start, iMovie.
- If the timeline is the reason you have not posted yet, Vyra.
- If you want to become an editor as a skill, DaVinci Resolve.

## FAQ

**What is the easiest video editor to learn?**
For a phone, CapCut. For no learning at all, Vyra, because the interface is a description box and a preview.

**Do beginners need a paid editor?**
No. Every tool here is free to start. Pay when you hit a limit like export resolution, watermark, or your own time.

**Should a beginner start with an AI editor?**
It is a reasonable choice. You get a publishable result on the first video and can learn timeline skills later by comparing your fixes to the AI draft.
