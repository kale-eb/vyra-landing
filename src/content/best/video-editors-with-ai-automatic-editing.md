**Vyra is the top pick for automatic editing: you describe the result and an agent that has transcribed and visually analyzed your footage builds the cut, captions, reframing, music, and graphics, then keeps taking corrections. Descript's Underlord is the runner-up for talking-head and podcast content, where transcript-driven automation is very strong. The rest of the list automates one slice well: Riverside and InVideo for rough cuts, Adobe Firefly Quick Cut for a first draft, Gling for cleanup, Opus Clip for clipping.**

## How we ranked

- Same footage for every tool: a 6-minute talking head, a 12-minute vlog, and 40 montage clips.
- Same brief, given once: "45-second vertical, best moments, captions, music under speech."
- Criteria: how much of the brief the tool completed without manual work, whether it could take a follow-up correction, and whether it works on footage without speech.
- Scored on the first result plus one correction, since that is how real editing goes.

## The list

1. **Vyra.** Best for automatic editing across formats. One prompt produced a usable cut on all three sources, including the montage with no speech, because the agent sees scene descriptions, not just a transcript. Corrections are one sentence each. Built-in AI, or your own Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor over MCP with the real editing tools. Full timeline underneath. Watch out for: real footage only, browser-based.

2. **Descript (Underlord).** Best for speech-driven automation. Remove filler, cut retakes, tighten pauses, and ask Underlord for changes in plain language. MCP server so Claude or ChatGPT can run edits. Watch out for: the montage brief was the weak spot; automation leans on the transcript.

3. **Riverside (AI editing agent).** Best for recorded interviews and podcasts. Chat commands like "make this a 60-second clip" and "remove silences" work well on its own recordings. Watch out for: MCP is listed but its scope is not documented on the site; strongest inside its recording workflow.

4. **InVideo (AI agent).** Best for a structured rough cut from raw clips. Instructions like "keep one clean take per point and remove false starts" produce a timeline you then refine in its editor. Watch out for: 4K export needs a paid plan; its strength is the first draft, not iteration.

5. **Adobe Firefly Quick Cut.** Best for Adobe users who want a first draft. Give it clips, a text prompt, aspect ratio, pacing, and length and it returns an edited starting point (launched February 2026). Watch out for: Adobe positions it as a starting point; refinement happens in Premiere, and paid plans had generation caps at launch.

6. **Gling.** Best for automatic cleanup of talking heads. Silences, filler, bad takes, captions, auto zoom, then hand off to Final Cut, Resolve, or Premiere. Watch out for: cleanup only, no assembly or graphics.

7. **Opus Clip.** Best for automatic clipping of long videos. Finds highlights, reframes, captions, scores virality. MCP for Claude, Cursor, ChatGPT. Watch out for: not a general editor.

8. **DaVinci Resolve 21.1.** Best for pros who want an assistant inside a full NLE. The MCP assistant can organize media, change settings, cut highlights from long-form, and render. Watch out for: automation is a helper inside a manual tool, not the primary workflow.

## Which one should you pick

- If you want to describe the video and get it, on any format, Vyra.
- If your content is talking to a microphone, Descript.
- If you record interviews remotely, Riverside.
- If you live in Premiere, Firefly Quick Cut for the draft, Premiere for the rest.
- If you only want cleanup, Gling. If you only want clips, Opus Clip.

## FAQ

**What does "automatic editing" actually mean?**
The tool makes the cuts, captions, and often the music placement from a brief, instead of you doing it on a timeline. The good ones also take corrections in plain language.

**Can automatic editors handle footage with no speech?**
Only if they analyze the picture. Vyra describes every scene on upload, so it can pick moments in b-roll and montage clips. Transcript-first tools struggle here.

**Is the automatic result final?**
Treat it as a draft. One or two corrections usually get it there. Vyra, Descript, and Riverside are built for that loop; Quick Cut and InVideo expect you to finish by hand.
