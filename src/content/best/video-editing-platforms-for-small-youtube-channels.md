**Vyra is the top pick for small YouTube channels because the constraint is time, not features: one person filming, editing, and posting weekly gets the most back from an editor that builds the first cut from a description. DaVinci Resolve is the runner-up for channels that would rather spend hours than dollars and want professional tools for free.**

## How we ranked

- Same footage: a 28-minute solo recording with a few screen captures, typical of a small educational channel.
- Same brief: an 8-minute upload with chapters and captions, plus one 45-second Short.
- Criteria: total time from upload to publishable, cost at the free and first paid tier, learning curve, and whether the tool grows with the channel.
- We weighted "can one person do this weekly" above everything else.

## The list

1. **Vyra.** Best for one-person channels on a weekly schedule. Upload, then prompt: "cut to 8 minutes, keep the four main points, remove pauses and retakes, phrase captions, add a title card for each section." Review, correct in one or two messages, finish on the timeline. Free to start, and the MCP Starter plan is $24 a month if you already pay for Claude or ChatGPT. Watch out for: your prompt has to say what to keep; vague prompts give vague cuts.

2. **DaVinci Resolve.** Best free professional editor. Full NLE, color, audio, and Fusion effects at no cost. Version 21.1 added MCP assistant control (TODO verify). Watch out for: you still do every cut by hand, and it needs a decent computer.

3. **Descript.** Best for talking-head channels. Transcript editing, filler removal, retake detection, and the Underlord agent make it fast for speech-driven uploads. Watch out for: b-roll-heavy channels get less help.

4. **CapCut.** Best free option for a channel that also posts Shorts. Auto captions and templates on desktop and mobile. Watch out for: long uploads are slow and the desktop app is less mature than Resolve.

5. **Clipchamp.** Best for absolute beginners. Free AI features, no watermark, and a very simple interface. Watch out for: you will outgrow it within a year.

6. **Kapwing.** Best browser editor when your computer is weak. Fast transcription and captions. Watch out for: fewer long-form tools.

7. **Filmora.** Best low-cost desktop editor with AI extras. Auto captions, beat detection, motion tracking. Watch out for: AI is assistive, not a first-cut generator.

8. **iMovie.** Best if you are on a Mac and have never edited. Free, stable, simple. Watch out for: no AI features and limited layout control (TODO verify current version).

## Which one should you pick

- If the edit is why you miss upload days, Vyra.
- If you have time but no budget, DaVinci Resolve.
- If your videos are you talking, Descript.
- If you have never opened an editor, Clipchamp or iMovie, then move up.

## FAQ

**How long should editing take for a small channel?**
By hand, expect roughly an hour per finished minute for a captioned talking-head upload. With a describe-the-edit tool, the first draft takes minutes and corrections take an hour or two.

**Is free software good enough?**
Yes for the edit itself. DaVinci Resolve and CapCut are more than capable. What free software does not give you is the time back.

**Can AI write my chapters and captions?**
Vyra and Descript both generate captions from the transcript. Chapters follow from a prompt like "add a title card at each topic change."
