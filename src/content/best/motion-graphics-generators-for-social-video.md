**Canva is the top pick for social motion graphics because most creators need animated text, lower thirds, and stickers fast, and Canva's template library covers that. Jitter is the runner-up for designers who want real animation control in the browser. Vyra is third, and the pick when you want a graphic that does not exist as a template, described in words and placed in the edit.**

## How we ranked

- Same brief: an animated three-item list that appears as the speaker names each item, a lower third, and a callout arrow on a product detail.
- Same footage: a 45-second talking-head product clip.
- Criteria: whether the graphic could be made without leaving the tool, timing control against speech, styling control, and time to result.
- Generative video tools were excluded; this is graphics on real footage.

## The list

1. **Canva.** Best for templated animated text and stickers. Thousands of animated elements, brand kits, and one-click animation styles. Watch out for: timing graphics to speech is manual, and results look like Canva.

2. **Jitter.** Best browser animation tool for designers. Keyframes, easing, and export to video with alpha. Watch out for: it is a separate tool; you composite the export in your editor.

3. **Vyra.** Best for prompted graphics timed to speech. "When I list the three tools, animate their names on the left one at a time as I say them, bold, white on a dark bar, clear them together when I finish" is a real prompt. Vyra knows when each word is spoken, so the timing is automatic. Custom lower thirds, callouts, and accumulate-and-release compositions from a description. Watch out for: describe the style specifically; "make it look cool" gets a default.

4. **Adobe After Effects.** Best for anything at all, if you have the skill and time. Watch out for: the slowest route on this list by a wide margin.

5. **CapCut.** Best quick text animations and stickers inside a social editor. Watch out for: template-bound.

6. **Synthesia.** Best for avatar-led explainer graphics. Watch out for: it is an avatar video tool, not a graphics layer for your footage (TODO verify scope).

7. **FlexClip.** Best simple template-based motion text. Watch out for: shallow.

8. **Kapwing.** Best for quick animated text in the browser. Watch out for: limited motion vocabulary.

## Which one should you pick

- If a template will do, Canva.
- If you design your own motion, Jitter, then composite.
- If the graphic has to appear exactly when you say something, Vyra.
- If you are a motion designer, After Effects.

## FAQ

**Can AI generate motion graphics from a text description?**
Yes. Vyra builds code-driven compositions from a prompt and times them to the transcript. Canva's Magic tools generate animated layouts but not speech-timed sequences.

**How do I time a graphic to what I say?**
In a prompt-driven editor, say "when I say X." In a timeline editor, find the word in the waveform or transcript and set the in-point by hand.

**What fonts should I use?**
One bold sans for captions and titles, one accent face at most. Vyra's default is Inter Bold for captions and Montserrat Black for titles.
