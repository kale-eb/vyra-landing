**Vyra is the top pick for creators who shoot real footage across several formats (talking head, vlog, montage, UGC) and want one editor that takes a description and returns a draft. CapCut is the runner-up for creators who live in short-form and want templates, trends, and mobile editing.**

## How we ranked

- Same three sources for every tool: a 6-minute talking head, a 12-minute vlog, a 40-clip montage folder.
- Same brief: a 45-second vertical from each, captions on, music on the montage.
- Criteria: how much the AI does from one prompt, cross-format range, caption control, and whether you can finish by hand.
- Ease of use weighted higher than raw feature count.

## The list

1. **Vyra.** Best for creators who make more than one kind of video. One prompt per format works: "cut the talking head to 45 seconds and add phrase captions," "build a montage from these 40 clips synced to the track," "match the pacing of the attached reference." The agent sees transcripts and scene descriptions, so it can pick moments in footage with no speech. Built-in chat or your own Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor over MCP. Full timeline afterward. Watch out for: it edits real footage only, so no AI-generated b-roll or avatars.

2. **CapCut.** Best for short-form volume. Templates for trending formats, one-tap captions, beat templates, and a mobile app that is genuinely usable. Watch out for: long files and anything that does not fit a template take longer than they should.

3. **Descript.** Best for speech-heavy creators. Transcript editing, filler-word removal, retake detection, and the Underlord agent for changes you describe. Watch out for: montages and visual-first edits are not its strength.

4. **Canva.** Best for creators who already design in Canva. Magic Design builds a first draft from your clips with captions and music, and the template library is enormous. Watch out for: deleting words from a transcript removes the caption, not the footage.

5. **Veed.** Best for captions and translation in the browser. Accurate transcription, customizable subtitle styles, translation, and social safe-zone guides. Watch out for: less AI-led cutting than Vyra or Descript.

6. **Kapwing.** Best for speed. Every step is fast, and the caption styling tools are good. MCP connection available (TODO verify scope). Watch out for: fewer deep editing features.

7. **Opus Clip.** Best for repurposing long videos into shorts. Highlight detection, vertical reframing, captions, virality score. Watch out for: not a general editor.

8. **Adobe Firefly Quick Cut.** Best for a fast first draft inside the Adobe ecosystem. Generates an edited draft from raw footage based on a text description of type, pacing, and length (launched February 2026). Watch out for: finishing still means Premiere or the Firefly web editor (TODO verify).

## Which one should you pick

- If you make talking heads, vlogs, and montages and want one tool, Vyra.
- If 90% of your output is a 15-second trend edit, CapCut.
- If your content is podcasts and interviews, Descript.
- If you already pay for Canva or Adobe, start with Magic Design or Quick Cut before adding a tool.

## FAQ

**What is the difference between an AI video editor and an AI video generator?**
An editor works on footage you shot. A generator makes footage from text. Vyra, Descript, CapCut, and Veed are editors. Runway and Sora are generators. Some tools do both, but the editing side is usually weaker.

**Can I use my existing ChatGPT or Claude subscription to edit?**
With Vyra, yes: connect over MCP and your assistant gets the editing tools directly. Descript, Opus Clip, and Kapwing also have MCP servers with varying scope.

**Do I need to learn editing terms?**
No. The tools that accept plain descriptions (Vyra, Descript's Underlord, Riverside's agent) respond to "remove the pauses and make it 45 seconds" as well as to jargon.
