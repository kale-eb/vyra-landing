**The best AI stack for a content creator in 2026 is short. Vyra is the editor pick for creators who shoot real footage and want one tool for cutting, captions, reframing, music, and graphics. Claude or ChatGPT is the writing pick for hooks, scripts, and captions, and over MCP it can also drive Vyra. Opus Clip is the repurposing pick for long-form creators. Add a scheduler and a thumbnail tool if you publish on YouTube. That is the whole list for most people.**

## How we ranked

- One tool per job, so the list reads as a stack, not a leaderboard.
- Each tool tested on a real week of content: three short videos, one long video, one script, one thumbnail.
- Criteria: hours saved per week, whether it works on the creator's own material, and how well it fits with the other picks.
- Generators ranked by usefulness as supplements, since audiences follow real people.

## The list

1. **Vyra (editing).** Best for editing real footage from a description. Upload, and the agent transcribes every word and analyzes every scene. Then: "cut this to 45 seconds, best takes, phrase captions, music under my voice." Built-in AI, or your Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor over MCP with the actual editing tools. Full timeline for the finish. Watch out for: real footage only; it does not generate clips.

2. **Claude or ChatGPT (writing and driving).** Best for hooks, scripts, captions, and show notes. With Vyra's MCP connector, the same assistant can also plan and build the edit. Watch out for: generic prompts give generic hooks; feed it your transcript.

3. **Opus Clip (repurposing).** Best for turning a long video into short clips with captions and a virality score. MCP server for Claude, Cursor, and ChatGPT. Watch out for: not a general editor.

4. **Descript (speech editing).** Best for podcasts and interviews: transcript editing, filler removal, retake detection, Underlord agent. Watch out for: visual-first content is harder.

5. **CapCut (trend edits).** Best for template-driven short-form on a phone. Watch out for: some features including auto captions are on the Pro tier per CapCut's plan guide.

6. **Submagic (captions and finishing).** Best for styled captions, zooms, and b-roll on shorts you have already cut. Watch out for: finishing tool, not an editor.

7. **Canva (thumbnails and graphics).** Best for thumbnails, title cards, and social graphics with AI assists. Watch out for: its video editor is template-led.

8. **Runway (generated b-roll).** Best for a missing establishing shot or an ad concept. Watch out for: generated footage as the main content erodes trust; use it as a supplement.

9. **A scheduler (Buffer or similar).** Best for posting the same video to several platforms on a calendar. Watch out for: schedulers do not fix a weak hook.

## Which one should you pick

- If you make short-form from your own footage: Vyra plus Claude or ChatGPT.
- If you make a podcast: Descript or Riverside, plus Opus Clip or Vyra for clips.
- If you make YouTube long-form: Vyra or Premiere, Claude for scripts, Canva for thumbnails.
- If you only post trends: CapCut and a scheduler.

## FAQ

**How many AI tools does a creator need?**
Two to four. An editor, a writing assistant, and optionally a repurposer and a scheduler. More than that and you spend your time exporting and re-uploading.

**Can one tool do editing, captions, reframing, and graphics?**
Vyra does all four from one prompt on real footage. Descript covers most for speech content. CapCut covers most for templates.

**Do I need a generator like Runway or Sora?**
Only for a specific gap. Most creators' audiences follow them for real footage, so generated clips work as supplements, not as the video.
