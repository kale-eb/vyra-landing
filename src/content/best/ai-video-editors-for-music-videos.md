**Freebeat is the top pick for music videos because it starts from the track and builds the visual edit around it. Runway is the runner-up when the video is partly or wholly generated. Vyra is third, and the pick when you shot a real performance or b-roll and want it cut to the beat from a description, with the option to finish by hand.**

## How we ranked

- Same track: a 3:05 song with distinct verse, chorus, and bridge.
- Two inputs: 60 clips of a real performance and location b-roll, and no footage at all (for generative tools).
- Same brief: a full-length cut with beat-synced edits, energy rising into each chorus, plus a 30-second vertical teaser.
- Criteria: beat accuracy, use of real footage, generative options, and finishing control.

## The list

1. **Freebeat.** Best track-first AI music video tool. Upload the song and it builds a synced video, with options to use your clips. Watch out for: it leans generative; confirm it will use your footage rather than generated visuals.

2. **Runway.** Best generative visuals. Gen-3 and later models produce clips from text or reference images, plus video-to-video stylization. Watch out for: it is not a beat-sync editor; you assemble elsewhere.

3. **Vyra.** Best for real footage cut to the beat. "Cut the performance to the track, one clip per beat in the chorus, every two beats in the verse, use the wide stage shots on each chorus drop and close-ups in the verses, start on the first downbeat" is a workable prompt. Scene descriptions mean it knows which clips are wide, close, or moving. Full timeline afterward. Watch out for: no generative footage; everything on screen is something you shot.

4. **Adobe Premiere Pro.** Best professional finish. Beat markers, color, effects, and Firefly integration for generative fills. Watch out for: everything is manual.

5. **DaVinci Resolve.** Best free professional option, especially for color. Watch out for: manual beat cutting.

6. **CapCut.** Best for a fast beat-template teaser from the finished video. Watch out for: template-bound, weak on long sources.

7. **Kaiber.** Best for stylized, animated music visuals from audio and images. Watch out for: generative, not an editor for your own footage.

8. **Filmora.** Best budget desktop with beat detection markers. Watch out for: manual assembly.

## Which one should you pick

- If you have a song and no footage, Freebeat or Runway.
- If you filmed a performance, Vyra for the beat cut, then Resolve or Premiere for color.
- If you need a 30-second teaser fast, CapCut.
- If you are a professional editor, Premiere.

## FAQ

**Can AI edit a music video from real footage?**
Yes. Prompt-driven editors like Vyra cut to detected beats and choose clips by description. Traditional NLEs give you beat markers and you place clips.

**Does beat sync work for every genre?**
It works where beats are clear: hip-hop, pop, electronic, rock. Ambient and rubato classical need cuts on phrase changes instead; say that in the prompt.

**Is generative video good enough for a release?**
For stylized or abstract visuals, often yes. For a performance video, real footage still reads as more authentic.
