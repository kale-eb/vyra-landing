**CapCut is the top pick for beat sync because its beat templates get a montage timed to a trending sound in under a minute. Filmora is the runner-up for editors who want beat markers on a real desktop timeline. Vyra is third, and the pick when you want to say how the sync should behave ("one clip per beat in the chorus, every two beats in the verse") instead of accepting a template.**

## How we ranked

- Same footage: 40 clips from a night walk through a city, no speech.
- Same track: a 2:10 song with a clear verse and chorus.
- Same brief: a 30-second vertical montage where cuts land on beats and the energy rises into the chorus.
- Criteria: accuracy of beat placement, control over which beats get a cut, how the tool chooses clips, and time to result.

## The list

1. **CapCut.** Best for speed with trending audio. Pick a beat template, drop clips in, and the cuts land on the beats. Auto beat marking on the timeline too. Watch out for: the template decides the rhythm; changing it means manual work.

2. **Filmora.** Best desktop beat detection. Add a track and Filmora marks every beat on the timeline; you snap clips to the markers. Watch out for: clip selection is on you.

3. **Vyra.** Best for described sync. "Cut to the beat, one clip per beat in the chorus, one every two beats in the verses, start on the first downbeat, hold the last shot for a second" is a prompt Vyra executes, and because it describes every scene it also picks the clips ("favor movement and faces"). Watch out for: it is not a one-tap template; you write two sentences.

4. **Freebeat.** Best AI music-video generator that builds edits around a track. Watch out for: leans generative; confirm it will use your footage rather than generated visuals.

5. **Adobe Premiere Pro.** Best for pros who want beat markers plus full control (via the Remix and marker tools). Watch out for: manual assembly.

6. **DaVinci Resolve.** Best free option; audio waveform and markers make manual beat cutting precise. Watch out for: beat cutting is manual with markers; there is no one-click beat sync.

7. **InVideo.** Best for template montages with music built in. Watch out for: template-bound.

8. **Canva.** Best for quick music-timed slideshows from photos and clips. Watch out for: shallow control.

## Which one should you pick

- If the sound is trending and speed matters, CapCut.
- If you want markers on a timeline and to place clips yourself, Filmora or Resolve.
- If you want to describe the rhythm and let the AI pick the clips, Vyra.
- If you want a music video generated from a track, Freebeat.

## FAQ

**Does beat sync work for slow songs?**
Yes, but cut on every other beat or on phrase changes. Tell a prompt-driven editor "cut every two beats" or "cut on the chord changes."

**Can the AI pick which clip goes on which beat?**
Vyra does, using scene descriptions ("put the widest skyline shot on the chorus drop"). Templates place clips in the order you add them.

**What BPM range works for montages?**
Roughly 90 to 130 BPM for one cut per beat. Faster tracks feel frantic with one cut per beat; halve the cut rate.
