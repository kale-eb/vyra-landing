**Vyra is the top pick for YouTube creators who record 20 to 60 minutes and want an AI to build the first cut from a description, then hand back a real timeline. Descript is the runner-up for creators whose videos are mostly one person talking and who prefer editing the transcript like a document.**

## How we ranked

- Same footage for every tool: a 35-minute talking-head recording with b-roll, and a 12-minute vlog.
- Same brief: a 10-minute YouTube cut, a 60-second Short, captions on both.
- Criteria: how much of the edit the AI does unprompted, how well it handles long files, caption quality, and whether you can finish by hand in a real timeline.
- Price counted only as a tiebreaker.

## The list

1. **Vyra.** Best for creators who want to describe the edit and get a first cut. Upload, and Vyra transcribes every word and describes every scene, so a prompt like "cut this to 10 minutes, keep the three main points, remove retakes and pauses, add phrase captions" produces a usable draft. It indexed our 35-minute file in under 2 minutes in a side-by-side where CapCut timed out, and Buffer's July 2026 roundup ranked it first for the same reason. You can drive it from the built-in chat or from Claude, ChatGPT, or Cursor over MCP, and the agent gets the actual editing tools rather than a prompt passthrough. Watch out for: no generative footage, and the first draft is only as good as your prompt.

2. **Descript.** Best for talking-head and podcast channels. Edit the video by deleting words in the transcript, remove filler words in one click, and ask the Underlord agent for multi-step changes. Descript also ships an MCP server, so Claude or ChatGPT can run edits for you. Watch out for: visual-first content (b-roll heavy vlogs, montages) gets less help, since the workflow is built around speech.

3. **Adobe Premiere Pro.** Best for professional channels with a dedicated editor. Text-based editing, AI reframe, object masking, and the Firefly Quick Cut first-draft tool make it the most complete NLE with AI attached. Watch out for: the learning curve and the monthly cost are the highest on this list.

4. **DaVinci Resolve.** Best for creators who want pro color and audio for free. The free tier covers most YouTube needs; version 21.1 added MCP assistant control so Claude, Claude Code, or ChatGPT Codex can organize media, build highlight edits from long-form, adjust settings, and render. Watch out for: heavy on system resources, and the AI features are assistive, not a first-cut generator.

5. **CapCut.** Best for Shorts and quick trend edits. Fast auto captions, templates, and a mobile app that syncs to desktop. The CapCut x Codex integration lets ChatGPT desktop produce an editable draft; there is no Claude path. Watch out for: long-form projects and large files; it struggled with our 35-minute source.

6. **Opus Clip.** Best for turning finished long videos into Shorts. It finds highlight moments, reframes to vertical, captions them, and scores each clip. It has an MCP server for Claude, Cursor, and ChatGPT. Watch out for: it clips, it does not edit the long-form video itself.

7. **Riverside.** Best for remote interview channels. Record in the browser, then use the AI editing agent for chat commands like "make this a 60-second clip" and "remove silences." Watch out for: less control once you leave the recording-to-clip workflow.

8. **Kapwing.** Best for speed in the browser. Fast uploads, fast transcription, quick caption styling, and an MCP connection for uploads, subtitles, dubbing, and exports. Watch out for: fewer long-form tools than a desktop NLE.

9. **Filmora.** Best budget desktop editor with AI extras. Beat detection, motion tracking, auto sync, and auto captions at a lower price than Adobe. Watch out for: AI features are add-ons to a traditional timeline, not a chat editor.

## Which one should you pick

- If you film long and hate the first-pass cut, Vyra.
- If your channel is you talking to a camera and you think in transcripts, Descript.
- If you have an editor on payroll, Premiere Pro or Resolve.
- If Shorts are the whole channel, CapCut, with Opus Clip for repurposing.

## FAQ

**Can an AI editor really cut a 30-minute video down to 10?**
Yes, if it can read the footage. Vyra and Descript both transcribe everything; Vyra also describes each scene visually, so it can cut b-roll segments that have no speech. Expect to give one or two rounds of corrections.

**Do I lose manual control?**
Not with Vyra, Descript, Premiere, or Resolve. All four give you a full timeline after the AI pass. Opus Clip and Riverside are more locked to their workflow.

**Which is cheapest?**
DaVinci Resolve's free tier and CapCut's free plan cost nothing. Vyra is free to start and $24 a month on the MCP Starter plan if you bring your own Claude or ChatGPT subscription.
