**Vyra is the top pick for artists and painters: it turns a two-hour studio recording into a 45-second process video from one description, handles the speed ramps for timelapses, and cuts a reveal to music without you scrubbing the footage. CapCut is the runner-up for quick phone edits of studio clips with templates. VN and Filmora cover the middle, and DaVinci Resolve is the pick for artists who want cinematic color on their work.**

## How we ranked

- Same footage for every tool: a 90-minute overhead painting session, 15 phone clips of a studio day, and a 20-second reveal pan of a finished canvas.
- Same brief: a 45-second vertical process video with a reveal at the end, and a 15-second timelapse.
- Criteria: how much scrubbing the tool removed, speed-ramp and timelapse control, music sync, and whether the colors of the artwork survived export.
- Ranked by time from footage to post, since artists would rather be painting.

## The list

1. **Vyra.** Best for process videos, timelapses, and reveals from a description. "Cut the 90-minute session to 45 seconds: the first marks, three moments where the composition changes, and the final reveal. Speed up the in-between, hold the reveal for 3 seconds, sync to the track." The agent has already described every scene, so it finds the composition changes without you logging timestamps. Time remapping handles the speed ramps; masks handle reframing the overhead shot to vertical. Watch out for: real footage only, browser-based.

2. **CapCut.** Best for a fast phone edit of studio clips. Speed controls, templates for art reveals, and beat templates for montages. Watch out for: long overhead recordings are slow to handle on a phone, and auto captions are on the Pro tier per CapCut's plan guide.

3. **VN Video Editor.** Best free phone editor for timelapse control. Curve speed, keyframes, and clean exports. Watch out for: no AI assembly; you still choose every moment.

4. **Filmora.** Best desktop editor for beat-synced art montages. Auto Beat Sync, motion tracking for callouts on the canvas, 4K export on free and paid. Watch out for: manual assembly, and the AI tools are on paid plans.

5. **DaVinci Resolve.** Best for color accuracy on artwork. Free, with professional color tools that keep pigments honest through export. Watch out for: weeks to learn, and no automatic assembly outside the 21.1 MCP assistant.

6. **InShot.** Best for a 15-second reveal from a phone in two minutes. Trim, Auto Beat, captions. Watch out for: limited for longer process videos.

7. **Descript.** Best if your art videos are mostly you talking about the work. Transcript editing and filler removal. Watch out for: silent process footage is not its strength.

## Which one should you pick

- If you record long sessions and want the video without scrubbing, Vyra.
- If you shoot a few clips on your phone and post the same day, CapCut or InShot.
- If you want precise speed ramps for free, VN.
- If your work is color-critical and you will learn a pro tool, DaVinci Resolve.

## FAQ

**How do I turn a long painting session into a short video?**
Upload it to Vyra and describe the beats: first marks, key changes, reveal. It picks the moments from its scene analysis, speeds up the rest, and syncs to your track. See /formats/process-video and /formats/art-timelapse.

**What is the best way to film a painting reveal?**
Slow pan or tilt across the finished piece in good, even light, 10 to 20 seconds, then a wide shot. Cut it to the last section of the track. See /formats/commission-reveal.

**Will my colors look right after export?**
Film in consistent light, avoid mixed color temperatures, and export at high quality. Vyra exports up to 1440p on Starter and 4K on Pro; DaVinci Resolve gives the most control if color is critical.
