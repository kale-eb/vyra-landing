# Glossary drafts (/glossary/{term})

Each entry: one quotable definition, one example, one "In Vyra" line. Schema: DefinedTerm + BreadcrumbList.

## hook
A hook is the first one to three seconds of a video, designed to stop a viewer from scrolling by promising a payoff, posing a question, or showing something surprising.
Example: "I quit my job with $400 in the bank" is a hook; "hey guys, welcome back" is not.
In Vyra: say "open on the line where I say X" or "start on the reveal shot" and the agent places it first.

## retention
Retention is the percentage of viewers still watching at each point in a video; platforms use it to decide how widely to distribute the video.
Example: a 30-second video where half the audience leaves by second 5 has a retention problem at the hook, not the ending.
In Vyra: ask for tighter pauses, an earlier hook, or a mid-video re-hook to address specific drop points.

## b-roll
B-roll is supplementary footage cut over or between primary footage to show what is being talked about or to hide an edit.
Example: a shot of the brushes while the speaker says "I use three brushes."
In Vyra: "when I mention the brushes, cut to the brush footage for 2-3 seconds, then back to me."

## a-roll
A-roll is the primary footage, usually the person talking to camera or the main action, that carries the story.
Example: the interview is a-roll; the office shots over it are b-roll.
In Vyra: the agent identifies talking-to-camera clips as the spine and treats other clips as coverage when you ask for a cut.

## jump cut
A jump cut is an edit between two shots of the same subject from the same angle, which makes the subject appear to jump.
Example: cutting out a pause in a talking head produces a jump cut.
In Vyra: "punch in 10 percent on the second clip instead of a jump cut" smooths the transition.

## j-cut
A J-cut is an edit where the audio of the next shot starts before its picture appears.
Example: you hear the street before you see the street.
In Vyra: "let the next clip's sound start half a second early" or describe the effect in plain words.

## l-cut
An L-cut is an edit where the audio of the current shot continues after its picture has changed.
Example: the speaker keeps talking while the video shows the product.
In Vyra: "keep my voice going while you show the product shot" produces an L-cut.

## punch-in
A punch-in is a cut to a slightly tighter framing of the same shot, used for emphasis or to hide a jump cut.
Example: zooming from a medium shot to a close-up on the same take.
In Vyra: "punch in 10 percent on the key line" or "use punch-ins instead of jump cuts."

## beat sync
Beat sync is cutting or animating on the beats of a music track so the edit feels locked to the sound.
Example: one clip per beat in the chorus, one every two beats in the verse.
In Vyra: upload the track and say "cut on the beats, start on the first downbeat."

## lower third
A lower third is a text graphic in the bottom third of the frame that names a speaker, place, or topic.
Example: "Sulan Zhang, @sulansart" appearing for the first four seconds.
In Vyra: "add a lower third with my name and handle for the first 4 seconds, slide in from the left."

## kinetic typography
Kinetic typography is text that moves, scales, or appears in time with speech or music to add emphasis.
Example: the word "never" popping up larger as it is spoken.
In Vyra: "animate the three tool names on screen one at a time as I say them, then clear them together."

## motion graphics
Motion graphics are animated graphic elements such as titles, callouts, charts, and shapes, layered over or between footage.
Example: an animated arrow pointing at a button in a screen recording.
In Vyra: the agent builds motion graphics from a description or a reference; they are not templates.

## color grade
A color grade is the deliberate adjustment of color and contrast across a video to create a consistent look.
Example: warm shadows and muted highlights for a calm vlog.
In Vyra: "grade this warmer and slightly desaturated, consistent across all clips."

## LUT
A LUT (lookup table) is a preset color transformation applied to footage to achieve a specific look quickly.
Example: a "teal and orange" LUT applied to every clip.
In Vyra: describe the look you want; the agent applies matching color effects without requiring a LUT file.

## aspect ratio
Aspect ratio is the width-to-height proportion of a video frame, such as 16:9 for YouTube or 9:16 for TikTok.
Example: a 1920x1080 video is 16:9; 1080x1920 is 9:16.
In Vyra: "make a 9:16 version that keeps me centered" reframes with a mask.

## safe zone
The safe zone is the part of the frame that stays visible and unobstructed by platform interface elements like buttons and captions.
Example: on TikTok, the bottom 20 percent and right edge are covered by UI.
In Vyra: "keep captions above the bottom 20 percent and away from the right edge."

## 9:16
9:16 is the vertical aspect ratio used by TikTok, Reels, and Shorts, typically 1080 by 1920 pixels.
Example: a phone held upright records 9:16.
In Vyra: "export a 9:16 version at 1080x1920 for TikTok."

## ducking
Ducking is lowering music volume automatically whenever speech is present so the voice stays clear.
Example: music at 25 percent while talking, 70 percent during b-roll.
In Vyra: "duck the music to about 20 percent whenever I am speaking."

## room tone
Room tone is the ambient sound of a recording space with no one speaking, used to fill gaps so cuts do not go dead silent.
Example: the faint hum of a studio behind an interview.
In Vyra: "keep the original ambience under the cut" preserves room tone through edits.

## talking head
A talking head is a video format where a person speaks directly to camera, usually framed from the chest up.
Example: a founder explaining a feature from their desk.
In Vyra: "clean up this talking head: remove pauses over 0.4 seconds, filler words, and retakes."

## UGC
UGC (user-generated content) is video that looks like it was made by a regular customer rather than a brand, often used in ads.
Example: a phone-shot "I tried this for a week" review.
In Vyra: cut a UGC-style ad with "open on the hook line, show the product in use, end on the result."

## POV video
A POV video is shot or framed so the viewer feels they are seeing from the creator's point of view, often with a text setup like "POV: you are the new intern."
Example: a phone camera at eye level walking into a room with the text on screen.
In Vyra: "add the POV text at the top for the first 3 seconds, then remove it."

## CTA
A CTA (call to action) is the line at the end of a video that tells the viewer what to do next: follow, comment, click.
Example: "comment 'guide' and I will send it to you."
In Vyra: "end on a 2-second title card that says 'comment GUIDE for the link'."

## pattern interrupt
A pattern interrupt is a sudden change in visuals, sound, or pacing meant to reset attention and keep viewers watching.
Example: a cut to black, a zoom, a sound effect, or a change of location.
In Vyra: "add a pattern interrupt around the 8-second mark: cut to the wide shot with a sound hit."

## open loop
An open loop is a question or promise raised early in a video and resolved later, so viewers stay to see the answer.
Example: "the third mistake cost me a client, more on that in a second."
In Vyra: "keep the line where I tease the third mistake near the top and the payoff at the end."

## cold open
A cold open starts a video in the middle of action or a strong moment before any intro or greeting.
Example: opening on the canvas being torn instead of "hey everyone."
In Vyra: "start on the reveal shot, no intro, then go back to the setup."

## montage
A montage is a sequence of short clips cut together, usually to music, to compress time or build energy.
Example: a 30-second recap of a trip cut on the beat.
In Vyra: "build a 30-second montage from the strongest 10 clips, fast to slow to fast, synced to the track."

## timelapse
A timelapse is video captured at a low frame rate and played back at normal speed so slow processes appear fast.
Example: a painting completed in 20 seconds of screen time.
In Vyra: speed up a clip with time remapping: "play the painting clip at 20x."

## hyperlapse
A hyperlapse is a timelapse where the camera moves through space between frames, producing a fast, traveling shot.
Example: walking through a city with the shot compressed to a few seconds.
In Vyra: "speed the walking clip up 8x and keep it stabilized-looking by trimming the shaky start."

## MCP (Model Context Protocol)
MCP (Model Context Protocol) is an open standard that lets AI assistants connect to external tools and data; Vyra uses it so Claude, ChatGPT, and Cursor can control the editor.
Example: Claude Desktop connected to Vyra can list your footage and cut a video.
In Vyra: connect your assistant once, then edit from its chat.

## AI video editor
An AI video editor is editing software that performs cuts, captions, and effects from natural-language instructions rather than manual timeline work.
Example: "remove the pauses and add captions" instead of dragging clips.
In Vyra: the editor understands your real footage and edits it; it does not generate synthetic video.

## conversational editing
Conversational editing is editing video through a back-and-forth chat where each message adjusts the cut.
Example: "tighter," then "swap the intro clip," then "bigger captions."
In Vyra: one correction per message keeps results accurate.

## reference video
A reference video is an example video whose style, pacing, or layout you want your own edit to match.
Example: attaching a trend video and asking for the same cut timing.
In Vyra: attach it, then say what to copy: "match its cut rhythm and caption placement."

## style matching
Style matching is replicating the pacing, text treatment, color, and music behavior of a reference video on different footage.
Example: your studio footage cut like a creator's vlog you admire.
In Vyra: "use the reference as the style guide; do not match its content or length."

## scene detection
Scene detection is automatic identification of where one shot ends and another begins, plus what each shot contains.
Example: an hour of footage split into 200 labeled clips.
In Vyra: every upload is scene-analyzed, so "find every shot of the canvas" works.

## transcription
Transcription is converting spoken audio into timestamped text.
Example: a 40-minute recording becomes searchable text with word timings.
In Vyra: speech is transcribed on upload, which powers search, cutting by phrase, and captions.

## word-level captions
Word-level captions display each word on screen at the moment it is spoken, rather than a whole sentence at once.
Example: TikTok-style captions that pop one word at a time.
In Vyra: "caption the whole video word by word, bold white, slightly above center."

## rough cut
A rough cut is the first full assembly of a video with the right structure and takes, before captions, music, and polish.
Example: a 60-second cut with the best takes in order and nothing else.
In Vyra: "make a rough cut, no captions or music yet, straight cuts only."

## fine cut
A fine cut is the refined version of a rough cut with pacing, transitions, and trims adjusted, close to final.
Example: pauses tightened, b-roll placed, hook moved to the top.
In Vyra: iterate on the rough cut one note per message until it is a fine cut.

## picture lock
Picture lock is the point where all visual edits are final and only sound, captions, and color remain.
Example: no more clip changes after this; captions and music are added on top.
In Vyra: lock the cut before asking for captions and music so they do not need redoing.
