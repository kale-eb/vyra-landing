# How to record clean audio for video on a phone

**Clean audio is mostly about distance and room. A phone mic within 30 cm of your mouth in a room with curtains, a rug, and furniture will beat an expensive mic across a bare kitchen. When you cannot stay close, use a clip-on lavalier or a wireless mic. Outdoors, a foam or furry windscreen is mandatory. Viewers tolerate soft video and abandon bad audio.**

## Why it matters

Audio is the half of the video people judge first and forgive last. Echo, hiss, and wind make a video feel cheap regardless of how it looks. Fixing audio in the edit is possible but always sounds worse than recording it right.

## The method

1. **Get close.** Phone built-in mic: 20 to 30 cm from your mouth. That usually means holding the phone at arm's length or closer, not on a tripod 1.5 m away.
2. **Choose the room.** Soft surfaces absorb reflections. Bedroom with curtains beats kitchen with tile. A closet full of clothes is a free vocal booth.
3. **Pick the mic for the situation.** Built-in mic for close talking moments. Lavalier for sit-down talking heads on a tripod. Wireless lav for walking, cooking, and demos. Shotgun mic only if you have someone to point it.
4. **Kill background noise before recording.** Fridge, fan, AC, open window. Turn it off, then turn it back on after.
5. **Shield from wind.** A furry windscreen on any external mic outdoors. For the built-in mic, turn your back to the wind and cup a hand loosely around the bottom of the phone.
6. **Check levels and record a test.** Speak your first line, play it back with headphones, then record. Peaks around minus 12 dB, never touching zero.
7. **Sync in the edit if you used a separate recorder.** Clap once at the start on camera. Vyra can align a separate audio track to the camera audio.

## Rules of thumb

| Situation | Mic | Distance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Talking head, phone in hand | built-in | 20 to 30 cm |
| Talking head on tripod | wired lav | clipped at sternum, 15 to 20 cm from mouth |
| Walking vlog | wireless lav | same clip position |
| Cooking, demo, hands busy | wireless lav | same |
| Two people | two wireless lavs | each |
| Outdoors, any mic | add windscreen | same |

Levels: target peaks between minus 12 and minus 6 dB. Room tone: record 10 seconds of silence in the room for noise reduction later.

## Examples

Studio talking head: wired lav clipped to the collar, phone on a tripod 80 cm away, fan off, curtains closed, 10 seconds of room tone recorded first.

Walking vlog through a market: wireless lav under a jacket lapel with a furry windscreen, phone held at chest height, talking in 8 second bursts.

Kitchen tutorial: wireless lav on an apron strap, extractor fan off, phone on a shelf 1.2 m away for the wide shot.

## Do it in Vyra

- "Sync the separate lav recording to the camera audio using the clap at the start, then mute the camera audio."
- "Reduce the room echo and background hum on my voice track, then normalize speech to around minus 14 LUFS. Do not touch the music."
- "Duck the music 8 dB whenever I speak and bring it back between sentences."

## Common mistakes

- Phone on a tripod across the room using the built-in mic. Distance is the enemy.
- Recording in a kitchen or bathroom. Hard surfaces echo.
- No windscreen outdoors. Wind noise is not fixable.
- Forgetting to turn the fridge and fan back off before take two.
- Levels too hot. Clipped audio is permanently distorted.

## FAQ

**Are AirPods good enough?**
For a quick talking head in a quiet room, they are acceptable. They compress the voice and pick up rustle. A wired lav costs less and sounds better.

**Wireless mic brands?**
Any current two-channel wireless lav kit that plugs directly into the phone works. Pick one with a furry windscreen included.

**Can AI fix bad audio?**
Noise reduction and de-reverb help with mild problems. They cannot fix wind, clipping, or a mic two meters away without making the voice sound processed.
