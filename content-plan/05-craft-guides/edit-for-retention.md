# How to edit a short-form video for retention

**Retention editing targets the three places viewers leave: the first 3 seconds, the slow middle, and the moment they sense the ending. Win the opening with a hook and an early cut, place a re-hook (a new promise, a question, a visual reset) every 8 to 12 seconds, deliver the first piece of real value before the midpoint, and end on the last useful frame. No outro, no fade, no "thanks for watching."**

## Why it matters

Average watch time and completion rate are the signals short-form platforms weight most for distribution. The same content with the same hook can double its reach on the strength of the middle and the ending alone. Retention is an editing problem more than a content problem.

## The method

1. **Cut the first 3 seconds last.** Once the whole video exists, rebuild the opening with the strongest line, a visual change inside 1.5 seconds, and a text hook.
2. **Deliver something before the midpoint.** The viewer stayed for a promise. Pay part of it early so the rest feels earned, not withheld.
3. **Place re-hooks on the dip points.** Every 8 to 12 seconds, do one of: preview the next point, ask a question, cut to a new location, or drop an on-screen word. Rotate types.
4. **Remove every "and then" transition.** Phrases like "so anyway," "moving on," and "the next thing" are exit doors. Cut straight to the next point.
5. **Tighten the last 20 percent.** Shorten shots, remove any summary, end on the final useful sentence or image.
6. **Cut on the last word.** The video should stop the frame after the last word lands. Looping formats can end on a frame that matches the opening.
7. **Check the retention graph and re-cut.** After 48 hours, find the dips. Add an interrupt 1 to 2 seconds before each one in the next video.

## Rules of thumb

| Point in video | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 1.5 s | hook line, text hook, first cut |
| by 3 s | second visual change |
| by 40 percent | first real payoff delivered |
| every 8 to 12 s | re-hook |
| last 20 percent | shot length decreasing |
| last frame | last useful word or image, hard cut |

Never: fade to black, "like and follow" as the closing line, a title card at the end, more than 1 second of silence anywhere.

## Examples

Re-hook types in one 45 second tutorial:
- 9 s: "and the second one is the one everyone gets wrong"
- 19 s: cut to a new angle plus the on-screen word "WRONG"
- 30 s: "this last one saved me an hour a week"
- 42 s: last tip, cut on the word "done"

Ending comparison: version A ends "so yeah, that's how I do it, let me know what you think." Version B ends on "and that is why I charge by the day." Version B keeps 2 to 3 seconds of average watch time on a 40 second video.

## Common mistakes

- Saving the best part for the end. Viewers who leave at 15 seconds never see it.
- A summary paragraph at the end. It signals the ending and people leave.
- Uniform pacing. No re-hooks means a steady bleed.
- Fading out. It reads as "you can go now."
- Editing from the top down. Cut the middle first, then the opening.

## FAQ

**Should I loop the video?**
If the last frame naturally matches the first, yes. Do not force it with a repeated line.

**How short is too short?**
Under 7 seconds, completion rate is high but watch time is low. 20 to 45 seconds is the range where both signals are strong for most formats.

**Does adding "wait for it" help?**
It works once. Viewers learn the trick quickly, and a video that needs it usually has a hook problem.
