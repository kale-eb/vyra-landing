# How to repurpose long-form video into shorts

**Repurposing works when the short is a complete thought, not a random excerpt. Find a 20 to 60 second segment that opens on a claim or a question and closes on a payoff, reframe the 16:9 source to 9:16 with the speaker's face on the upper third, add phrase captions, and fix any missing context with a 3 to 7 word text hook or one bridging sentence. One 20 minute video usually yields 3 to 6 usable shorts.**

## Why it matters

Long-form takes hours to make and earns one upload. Each short from it is a new front door to the channel at almost no cost. The failure mode is clips that make sense only if you watched the full episode, which is exactly what the new viewer did not do.

## The method

1. **Read the transcript, not the timeline.** Scan for lines that make a claim, ask a question, or start a story. Mark 8 to 10 candidates.
2. **Test each candidate for completeness.** Does it have a setup, a point, and an end inside 60 seconds? If it needs the previous 5 minutes, cut it.
3. **Trim to the strongest first line.** Start on the claim, not the lead-in. Remove "so like I was saying" and any reference to earlier parts.
4. **Reframe to vertical.** Crop 16:9 to 9:16 around the speaker. Face on the upper third, shoulders in. For two speakers, use a split layout or cut between them.
5. **Add captions.** Phrase captions, 3 to 5 words, safe zone. Podcast audio is often muted in the feed.
6. **Restore context.** A text hook for the first 2 seconds ("Why I stopped taking commissions") or one bridging sentence if a name or term needs explaining.
7. **Cut the internal pauses.** Long-form pacing is slower. Remove pauses over 0.3 seconds and tighten to short-form speed.
8. **End on the last useful word.** No "anyway" and no return to the conversation.

## Rules of thumb

| Element | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Short length | 20 to 60 s |
| Shorts per 20 min episode | 3 to 6 |
| Time to first spoken word | under 0.5 s |
| Reframe | face on upper third, 15 to 25 percent zoom max |
| Captions | phrase, lower-middle safe zone |
| Context fix | text hook or 1 sentence, never a paragraph |
| Pause removal | over 0.3 s |

Two-speaker rule: cut to whoever is speaking. Only use split screen when the reaction matters as much as the words.

## Examples

Podcast episode about pricing art, 22 minutes. Candidates: "why I charge by the day" (44 s), "the client who ghosted" (58 s), "my first sale" (37 s), "buffer days" (29 s). Rejected: "as I mentioned earlier about materials" (needs earlier context).

Reframe of a two-camera interview: guest wide at 16:9 becomes a 9:16 crop on the guest at 1.2x with face on the upper third; host reactions cut in at full frame for 1 to 2 seconds on the strongest lines.

Context fix: the clip starts "and that is when she said no." Text hook for 2 seconds: "The client said no to Friday." Now it stands alone.

## Common mistakes

- Clipping the funniest 15 seconds with no setup. It lands only for people who saw the episode.
- Keeping long-form pacing. The pauses that felt natural in 20 minutes kill a 40 second clip.
- Center-cropping a wide shot and cutting off the speaker's head.
- No captions on podcast clips. Most are watched muted.
- Uploading the same short to every platform with the in-app sound baked in.

## FAQ

**Should I shoot long-form with shorts in mind?**
Yes. Frame the speaker with room to crop vertical, and have them deliver key points as complete sentences without referring to earlier parts.

**How many shorts is too many from one episode?**
Six is a practical ceiling. Past that, the segments are usually weak or repetitive.

**Do shorts cannibalize the long-form?**
No. They are discovery for the long-form. Put the episode link in the caption or a pinned comment.
