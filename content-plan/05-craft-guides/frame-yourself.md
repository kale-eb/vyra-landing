# How to frame yourself for a talking head video

**Good talking head framing is a medium close-up: lens at eye level, eyes sitting on the upper third of the frame, both shoulders fully inside the edges, and a hand's width of space above your head. Sit or stand at least an arm's length from the background, face a window or soft light source, and shoot vertical if the video is for short-form. Get this right in the room and the edit is already half done.**

## Why it matters

Framing is the first thing a viewer reads before a word is spoken. Cropped shoulders, a lens pointed up your nose, or a wall two inches behind your head all signal "amateur" in a fraction of a second. Correct framing is free and takes 30 seconds to set.

## The method

1. **Put the lens at eye level.** Stack books, use a tripod, or prop the phone on a shelf. Shooting from below adds chin and ceiling. Shooting from above shrinks you.
2. **Place your eyes on the upper third line.** Turn on the grid in your camera app. Your eyes sit on or just above the top horizontal line, not in the center.
3. **Keep both shoulders in frame.** For 9:16, this means a medium close-up from mid-chest up. If a shoulder is cut, step back or zoom out.
4. **Leave a hand's width of headroom.** Too much headroom pushes you down the frame and wastes the top third. Too little feels cramped.
5. **Get distance from the background.** An arm's length minimum. Two meters if you can. This separates you from the wall and lets a phone's portrait mode or a real lens blur it.
6. **Face the light.** Sit with a window in front of you or 45 degrees to one side. Never with the window behind you.
7. **Look at the lens, not the screen.** On a phone, that is the small dot near the top. Tape a sticker next to it if you keep drifting.

## Rules of thumb

| Setting | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Lens height | at eye level, plus or minus 5 cm |
| Eye position | upper third line |
| Headroom | 1 hand width |
| Shoulders | both fully visible |
| Distance to background | 1 to 2 m |
| Distance to camera (phone, 1x lens) | 60 to 90 cm |
| Phone lens | 1x, never 0.5x for faces |
| Orientation | 9:16 for short-form, 16:9 for YouTube |

Depth trick: put something 1 to 3 meters behind you (a plant, a lamp, a shelf) so the background has layers. A flat wall reads as a webcam call.

## Examples

Standard 9:16 talking head: phone at eye level on a tripod, you seated 75 cm away, eyes on the top grid line, window to camera left, bookshelf 1.5 m behind.

Standing product demo: phone slightly below eye level so the product in your hands sits in the lower half of frame, shoulders in, you step 30 cm closer for the demo moment.

Studio with art: subject centered, canvas 2 m behind and offset to the right third so it is visible but soft. This is the framing used in most of @sulansart's studio talking heads.

## Do it in Vyra

Framing is set on the shoot, but Vyra can rescue near-misses:

- "Reframe this 16:9 talking head to 9:16. Keep my face on the upper third and both shoulders in frame. Use a rectangle mask, do not crop the shoulders."
- "The headroom in this clip is too large. Punch in 15 percent and reposition so my eyes sit on the upper third line, then keep that framing for the whole video."
- "Track my face across this handheld clip and keep it centered with a smooth mask so the framing does not drift."

## Common mistakes

- Phone on the desk pointing up. Add height until the lens meets your eyes.
- Using the ultra-wide 0.5x lens for a face. It distorts noses and foreheads.
- Sitting against a wall. Zero depth, harsh shadow behind your head.
- Eyes in the dead center of the frame. Move them up.
- Looking at yourself on screen instead of the lens. Eye contact drops 5 degrees and viewers feel it.

## FAQ

**Should I shoot 16:9 and crop to 9:16 later?**
Only if you also need a 16:9 version. If the video is short-form only, shoot vertical so you use the full sensor and frame correctly in the room.

**What if I only have a window behind me?**
Turn 90 degrees so the window is to your side, or close the blinds and use a lamp in front of you. Backlight from a window makes you a silhouette.

**Do I need a tripod?**
Any stable surface at eye level works. A cheap phone tripod removes the stacking of books and takes 20 seconds to set.
